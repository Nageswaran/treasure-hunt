Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Grand Prix Pit Crew Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-95 minutes

Vesper Motorsport is one hour from qualifying, and the strategist's notebook — every lap time, every tire plan, every pit-stop code — has vanished from the garage. Worse, today's flag signals and radio calls have gotten tangled together, and nobody can tell which message belongs to which car.

The Chief Mechanic has called in the Internet Detective Academy. Rebuild the strategy, untangle the signals, and get the right car back on track before the five-minute warning light comes on.

MISSION 01
EASY

Pit Stop Choreography

Before anything else, the crew chief wants to know you understand how a real pit stop works. A mechanic scribbled the five steps on a whiteboard, but knocked them out of order.

Part A

Here are the five steps, each given a letter, all mixed up:

W — Wheel nuts are loosened, old tires come off, new tires go on and are tightened.
X — The light or "lollipop" signal shows go, and the car accelerates away.
Y — The car enters the pit lane and slows to the pit lane speed limit.
Z — Front and rear jacks lift the car off the ground.
V — The car is lowered back down off the jacks.

Type the five letters in the correct order, with no spaces (for example, "ABCDE").

Hint 1

Think about what has to be true before the next step can happen — the car can't be lowered before it's even been lifted, and it can't be lifted before it has even arrived.

Part B

Chalked on the pit wall in Dutch is today's word of the day: circuit (the racetrack itself). Is it "de circuit" or "het circuit"?

Type the correct lidwoord (article) together with the word, like "de circuit" or "het circuit".

Hint 1

Most Dutch words borrowed from other languages that end in "-uit" or describe a place or system often take "het," but not always — this one's worth double-checking in a Dutch dictionary.

Search to find out: in what year did the Silverstone Circuit open, on the site of a former World War II bomber airfield?

Type the year as a four-digit number.

Team Radio Log While you search, the pit wall radio crackles: "Copy that — box this lap, garage 6." Worth remembering: which garage number the crew just called out.

Search for "Silverstone Circuit history" and look for a reputable source like Wikipedia — the year it opened is usually in the first paragraph.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Pit Lane Blueprint

A hand-drawn sketch of today's pit lane turns up, taped inside a toolbox lid. A car drives down the straight section, then curves along a quarter-circle bend to merge back onto the main straight.

The straight section is 60 m long. The curved bend is a quarter circle with a radius of 14 m.

Find the total distance a car drives, from pit entry to pit exit, along the straight plus the curve. Use π ≈ 22/7.

Type the total distance in meters (just the number).

Hint 1

The length of a full circle's edge (its circumference) is 2 × π × radius. A quarter circle is exactly one quarter of that.

Hint 2

First find the length of just the curved quarter, then add it to the 60 m straight section.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Whispers on the Radio

Right now, hidden sensors inside the car are streaming live data — tire temperature, brake heat, fuel level — back to the pit wall over radio waves, faster than anyone could shout it. Engineers call this telemetry.

But one older piece of pit-stop technology is what you need to find. Before the 1970s, mechanics loosened a car's single wheel nut by swinging a heavy hammer, and a pit stop could take over 40 seconds. Then a new pneumatic (compressed-air-powered) tool started replacing the hammer, and pit stops got dramatically faster.

Search to find: what two-word tool name (it's still used in every F1 pit stop today) does this tool go by?

Hint 1

Search for "F1 pit stop wheel tool history" or "how do F1 pit crews change tires so fast."

Hint 2

The tool is named after what it does (spins a wheel nut loose) and how it works (with a sudden burst of force, like a small firearm).

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Hairpin's Old Name

The Circuit de Monaco has hosted a Grand Prix since 1929, winding through the streets of Monte Carlo. It has one corner so tight and so slow that drivers brake down to a walking-pace crawl — by far the slowest corner in the whole Formula 1 calendar.

That corner has been renamed several times over the decades, after whichever hotel stood beside it. But it has one original name from long before any hotel was ever built there — back when a real train station stood on that exact spot.

Search to find: what was this hairpin corner ORIGINALLY called, named after the train station that used to be right there?

Type the two-word name.

Hint 1

Search for "Monaco Grand Prix corner names history" and look for a reputable motorsport history source.

Hint 2

The name has nothing to do with racing at all — it's named after a building that used to sit right beside the track.

💡 Did you know? Scuderia Ferrari was founded on 16 November 1929 in Modena, Italy — eighteen years before Ferrari built its very first car in 1947.

MISSION 06
HARD

Four Cars, Four Tires

Four drivers — Voss, Marchetti, Okafor, and Lindqvist — finished a practice session 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th. Each one used a different tire compound: Soft, Medium, Hard, or Wet. Use these clues to work out the full picture:

  1. Marchetti finished 1st.
  2. The driver on Hard tires finished immediately after Marchetti.
  3. Voss did not use Soft or Hard tires, and did not finish 2nd or 3rd.
  4. Lindqvist used Medium tires, and finished ahead of Voss (a lower position number).
  5. The driver on Wet tires finished last.

Which tire compound did Marchetti use?

Hint 1

Start with clue 3 — it rules out three positions for Voss at once, leaving only one place Voss could possibly be.

Hint 2

Once you know Voss's position, clue 5 tells you Voss's tire. Then clue 2 tells you who's 2nd, and clue 4 tells you Lindqvist's tire and rules out where Lindqvist can't be. Marchetti's tire is whatever's left over.

Two Mechanics, One Story

A spare gearbox has gone missing from its shelf, and two mechanics tell you two different stories about where it came from.

Mechanic Petrov says: "It came from garage 6 — I'm sure, because that's what I heard called out over the radio earlier today."

Mechanic Alvarez says: "No, it was garage 9 — I just checked the shelf label myself, five minutes ago."

Only one of them can be right. Using what you already know, which mechanic is correct?

Type the mechanic's last name.

Hint 1

Neither claim comes with proof you can check right now — but one of these garage numbers should already sound familiar from earlier today.

Hint 2

Scroll back through today's case file. A radio call earlier mentioned a specific garage number.

💡 Did you know? The Nürburgring opened on 18 June 1927. Its Nordschleife loop was so long and twisty that champion driver Jackie Stewart nicknamed it "The Green Hell" after racing there in dense fog and rain in 1968.

MISSION 08
HARD

Fuel, Flags, and a Hidden Number

Stage 1 of 2

Grab a pencil for this one. The car's fuel tank holds exactly 100 liters at the start of the day.

  • During Q1, the car used 18% of the tank.
  • During Q2, it used another 24% of the tank.
  • During Q3, it used another 21% of the tank.

How many liters are left in the tank after Q3? (Each percentage is of the original full 100-liter tank.)

Hint 1

Add up all three percentages used first, then subtract that total from 100.

MISSION 09
BIG AHA!

The Widening Gap

At the checkered flag, Car 7 finished 14.6 seconds behind Car 3.

On the very last lap, Car 3 made a small mistake, and Car 7 clawed back 3.2 seconds — that's what set the final 14.6-second gap.

Before that last lap, the gap had been climbing steadily: it grew by exactly 2.5 seconds on each of the five laps just before the final lap.

What was the gap between the two cars at the exact moment those five climbing laps began?

Type the number of seconds (for example, "12.5").

Hint 1

Work backward from the finish. First undo the last lap's change to find the gap right before the final lap started.

Hint 2

Once you have the gap right before the final lap, undo all five laps of steady climbing, 2.5 seconds at a time, to find where the climb began.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Final Call

Part A

The strategy is rebuilt and the signals are clear — but there's one last decision. Vesper Motorsport needs to pay for a last-minute upgrade, and two sponsors have made offers. The team wants to spend the least money possible.

  • Ignition Energy Drinks offers a flat €12,000, no matter what.
  • Apex Watches offers €7,000 flat, plus €50 for every lap the car completes in today's race. Today's race is 60 laps, and the car is expected to finish all of them.

Work out the total cost of each offer. Which sponsor is the better deal?

Type the sponsor's name (one word).

Hint 1

For Apex Watches, multiply the per-lap fee by the number of laps first, then add the flat fee.

Hint 2

Compare the two total costs directly — the lower total is the better deal.

Part B

The strategist crunched the numbers. The mechanics fixed every sensor. The driver trusted every signal from the pit wall, lap after lap. None of it would have worked alone.

Type the one word for what really won the race today.

Hint 1

Think about what it's called when very different people all pull together toward one shared goal.

Case closed?

Car #37 rolls out of the garage right on time, strategy notebook rebuilt page by page. The strategist's math, the mechanics' hands, and the driver's trust all had to work together — the pit crew calls that teamwork. Case closed, Detective.
