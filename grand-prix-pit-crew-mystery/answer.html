Mission 01 · Easy · Logical Reasoning (Foundational: sequencing) + Dutch Language

Pit Stop Choreography

Part A: YZWVX
Part B: het circuit
How to get there

Part A: The real order of a pit stop is: (Y) car enters and slows to the pit lane speed limit → (Z) jacks lift the car → (W) wheels are changed → (V) car is lowered → (X) lights/lollipop signal go and the car leaves. So the correct order of the given letters is Y, Z, W, V, X.

Part B: "Circuit" is a het-word in Dutch — "het circuit." (Confirmed via Van Dale and multiple Dutch lidwoord-reference sites.)

Why this is the only answer

Part A: each step has exactly one logical prerequisite (you can't change wheels before the car is jacked up, can't lower the car before it's raised, can't drive off before the go signal), so only one ordering is physically possible. Part B: Dutch grammatical gender for a given noun is fixed — "circuit" is a het-word, not a de-word, in standard Dutch.

Source / fact used

Part A: general, verified real-world pit-stop procedure (see Mission 04's sourced pit-stop history). Part B: Van Dale Dutch dictionary and welklidwoord.nl / webwoordenboek.nl, both confirming "het circuit."

Mission 03 · Medium · Mathematics (geometry/measurement, self-drawn diagram)

The Pit Lane Blueprint

Answer: 82 (meters)
How to get there

The quarter-circle curve's length is one quarter of a full circle's circumference: circumference = 2 × π × radius = 2 × (22/7) × 14 = 88 m. A quarter of 88 is 22 m. Add the straight section: 60 m + 22 m = 82 m.

Why this is the only answer

The diagram's two labeled dimensions (60 m straight, 14 m radius) are the only numbers given and are self-consistent with the shape drawn (a straight segment meeting a quarter-circle arc). Using π ≈ 22/7 as instructed gives a clean, exact 22 m arc length, so there's exactly one resulting total.

Source / fact used

Self-drawn fictional diagram (SVG); circle/arc-length math recomputed by hand and confirmed: 2×(22/7)×14 = 88, 88÷4 = 22, 60+22 = 82.

Mission 04 · Medium · Technology (pit-stop tech, verified) + Internet Research

Whispers on the Radio

Answer: wheel gun
How to get there

Search "F1 pit stop wheel gun history" or "F1 pneumatic wheel tool." Before the 1970s, F1 cars used a single central wheel nut loosened by hand with a hammer, and pit stops took 40 seconds or more. During the 1970s and 80s, pneumatic (compressed-air) "wheel guns" (impact wrenches) began replacing the hammer, dramatically cutting tire-change time. Wheel guns are still standard equipment in every F1 pit stop today.

Why this is the only answer

Multiple independent motorsport-history sources agree on this term and the 1970s timeframe; "wheel gun" is the specific two-word name the puzzle asks for (a general "impact wrench" is the same idea but not the two-word racing term requested, so the puzzle wording steers toward the one intended phrase).

Source / fact used

racingcarnetwork.com ("Paoli pit gun"), interestingengineering.com ("F1 wheel guns are truly amazing"), and thenewswheel.com ("History of F1 Pit Stops") — all describing the pre-1970s hammer-and-single-nut method and the 1970s-80s pneumatic wheel gun changeover.

Mission 05 · Medium · Navigation / Spatial Reasoning (real circuit layout) + Internet Research

The Hairpin's Old Name

Answer: station hairpin
How to get there

Search "Monaco Grand Prix corner names history." The Circuit de Monaco's slowest, tightest corner (drivers slow to a crawl, around 30-40 km/h) has been renamed several times as different hotels were built and rebranded next to it. But its ORIGINAL name, from before any hotel stood there, was the "Station Hairpin" — named for a real train station that used to occupy that spot.

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle specifically asks for the name tied to the train station (a fixed historical fact that can't be rebranded, unlike the hotel-based names that have changed multiple times over the decades) — this avoids any answer that depends on the current, changeable sponsor name.

Source / fact used

Formula1.com, "Explained: How every Monaco Grand Prix corner got its name," and monacolife.net, both confirming the corner's original name was the Station Hairpin, before later being called the Loews, Grand Hotel, Sun Casino, and (most recently) Fairmont Hairpin.

Mission 08 · Hard · MULTI-STAGE: Mathematics (percentages, pen-and-paper) + Cryptography (flag-code decode)

Fuel, Flags, and a Hidden Number

Stage 1: 37
Stage 2 (final): overtake caution pits finish
How to get there

Stage 1: Total percentage of the tank used: 18% + 24% + 21% = 63%. Fuel remaining: 100% − 63% = 37%, which is 37 liters since the tank holds 100 liters.

Stage 2: The Stage 1 answer (37) is also a car number in the flag log. Filtering only car 37's rows, in lap order: Lap 13 = Blue (overtake), Lap 15 = Yellow (caution), Lap 17 = Black (pits), Lap 19 = Checkered (finish). Reading the code words in order gives "overtake caution pits finish."

Why this is the only answer

Stage 1 is a straightforward, unambiguous percentage subtraction. Stage 2 genuinely depends on Stage 1: without the correct number (37), a solver wouldn't know which four rows of the eight-row log to use, and using the wrong car's rows produces a different (wrong) sequence of code words. The flag key maps each real, verified flag meaning to exactly one code word, so the decode is deterministic.

Source / fact used

Fuel percentages are an invented but internally consistent scenario (tank = 100 L, so percent = liters directly, verified by hand: 18+24+21=63, 100−63=37). Flag meanings are real and verified: yellow (caution/danger), blue (faster car, move aside), white (slow vehicle on track — this is the Formula 1-specific meaning; other series like NASCAR use white for "final lap"), solid black (disqualified, return to pits), red (session stopped), green (track clear), checkered (race finished) — sourced from racingnews365.com, planetf1.com, autosport.com, and williamsf1.com.

Mission 09 · Big Aha! · Logical Reasoning (Advanced: working backward)

The Widening Gap

Answer: 5.3 (seconds)
How to get there

Work backward from the finish. The final gap was 14.6 seconds, and the last lap alone closed it by 3.2 seconds, so the gap right before the final lap was 14.6 + 3.2 = 17.8 seconds. That 17.8-second gap was the end result of five laps of steady 2.5-second growth, so undo all five laps: 17.8 − (5 × 2.5) = 17.8 − 12.5 = 5.3 seconds.

Why this is the only answer

Every value in the chain is fixed and given (14.6, 3.2, 2.5, and "five laps"), and the two backward steps (undo the final lap, then undo the five-lap climb) are the only way to reach the moment described ("the exact moment those five climbing laps began"), so there is exactly one resulting number.

Source / fact used

Self-contained fictional math scenario, double-checked by hand: 14.6+3.2=17.8; 17.8−12.5=5.3.
