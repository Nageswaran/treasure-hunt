Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Library's Vanishing Chapter

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes

Tomorrow morning, Fenwick Ridge Public Library launches its new Rare Illustrated Books Archive online — years of carefully scanning beautiful, fragile old picture books, finally ready for the whole world to see. Tonight, the preview site is glitching: Chapter Seven of the library's oldest illustrated fairy-tale collection shows up scrambled and broken. Worse, an anonymous online auction just appeared, claiming to sell "the leaked original manuscript pages" for cash before the archive even opens. Ms. Okafor, head of Special Collections, needs a sharp young detective to figure out the truth before the launch: is this a digitization glitch, a prank, or a scam?

MISSION 01
EASY

The Scanner's Secret Code

Instead of its usual green "OK" light, the library's book-scanning machine printed this on its little screen when it choked on Chapter Seven:

01010011 01000011 01000001 01001110

Computers store letters as binary numbers using a code called ASCII. Decode these four 8-digit groups into four letters to find out what mode the scanner says it's stuck in.

Hint 1

Each 8-digit group is one letter. Add up the value of every "1" digit using these place values: 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Then match the total number to a letter using an ASCII chart (A=65, B=66, C=67...).

MISSION 02
EASY

Sorting the Returns Cart

While the scanner problem gets looked at, Ms. Okafor asks you to help sort six books off the returns cart. In Dutch, every noun is either a "de" word or a "het" word — and every one of these six is a real library word:

  • boek (book)
  • bibliotheek (library)
  • verhaal (story)
  • kaart (card / map)
  • pagina (page)
  • raadsel (riddle)

Sort all six into a "DE" pile and a "HET" pile. How many of the six belong in the "HET" pile?

Hint 1

There's no single trick that works for every word — Dutch children mostly memorize each one. A search for "de bibliotheek" or "het bibliotheek" (with the quote marks) will show you which one people actually use.

🕵️ Agent's Notebook: While you wait, you notice the brass plaque mounted by the front doors: "Fenwick Ridge Public Library — Est. 1911." Jot that down — you never know when an old number like that might matter again.

💡 Did you know? Libraries organize their books using the Dewey Decimal system, which today is used in over 200,000 libraries in at least 135 countries around the world.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Illustrator's Legacy

The rare volume with the vanishing chapter is a hundred-year-old collection of illustrated fairy tales, bound in the grand tradition of Victorian-era English picture books — the same tradition that made illustrator Randolph Caldecott famous.

Caldecott's lively, detailed drawings for children's books were so admired that a major American award for picture-book illustration was later named after him. Search the internet to find out: in what year was the very first Caldecott Medal actually awarded? Type just the year.

Hint 1

Try searching "Caldecott Medal first awarded year."

Hint 2

The medal itself was created by the American Library Association one year, but the very first WINNER was announced the year after that. Make sure you find the year of the first winner.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Real Site or Copycat?

Late last night, a listing appeared on an unfamiliar site:

"SELLING: leaked original manuscript pages from Fenwick Ridge's rare archive — CASH OR GIFT CARD ONLY, act fast before the library shuts this down!"

Something about it feels off. Before jumping to conclusions, real internet detectives always check a website itself first. Cybersecurity experts (like the ones at CISA, the U.S. government's cybersecurity agency) recommend three checks:

  1. Read the web address letter by letter. Scammers often add extra words, hyphens, or swap in a similar-looking domain ending.
  2. Look for "https://" and a small padlock icon, which means the connection is encrypted — though a scam site can have this too, so it's a helpful clue, not proof by itself.
  3. When in doubt, don't click the link at all. Search for the organization's name yourself and go straight to its official site.

Four different links claim to be Fenwick Ridge Public Library's real website. Using the checks above, read each one carefully:

A) http://fenwickridge-library-auctions.net
B) https://www.fenwickrldgelibrary.org
C) https://www.fenwickridgelibrary.org
D) https://fenwickridgelibrary-rarebooks.com

Type just the real domain name — without "https://" or "www." — like this format: example.org

Hint 1

Read each option letter by letter. One of them has a single sneaky letter swapped for a very similar-looking one.

Hint 2

Real public libraries almost always use a plain ".org" address — not ".net," not ".com," and not extra words tacked on like "auctions" or "rarebooks."

💡 Did you know? The real FTC (a U.S. government consumer-protection agency) says it plainly: "Gift cards are for gifts. Only gifts. Not payments." If anyone — even a friendly seller — asks you to pay with a gift card code, it's a scam, every single time.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Bookplate's Missing Corner

Tucked inside the front cover of the rare volume is its original ownership bookplate — but one corner was neatly trimmed off long ago, probably to fit an old picture frame.

Grab a pencil for this one: split the bookplate's outline into a rectangle and a right triangle, work out each shape's area separately, then subtract to find the bookplate's total area in square centimeters.

The full rectangle would measure 18 cm by 10 cm if the corner weren't missing. The missing corner is a right triangle with the two shorter sides shown.
Hint 1

Find the area of the whole 18 cm by 10 cm rectangle first, as if the corner were still there.

Hint 2

The missing corner is a right triangle with a 6 cm side and a 4 cm side. A triangle's area is half of base times height. Subtract that from the rectangle's area.

MISSION 06
HARD

How Old Is the Archive?

Every book in the archive is organized using the Dewey Decimal system. Search the internet to find the real year Melvil Dewey first published his famous library classification system.

Then subtract that year from the founding year you noted earlier in this case — it's on that old brass plaque. Type the number of years between them.

Hint 1

Try searching "Melvil Dewey Decimal Classification year published."

Hint 2

Once you have Dewey's real year, subtract it from the year on that brass plaque you noted down earlier in the case. Don't restate the plaque's year here — just use what you already wrote down.

MISSION 07
HARD

Who Was Cataloging Folklore?

Four curators worked in the archive this week, each on a different day and each cataloging a different section. Use these clues to find out who was cataloging Folklore on Wednesday — the exact shelf where Chapter Seven belongs:

  1. Farah worked exactly one day after Ben.
  2. Ben cataloged Poetry, and worked on Monday.
  3. The curator who cataloged Maps worked on Tuesday.
  4. Priya worked exactly two days after Ben, and did not catalog Manuscripts.

Grab some scrap paper and build a small table with the four curators, the four days (Monday-Thursday), and the four sections (Poetry, Maps, Folklore, Manuscripts).

Hint 1

Start with clue 2 — it pins down one curator's day and section completely. Then work out the other days using clues 1 and 4.

Hint 2

Once all four days are set, only two sections are left to hand out. Clue 4 tells you which one Priya does NOT catalog — that leaves only one option for her.

MISSION 08
HARD

Two Restorers, One Decision

Before the volume goes on permanent display, its 15 damaged pages need professional restoration. Two studios sent in quotes:

  • Old Towne Bindery: a flat inspection fee of €50, plus €6 per page.
  • QuickFix Paper Studio: no flat fee at all, but €11 per page.

Work out the TOTAL price from each studio for all 15 pages. Which studio actually costs less overall — and what is that lower total, in euros? Type just the number (for example: 99).

Hint 1

Work out each studio's total separately. Multiply the per-page price by 15 first, then add any flat fee on top.

Hint 2

A price with no flat fee can look cheaper at first glance than one with a fee attached — but for 15 pages, you have to compute both totals all the way through before you can be sure which is really lower.

💡 Did you know? Randolph Caldecott died young, at just 39 — but his lively, funny drawings changed picture books forever, which is why America's top illustration award still carries his name today.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

The Marginalia's Secret

In the scrambled preview, one thing survived clearly: a faint handwritten note in the margin of Chapter Seven, in curling old ink. It looks like a code.

Stage 1 of 3

This kind of code is called a Caesar cipher, named after a real Roman general and statesman. According to the ancient historian Suetonius, he really did use this trick to protect his military messages — always shifting each letter forward by the same small, fixed number of places in the alphabet.

Search the internet to find: what shift number is Julius Caesar's own cipher famously said to have used? Type just the digit.

Hint 1

Try searching "Caesar cipher historical shift Suetonius."

Stage 2 of 3

Good. Now use that shift number. The marginalia reads: VHYHQ

Shift every letter BACKWARD by that many places in the alphabet (for example, if the shift number were 1, Z would become Y). The letters will spell out a number, written as a word. Type that number as a digit — for example, if you decode the word "FOUR," type 4.

Hint 1

V shifted backward becomes S. Do the same thing to each of the other four letters.

Hint 2

Here's the alphabet to count backward on: ...P Q R S T U V W X Y Z. Shift each letter of VHYHQ back the same number of places you found in Stage 1.

Stage 3 of 3

That number is the shelf where Chapter Seven's original volume lives. Multiply it by the 12 volumes stored on that shelf, then subtract 50. The result is the exact page number the old scanner choked on. Type that page number.

Hint 1

Multiply first, then subtract — do the steps in that order.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Case Closed: Who Forgot to Log Out?

You now know the scrambled chapter was just an honest accident — a torn page an old scanner couldn't read. But how did "RareFindsBidz88" know enough about the archive to invent a believable-sounding auction post? The answer traces back to a public computer terminal that was found still logged into the archive system the next morning.

Four volunteers used that terminal the evening before, each tapping their staff badge out at a different time as they left. The library's front doors auto-lock at exactly 8:00 PM, and whoever left LAST is the one who forgot to log out. Work backward from the clues below:

  1. Deshawn tapped out at 7:30 PM.
  2. Aisha tapped out exactly 10 minutes after Deshawn.
  3. Carla tapped out exactly 5 minutes before the doors auto-locked.
  4. Marco tapped out exactly 20 minutes before Carla.

Who forgot to log out?

Hint 1

Start from the one clue anchored to a fixed time — the 8:00 PM auto-lock — and work backward from there to find Carla's exact time first.

Hint 2

Once you know Carla's exact time, Marco's follows directly from clue 4. Then just line up all four times to see who left last.

Case closed?

Piecing together a torn page, a forgotten log-out, and a scammer's empty bluff — instead of jumping straight to the scariest explanation — takes real wisdom. Case closed, Agent.
