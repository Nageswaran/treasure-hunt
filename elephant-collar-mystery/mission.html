Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Matriarch's Missing Signal

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 80-100 minutes

You've just joined a real research park in Kenya as a Junior Wildlife Ranger. The research team tracks a wise old elephant matriarch nicknamed Nyota — but her GPS collar has stopped transmitting, and nobody knows if she's simply out of range or truly in trouble. Follow the clues her rangers left behind, solve all ten field logs, and you'll find out what quality kept the whole ranger team searching until they found her.

MISSION 01
EASY

Sorting the Savanna

The ranger station keeps a whiteboard listing six animals seen this week: Lion, Zebra, Elephant, Cheetah, Wildebeest, Crocodile. How many of these six animals are BOTH plant-eaters (herbivores) AND live together in herds?

Hint 1

Lion, Cheetah, and Crocodile are meat-eaters, so they're out. Check which of the rest actually travel in herds.

💡 Did you know? An elephant's pregnancy lasts about 22 months — the longest of any land mammal on Earth.

MISSION 02
EASY

Sanne's Dutch Field Note

A Dutch volunteer ranger named Sanne left a note: "Nyota loopt door de ___" (Nyota is walking through the savanna). Is it "de savanne" or "het savanne" in Dutch? Type just the correct little word: de or het.

Hint 1

Search a Dutch dictionary for "savanne" and check which article it lists.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Nyota's Home Range

Researchers mapped Nyota's family group's protected home range as a trapezoid shape between the river and the hills. Using the measurements marked on the diagram, calculate its total area in km².

Area of a trapezoid = (sum of the two parallel sides ÷ 2) × height.
Hint 1

Add the two parallel sides together first, then divide by 2, then multiply by the height.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Three Rangers, Three Waterholes

Three rangers — Kofi, Aisha, and Juma — each radioed in from a different waterhole this morning: Elephant Pool, Observation Spring, and Thorn Tree Well. Use these clues to figure out which waterhole Aisha was at:

Clue 1: Kofi was not at Elephant Pool.
Clue 2: The ranger at Thorn Tree Well photographed a rhino — but neither Aisha nor Kofi saw a rhino today.
Clue 3: Juma was not at Observation Spring.

Hint 1

Start with Clue 2 — it tells you exactly who was at Thorn Tree Well.

Hint 2

Once you know who was at Thorn Tree Well, Clue 1 pins down Kofi's location — and Aisha gets whichever waterhole is left.

💡 Did you know? Elephants can't jump. Their leg muscles and tendons don't give them the springy "bounce" needed to lift all four feet off the ground at once.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Trail-Marker Code

Rangers carve secret animal-emoji codes into marker posts along the trails. Each emoji stands for the FIRST letter of that animal's name. Use the key to decode the message carved on Nyota's trail post:

🐍🐢🐜🦏🦒🦁
SnakeTurtleAntRhinoGiraffeLion

🐍 🐢 🐜 🦏

Hint 1

Take the first letter of each animal's name, in order, to spell a 4-letter word.

📓 Agent's Notebook: "Nyota" is Swahili for star — and the word you just decoded is her official trail codename. Jot it down, you'll need it again later.

MISSION 06
HARD

Counting the Family Group

Stage 1 of 2

✏️ Grab a pencil for this one. Last year, Nyota's family group had 40 elephants. This year, the group grew by 15% thanks to several new calves. How many elephants are in the group now?

Hint 1

Find 15% of 40 first, then add that to the original 40.

Stage 2 of 2

Rangers set up 1 water station for every 8 elephants in a group. Using your new total from Stage 1, how many water stations are needed? (If there's a partial group left over, that still needs one more full station.)

Hint 1

Divide the new total by 8. If it doesn't divide evenly, round UP — you can't build half a water station.

MISSION 07
HARD

Plotting the Last Signal

Nyota's collar sent one last weak ping before going quiet. Starting from the ranger station, the signal came from 3 km EAST, then 4 km NORTH. Using the Pythagorean theorem, how far away (in a straight line) is that final signal from the ranger station, in km?

Hint 1

The east and north legs form a right angle. The straight-line distance is the hypotenuse: a² + b² = c².

💡 Did you know? Amboseli National Park in Kenya sits right in the shadow of Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's tallest mountain.

MISSION 08
HARD

Choosing a Data Plan

The ranger team needs GPS collar data for exactly 9 more months while they search for Nyota. Two conservation groups offer data access: Group A charges a flat €60 for 12 months (any amount of that time). Group B charges €8 per month, with no long commitment. For exactly 9 months of data, which group is the better value?

Hint 1

Group A's price doesn't change no matter how many of the 12 months you actually use. Work out Group B's total cost for 9 months and compare.

MISSION 09
HARD

The Aha! Moment — Night Patrol Roster

Four rangers — Kofi, Aisha, Juma, and you, the newest recruit — need to cover night patrol, one ranger per night, over 4 nights. Every ranger patrols exactly once, and the order matters (patrolling Night 1 vs. Night 4 is different). How many different possible patrol-order arrangements are there for the 4 rangers across the 4 nights?

Hint 1

Think about it step by step: how many choices for Night 1? Then how many are left for Night 2? Keep going.

Hint 2

Multiply your choices together: 4 × 3 × 2 × 1.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Nyota Comes Home

Nyota's collar reactivates! To confirm it's really her, the tracking app asks for her trail codename from Mission 5 — but shift every letter FORWARD by 2 places in the alphabet first (A becomes C, B becomes D, and so on).

Take her codename, shift each letter forward by 2, and type the result to unlock her final location.

Hint 1

Scroll back to Mission 5's Agent's Notebook if you need her codename again.

Hint 2

S+2=U. Shift each of the four letters the same way.

Case closed?

Nyota is safe, grazing peacefully with her family group by the river. It was the ranger team's kindness — patient, careful, never giving up on one old elephant — that brought her home. Well done, Agent.
