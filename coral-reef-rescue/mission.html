The Case of the Silent Reef

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60-75 minutes
Dr. Reyes runs a small research station that watches over a coral nursery, checking every day that the reef nearby is healthy. Last night, her sensors sent one garbled signal — then went completely silent. She needs a sharp-eyed detective to retrace her steps, work out what happened, and get the sensors talking again before the next research boat arrives this afternoon.
MISSION 01
EASY

Dr. Reyes's Map

Pinned above Dr. Reyes's desk is a map of the coral reef she studies, with a note: "Part of the biggest coral reef system on Earth — you can even see it from space."

Search online to find the name of the world's largest coral reef system, located off the coast of Australia. What is it called?

Hint 1

Try searching "world's largest coral reef system Australia."

MISSION 02
EASY

The Logbook's Ocean Words

Dr. Reyes's logbook lists ocean words with the little word "de" or "het" missing: vis (fish), zee (sea), oceaan (ocean), and rif (reef).

Three of these words take "de." One is the odd one out and takes "het" instead. Which word is the odd one out?

📓 Field Notebook: A sticky note on the page reads: "Backup sensor buoy always goes down to exactly 14 meters — deep enough that boat propellers can't reach it." Worth remembering!

Hint 1

Say "het" in front of each word out loud. Only one of the four will sound right.

💡 Did you know? Coral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor, but they're home to an enormous share of all known marine species — far more than their tiny size would suggest.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Warning Thermometer

Marine biologists have found that on many reefs around the world, coral starts to turn white and struggle once the water passes about 30°C and stays that warm for two weeks or more.

Dr. Reyes's old thermometer only shows Fahrenheit. Grab a pencil — convert 30°C to Fahrenheit using the formula F = C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32.

Hint 1

First multiply 30 by 9, then divide that answer by 5.

Hint 2

30 × 9 = 270, and 270 ÷ 5 = 54. Now add 32 to finish the formula.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Two Circles

Dr. Reyes's field guide lists five reef animals with one fact each:

1. Shark — breathes using gills, never leaves the ocean.

2. Jellyfish — has no lungs or gills, never leaves the ocean.

3. Clownfish — breathes using gills, never leaves the ocean.

4. Sea turtle — breathes using lungs, but comes ashore on a beach to lay its eggs.

5. Dolphin — breathes using lungs, never leaves the ocean.

Picture two circles: one labeled "breathes using lungs," the other labeled "never leaves the ocean." Which single animal belongs inside both circles at once?

Hint 1

Cross off any animal that fails either circle. Gills, not lungs? Out of the lungs circle. Ever comes ashore? Out of the ocean circle.

Hint 2

Only one animal on the list both breathes with lungs and never sets a flipper or fin on land.

💡 Did you know? When coral gets too stressed by warm water, it pushes out the tiny colorful algae living inside it — that's what makes the coral turn ghostly white.

MISSION 05
HARD

Which Buoy?

Two lines are scrawled in the dive log:

1. "The damaged sensor is on Buoy 3 OR Buoy 5 — but not both."

2. "It is NOT Buoy 3."

Which buoy has the damaged sensor? Type just the number (for example: 3).

Hint 1

Line 1 tells you it's exactly one of the two buoys, never both. Line 2 rules one of them out completely.

Hint 2

If it's not Buoy 3, and it has to be exactly one of Buoy 3 or Buoy 5, there's only one buoy left it could be.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Sensor's Report

Buoy 5's sensor finally reconnects and sends photos: the coral nearby has turned pale and ghostly white. Search online for the scientific name of this process — when coral turns white after pushing out the colorful algae living inside it, usually because the water got too warm. What is it called?

Hint 1

Try searching "coral turns white warm water term."

Hint 2

It's a two-word term. The second word describes what happens to laundry left out in the sun too long.

💡 Did you know? Some scientists say the world's largest coral reef system is so big it can be picked out from photos taken in outer space.

MISSION 07
BIG AHA

The Backup Sensor

Stage 1 of 2

To fully understand the danger, Dr. Reyes wants one more number. Search online: about what percentage of all known marine species live on or near coral reefs, even though reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Try searching "percentage of marine species live on coral reefs."

Stage 2 of 2

Add that percentage to the depth in meters only Dr. Reyes's field notebook would know. That total is the section number where the backup sensor is hidden.

Hint 1

Think back to the sticky note in Mission 2 — how deep does the backup sensor buoy always go?

Hint 2

Add Stage 1's percentage to 14.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

What the Reef Taught You

With the backup sensor back online, the whole reef map lights up on Dr. Reyes's screen. She smiles and says:

"Every great scientist chases the same feeling — learning something brand new about the world, something nobody told you, that you worked out for yourself. What's the one word for that feeling?"

Hint 1

Think about what it feels like the very first time you figure something out that no one showed you.

Hint 2

It's the word scientists use for finding out something new — like discovering a new species.

Case closed?

Every sensor on the reef map blinks back to life, and Dr. Reyes radios the research boat with good news. You didn't just fix the sensors — you experienced the thrill of real discovery. Well done, Agent.
