Mission 01 · Easy

Dr. Reyes's Map

Answer: Great Barrier Reef
How to get there

Search "world's largest coral reef system Australia." The Great Barrier Reef, off the coast of Queensland, Australia, is the world's largest coral reef system — over 2,300 km long and made of more than 2,900 individual reefs.

Why this is the only answer

It is the specific, consistently documented largest coral reef system on Earth; no other reef matches that description.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search summarizing Wikipedia, Britannica, and NOAA Ocean Service: Great Barrier Reef, Coral Sea, off Queensland, Australia — the world's largest living structure.

Mission 02 · Easy

The Logbook's Ocean Words

Answer: rif
How to get there

"Vis" (de), "zee" (de), and "oceaan" (de) all take "de." "Rif" (reef) is a neuter word and takes "het" — the odd one out.

Why this is the only answer

Each word's article was individually verified; exactly one of the four ("rif") takes "het" while the other three take "de." ("Koraal" was deliberately left out of this puzzle — some Dutch grammar references list it as accepting both "de" and "het," which would make it an unsafe answer to grade.)

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against Dutch grammar reference sites (welklidwoord.nl, webwoordenboek.nl) for each word: de vis, de zee, de oceaan, het rif.

Planted detail (delayed recall — needed again in Mission 7, Stage 2)

Mission 03 · Medium

The Warning Thermometer

Answer: 86
How to get there

F = C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32. F = 30 × 9 ÷ 5 + 32 = 270 ÷ 5 + 32 = 54 + 32 = 86.

Why this is the only answer

Fixed unit-conversion formula with one correct result; recomputed by hand as 86°F.

Source / fact used

The 30°C figure reflects real reef science: research on Caribbean reefs shows coral bleaching when sea surface temperature exceeds 30°C for about two consecutive weeks, and general bleaching thresholds worldwide are commonly cited in this range. Verified via web search summarizing NOAA and peer-reviewed coral bleaching research. The conversion itself is standard arithmetic, not something that needed a lookup.

Mission 04 · Medium

Two Circles

Answer: Dolphin
How to get there

Shark and clownfish breathe with gills, not lungs — out of the "lungs" circle. Jellyfish has neither lungs nor gills — out of the "lungs" circle. Sea turtle breathes with lungs but comes ashore to nest — out of the "never leaves the ocean" circle. Only the dolphin breathes with lungs AND never leaves the ocean, so it's the only animal in both circles.

Why this is the only answer

Every fact needed is stated directly in the puzzle text, so the deduction is closed and unambiguous — no outside knowledge or lookup required, and exactly one animal satisfies both stated conditions.

Source / fact used

Self-contained classification puzzle (two-circle Venn diagram); the underlying animal facts (breathing method, whether the species ever comes ashore) are standard, uncontested biology, restated directly in the puzzle so no verification search was needed to solve it.

Mission 05 · Hard

Which Buoy?

Answer: 5 (Buoy 5)
How to get there

Line 1 is an XOR: exactly one of Buoy 3 or Buoy 5 is damaged, never both. Line 2 states it is NOT Buoy 3. Since it must be exactly one of the two, and Buoy 3 is ruled out, it must be Buoy 5.

Why this is the only answer

This is a closed two-statement formal-logic puzzle (XOR + NOT); given both statements are true, Buoy 5 is the only value consistent with both.

Source / fact used

Self-contained logic puzzle; solvable purely by reasoning from the two given statements, no outside lookup needed.

Mission 06 · Hard

The Sensor's Report

Answer: coral bleaching
How to get there

Search "coral turns white warm water term." When water gets too warm, coral expels the colorful algae (zooxanthellae) living in its tissues and turns white — this process is called coral bleaching.

Why this is the only answer

"Coral bleaching" is the specific, universally used scientific term for this exact process; sources converge on this term immediately.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search summarizing NOAA Ocean Service and the Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS): coral bleaching is the loss of colorful algae from coral tissue due to heat stress, turning the coral white.

Mission 07 · Big Aha (Multi-Stage)

The Backup Sensor

Stage 1 answer: 25

Search "percentage of marine species live on coral reefs." Coral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor but are estimated to support about 25% of all known marine species.

Stage 2 (final) answer: 39

Stage 1's answer (25) plus the depth planted in Mission 2 (14 meters): 25 + 14 = 39.

Why this is the only answer

Both the percentage (verified fact) and the depth (fixed story detail) are single, unambiguous numbers, so their sum has exactly one correct value.

Source / fact used

Stage 1 verified via web search summarizing NOAA Fisheries and UNEP: reefs occupy under 1% of the ocean floor yet support roughly 25% of marine species. Stage 2's depth (14 meters) traces back to the Mission 2 Field Notebook plant.

What the Reef Taught You

Of the eight possible reward words (curiosity, courage, discovery, creativity, teamwork, kindness, wisdom, perseverance), "discovery" is the one that specifically names the moment of finding something new, rather than the drive to look (curiosity) or the act of making something (creativity).
