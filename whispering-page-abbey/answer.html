Mission 02 · Easy

The Four Faces of the Gospels

Answer: lion
How to get there

Searching "four evangelist symbols Mark" (or "Book of Kells evangelist symbols") shows the traditional pairing used across medieval Christian art and in the Book of Kells itself: Matthew = winged man/angel, Mark = lion, Luke = ox/calf, John = eagle.

Why this is the only answer

This symbolic scheme (traced to St. Jerome, c. 347-420 AD) is the one that "prevailed in later manuscripts and in Western Art," including the Book of Kells, so any reasonably careful search converges on lion for Mark specifically.

Mission 03 · Medium (Foundational logic — classification / odd-one-out)

Brother Anselm's Workbench

Answer: printing press
How to get there

Quill pens, iron-gall ink, gold leaf, and vellum were all standard tools of an early medieval scriptorium. The printing press is anachronistic for the year 800 — it does not belong with the other four.

Why this is the only answer

Every other item is independently verifiable as period-correct scriptorium material; the printing press is the sole item whose invention postdates the year 800 by roughly six and a half centuries, giving one clean, checkable odd-one-out.

Source / fact used

General history knowledge, cross-checked via web search: the movable-type printing press is credited to Johannes Gutenberg, c. 1440s, roughly 640 years after the c. 800 AD scriptorium setting of this puzzle.

Mission 06 · Hard (Cryptography + Mathematics — Ogham cipher with a computed shift)

The Monk's Secret Notch-Code

Answer: Folio 19
How to get there

Step 1 — find the shift: 1150 - 1146 = 4.

Step 2 — decode the raw Ogham notches using the key (Family 1 = above line = B,L,F,S,N; Family 2 = below line = H,D,T,C,Q; Family 3 = through line = M,G,NG,Z,R; Family 4 = diagonal = A,O,U,E,I). Reading the diagram left to right gives the scrambled letters: D B H S B   C S C F G F F C.

Step 3 — shift each letter backward by 4 through the 20-letter Ogham order (B-L-F-S-N-H-D-T-C-Q-M-G-NG-Z-R-A-O-U-E-I, wrapping around):
D→F, B→O, H→L, S→I, B→O  |  C→N, S→I, C→N, F→E, G→T, F→E, F→E, C→N
Result: F O L I O   N I N E T E E N = "FOLIO NINETEEN," i.e. Folio 19.

Why this is the only answer

The shift is a single forced number from real subtraction (1150-1146=4), the Ogham decoder key is a fixed real alphabet with no ambiguity between letters (each stroke count/position maps to exactly one letter), and shifting backward by exactly 4 through the 20-letter order is a deterministic, reversible operation — there is exactly one plaintext this cipher decodes to. Verified end-to-end: encoding "FOLIO NINETEEN" forward by 4 through the same order reproduces the exact cipher sequence shown in the diagram.

Source / fact used

The real Ogham alphabet's structure (20 letters, 4 families/"aicme" of 5, each letter a cluster of 1-5 strokes along a stem line, traditional order B-L-F-S-N / H-D-T-C-Q / M-G-Ng-Z-R / A-O-U-E-I) was verified via web search (Omniglot's Ogham page and Wikipedia's Ogham article, cross-referenced). The Caesar-style shift-by-a-computed-number is an invented puzzle mechanic layered on top of the real alphabet, not a claim about historical Ogham usage.

Mission 07 · Hard (Logical Reasoning — Intermediate: knights and knaves)

Three Monks, One Truth

Answer: Brendan
How to get there

Let H = who last held the page. Aidan's statement is true iff H=Brendan. Brendan's statement is true iff H≠Brendan (so Aidan and Brendan's truth-values are always exact opposites of each other, whichever holder is real). Ciaran's statement is true iff Aidan is lying.

Test all three possible holders under the rule "exactly one of the three tells the truth":

• If H=Aidan: Aidan lies, Brendan tells truth, and Ciaran (true iff Aidan lies) also tells truth → 2 truth-tellers. Rejected.
• If H=Brendan: Aidan tells truth, Brendan lies, Ciaran (true iff Aidan lies) is false → exactly 1 truth-teller (Aidan). Fits.
• If H=Ciaran: Aidan lies, Brendan tells truth, Ciaran also tells truth → 2 truth-tellers. Rejected.

Only H=Brendan produces exactly one truth-teller, so Brendan last held the page.

Why this is the only answer

All three candidate holders were tested exhaustively; only one satisfies the given "exactly one truth-teller" constraint, so the solution is forced, not guessed.

Source / fact used

Self-contained logic puzzle, solved by exhaustive case-check as shown above (no outside lookup required, per the skill's logical-reasoning rules).

Mission 08 · Hard (Critical Thinking & Media Literacy + 2nd internet research)

Two "Expert" Blurbs

Answer: Trinity College Dublin
How to get there

Blurb A is accurate: the Book of Kells was likely begun c. 800 AD by monks of the Columban community, probably starting on Iona before Viking raids (from 795 AD onward) pushed the community to Kells, and it has been kept at Trinity College Dublin since Bishop Henry Jones presented it there in 1661. Blurb B's single planted error is its claimed current location: the British Library in London. The real Book of Kells has never been housed at the British Library (that institution holds other treasures, such as the Lindisfarne Gospels — a different, though sometimes confused-with, manuscript). A quick search for "where is the Book of Kells kept today" resolves this immediately and unambiguously.

Why this is the only answer

Where a specific, named physical object is currently held is a plain fact, not a matter of interpretation, and it is trivially confirmed by any reputable source (Trinity College Dublin's own site, Wikipedia, major news coverage of the exhibit). Blurb B is otherwise phrased very plausibly, which is exactly why the child must actually check rather than assume.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: Trinity College Dublin's official library pages (tcd.ie/library/research-collections/book-of-kells.php) and Wikipedia both confirm the Book of Kells is held and displayed at Trinity College Dublin, Ireland, and has been since 1661 — not the British Library.

Mission 09 · Big "Aha!" (Logical Reasoning — Advanced: 4-category multi-constraint grid)

The Night-Watch Grid

Answer: Ciaran
How to get there

Categories: monk, cell (Gate/Nave/Tower/Garden), pigment (gold leaf/red madder/blue lapis/green verdigris), day (Monday-Thursday).

Clue 3 fixes one row completely: Garden cell = green verdigris = the last of the four days = Thursday.

Clue 1 ("gold leaf's day + 2 = Tower cell's day") has two candidate day-pairs within Monday-Thursday: (Mon,Wed) or (Tue,Thu). The (Tue,Thu) option would put the Tower cell on Thursday — but Thursday is already taken by the Garden cell (clue 3), which is impossible since cells are distinct. So gold leaf = Monday and Tower cell = Wednesday.

Clue 4 ("red madder's day = gold leaf's day + 1") gives red madder = Tuesday. With Monday=gold, Tuesday=red, Thursday=green already placed, the only day left, Wednesday, must be blue lapis — matching the Tower cell (Wednesday) found above.

So far: Monday=gold/?cell, Tuesday=red/?cell, Wednesday=blue/Tower, Thursday=green/Garden. Only Gate and Nave remain, for Monday and Tuesday in some order.

Clues 5 and 6 together: Aidan = gold leaf (clue 5), and the Gate cell = gold leaf (clue 6), so Aidan = Gate cell = Monday.

That leaves Nave cell = Tuesday = red madder, for whichever monk is not yet placed.

Clue 2: Brendan is not Tower or Garden, so Brendan must be Gate or Nave — Gate is taken by Aidan, so Brendan = Nave = Tuesday = red madder.

Only Ciaran and Declan remain, for Tower (Wednesday, blue lapis) and Garden (Thursday, green verdigris). Clue 7: Ciaran did not tend the garden, so Ciaran = Tower = Wednesday, and Declan = Garden = Thursday.

Full solution: Aidan-Gate-gold leaf-Monday; Brendan-Nave-red madder-Tuesday; Ciaran-Tower-blue lapis-Wednesday; Declan-Garden-green verdigris-Thursday.

Since the page vanished during Wednesday night, the Wednesday watch-keeper, Ciaran, is the culprit.

Solved by hand from only the seven given clues (no branching guesses needed): clue 3 anchors one full row, clue 1 is disambiguated by the pigeonhole conflict with clue 3, clues 4-6 chain forward from there, and clues 2 and 7 resolve the final two-way splits. Every one of the 4x4=16 monk/cell/pigment/day assignments is pinned down, leaving exactly one consistent grid.
