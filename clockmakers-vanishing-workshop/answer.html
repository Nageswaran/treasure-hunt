Mission 01 · Easy

The Telegram on the Workbench

Answer: 14:00
How to get there

Amsterdam (Netherlands) is always exactly 1 hour ahead of Greenwich, England, all year round — both places switch to daylight saving on the same dates, so the 1-hour gap never changes. Greenwich time 13:00 + 1 hour = 14:00 in Amsterdam.

Why this is the only answer

The 1-hour offset is constant year-round (CET is UTC+1 vs GMT UTC+0 in winter; CEST is UTC+2 vs BST UTC+1 in summer — always a 1-hour gap), so there is exactly one correct time regardless of season. Accept only the 24-hour form "14:00" as specified in the puzzle.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search (timeanddate.com / greenwichmeantime.com world clock converters): the Netherlands is always exactly one hour ahead of the United Kingdom.

Mission 02 · Easy

The Apprentice's Dutch Notes

Answer: de het
How to get there

"Slinger" (pendulum) is a de-word: "de slinger." "Rad" (wheel/gear) is a het-word: "het rad" (as in "het Rad van Fortuin," Dutch for "Wheel of Fortune"). Typed in order: "de het".

Why this is the only answer

Dutch nouns each take exactly one fixed article; "de slinger" and "het rad" are the standard, dictionary-confirmed forms — there is no acceptable alternative pairing.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against Dutch lidwoord ("de/het") reference sites (welklidwoord.nl, deofhet.expert, woorden.org) for both "slinger" and "rad."

Mission 06 · Hard

The Wilted Garden Vines

Answer: moonflower
How to get there

Morning glory (Ipomoea) opens around sunrise and closes by midday — it would be shut long before 11 PM. Moonflower (Ipomoea alba) does the opposite: its large white blooms open at dusk, stay open through the night, and close again in the morning sunlight. A flower wide open and glowing white at 11 PM must be a moonflower.

Why this is the only answer

The two flowers' schedules are opposites, and 11 PM falls squarely inside the moonflower's open window and squarely outside the morning glory's — there's no time-of-day ambiguity.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: multiple gardening references (bybrittanygoldwyn.com, nybg.org Plant Talk, gardenista.com) confirm morning glories close by midday and moonflowers open at dusk and close at sunrise.

The Coded Drawer

Stage 1 answer: clockwork
Stage 2 (final) answer: 972
How to get there

Stage 1: "JSVJRDVYR" was made by shifting each letter of the plaintext 7 places forward in the alphabet. Shifting each letter of the ciphertext back 7 places decodes it to "CLOCKWORK" (J→C, S→L, V→O, J→C, R→K, D→W, V→O, Y→R, R→K).

Stage 2: "CLOCKWORK" has 9 letters, so there are 9 gear-clusters. One cluster: 24 + 36 + 48 = 108 teeth. Total: 108 × 9 = 972 teeth.

Why this is the only answer

The Caesar shift is fully deterministic (only one decoding matches a real English word), and the letter-count-to-cluster-count link is stated explicitly, so both stages resolve to exactly one number.

Source / fact used

Self-contained cipher and arithmetic puzzle; cipher shift and multiplication both recomputed independently before publishing (see verification script output: CLOCKWORK ↔ JSVJRDVYR confirmed both directions; 108 × 9 = 972 confirmed).

Mission 09 · Big Aha! (Real-World Decision-Making)

Two Repair Shops, One Decision

Answer: 195
How to get there

Shop A: €180 + €15 delivery = €195 total. Shop B: 20% off €250 = €250 × 0.8 = €200, then + €10 rush fee = €210 total. €195 (Shop A) is lower than €210 (Shop B), so Shop A is the better deal and 195 is the answer.

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle asks for the single lower total in euros, and only one of the two shops' totals is lower — the "20% off" framing is a deliberate trap, since it looks cheaper at first glance but isn't once the rush fee is included.

Source / fact used

Self-contained financial-literacy puzzle; both totals recomputed independently before publishing (180+15=195; 250×0.8+10=210).

Grand Finale

The Vault Behind the Grandfather Clock

Answer: 1904
How to get there

Part one: the brass plaque from Mission 1 reads "Ticking Since 1847." Part two: the 12-chime noon salute has 12 chimes at 2 seconds each (24 seconds) plus 11 silent gaps between them (not 12 — there's one fewer gap than chimes) at 3 seconds each (33 seconds); 24 + 33 = 57 seconds total. Vault code: 1847 + 57 = 1904.

Why this is the only answer

The plaque year is a fixed, previously-noted detail, and the chime-timing math is fully specified (2 seconds per chime, 3 seconds per gap, 12 chimes → 11 gaps) — the classic "fencepost" trap of counting 12 gaps instead of 11 is the only way to get a different, wrong number.

Source / fact used

Self-contained puzzle combining the Mission 1 recalled detail with an internally consistent arithmetic sequence; recomputed independently before publishing (12×2 + 11×3 = 57; 1847+57=1904).
