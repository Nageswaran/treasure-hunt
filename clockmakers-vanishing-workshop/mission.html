Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Clockmaker's Vanishing Workshop

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes
Master clockmaker Joost Verhoeven has vanished from his canal-side workshop in Gouda, the night before he was set to unveil his masterpiece: a clock said to never lose a second in a hundred years. His apprentice found the workshop door unlocked, gears scattered across the workbench, and one note pinned to the door: "Follow the time, and you'll follow me." The Internet Detective Academy has been called in. Follow every tick, tock, and clue — and find him before the Grand Unveiling.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Telegram on the Workbench

📓 Agent's Notebook: A brass plaque by the workshop door reads "Ticking Since 1847." Jot this down — case-file numbers like this have a way of mattering later.

On the workbench sits an unfinished telegram. It was sent from the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, England — home of the Prime Meridian, the invisible line where the world's clocks are set from. The telegram is stamped 13:00, Greenwich time.

Search to find out: is Amsterdam ever in the exact same time zone as Greenwich? If the telegram left Greenwich at 13:00, what time would it be, at that very same moment, on the workshop's clock in Amsterdam? Type the time in 24-hour format, like 09:00.

Hint 1

Search for "time difference Amsterdam London." The Netherlands and the United Kingdom are close neighbours on the map — but are they ever on the same clock?

MISSION 02
EASY

The Apprentice's Dutch Notes

Taped inside a drawer is a torn sentence from Meneer Verhoeven's notebook, with two words rubbed away:

"Achter het glas hangt ___ slinger, die ___ rad in beweging houdt."
(Behind the glass hangs the ___ pendulum, which keeps the ___ wheel moving.)

Each blank needs either "de" or "het" — Dutch has two little words for "the," and every noun only ever takes one of them. Figure out which word fits "slinger" (pendulum) and which fits "rad" (wheel), then type both, in order, separated by a space (for example: het de).

Hint 1

"Rad" behaves like its cousin "het wiel" — most words for wheel-like machine parts in Dutch take "het." "Slinger" behaves more like an ordinary object, the way most Dutch nouns do.

💡 Did you know? The world's oldest working clock has been ticking at Salisbury Cathedral in England since before the year 1386 — over 600 years! It doesn't even have a face, because it was only ever built to strike the hours, not show the time.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Missing Backing Board

Behind the workshop's tall case clock is a wooden backing board. Its outer edge measures 18 cm wide and 24 cm tall — but a rectangular slot has been cut from the bottom edge so the pendulum can swing freely through it: 6 cm wide and 8 cm tall, cut straight up from the middle of the bottom edge.

The wood is everything inside the outline — except the notch cut out at the bottom.

What is the area of the remaining wood, in square centimeters? Grab a pencil for this one. Type just the number.

Hint 1

Try splitting the shape into simpler rectangles, the way a real carpenter would plan the cut.

Hint 2

One way: find the area of the full outer rectangle first (18 × 24). Then subtract the area of the missing notch (6 × 8).

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Five Timekeepers on a Shelf

On a dusty shelf, the team finds five timekeeping devices: a grandfather clock, a cuckoo clock, a digital wristwatch, a battery-powered quartz wall clock, and a sundial.

Picture two overlapping circles: one labeled "Has a swinging pendulum," the other labeled "Hangs on a wall." Exactly one of the five devices belongs in both circles at once — in the overlap in the middle. Which one?

Hint 1

Where would a grandfather clock go — it definitely has a pendulum, but does it hang on a wall, or stand on the floor?

Hint 2

A grandfather clock has a pendulum but stands on the floor. A quartz wall clock hangs on the wall but has no pendulum. Which single object has BOTH features?

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Journal Entry

A page in Meneer Verhoeven's journal reads:

"I never met the man, but every pendulum clock I've ever built owes its steady heartbeat to him. He was Dutch, like me — a mathematician, astronomer, and clockmaker who built the first working pendulum clock in 1656 and patented it a year later. Strangely, he's just as famous for pointing a telescope at the sky: he discovered Saturn's largest moon. Who is he?"

Search to find this scientist's name, then type his last name.

Hint 1

Search "first pendulum clock inventor 1656." He was Dutch, just like Meneer Verhoeven.

Hint 2

Double-check your answer by searching his name together with "Titan moon Saturn" — the same scientist should come up both times.

💡 Did you know? "Big Ben" is actually just the nickname of the giant bell inside London's famous clock tower. The tower itself is officially called the Elizabeth Tower — it was renamed in 2012 in honour of Queen Elizabeth II.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Wilted Garden Vines

Behind the workshop is a small garden with two wilted flower vines and a note: "I planted these so I'd never need a clock in the garden — flowers can tell time too, if you know how to read them."

The two vines are morning glory and moonflower — close plant relatives that keep exactly opposite hours. Search to find out each flower's daily schedule: when does it open, and when does it close?

Then answer this: if the detective team found a patch of these flowers wide open and glowing white at 11 PM, which vine were they looking at?

Hint 1

Search "morning glory opening time" and "moonflower opening time" separately — write down both schedules before you decide.

Hint 2

One flower loves sunrise and naps all afternoon. The other only wakes up after dark, like an owl. Which one would still be open at 11 PM?

MISSION 07
HARD

Four Apprentices, Four Rooms

On the night Meneer Verhoeven vanished, his four apprentices — Anna, Bram, Cato, and Daan — were each somewhere in the building: the Workshop, the Cellar, the Attic, or the Shop-front. Each apprentice was in a different room. Here's what the team found out:

  • Anna was not in the Workshop and not in the Shop-front.
  • Bram swears he never went upstairs the entire evening (so he wasn't in the Attic).
  • Cato spent the whole night polishing gears — a job that only ever happens in the Workshop.
  • Daan was counting the day's coins — a job that only ever happens in the Shop-front.

Who was in the Attic when the church bells struck — and might have seen Meneer Verhoeven slip out the top window?

Hint 1

Start with the clues that give a direct, certain answer — which apprentices have a job that only happens in one exact room?

Hint 2

Once Cato and Daan's rooms are locked in, only the Cellar and the Attic are left — for only Anna and Bram. One clue rules Bram out of the Attic. Where does that leave him, and where does that leave Anna?

MISSION 08
HARD

The Coded Drawer

Stage 1 of 2

Inside a locked desk drawer is a scrap of paper with one word on it:

JSVJRDVYR

A note beneath it explains: "Every letter here has been shifted 7 places FORWARD in the alphabet. Shift each one back 7 places to read my word." (For example, if a letter had shifted to H, it started as A.) Grab a pencil and write the alphabet out to help you count.

Hint 1

Write the alphabet in a row, A to Z. For each letter in JSVJRDVYR, count back 7 letters (wrapping around from A back to Z if you run past the start).

Stage 2 of 2

Count the letters in the word you just decoded. That's exactly how many identical gear-clusters fill the workshop's tall case clock. Each cluster holds 3 gears, with 24, 36, and 48 teeth.

First add the teeth in one cluster. Then multiply that total by the number of clusters, to find the TOTAL number of teeth in the whole clock. Grab a pencil — this one's worth doing on paper!

Hint 1

24 + 36 + 48 = the teeth in one cluster. Then multiply that number by however many clusters you counted in Stage 1.

💡 Did you know? Clocks turn "clockwise" because that's the direction shadows move on a sundial in the Northern Hemisphere. If mechanical clocks had first been invented south of the equator, they might spin the opposite way today!

MISSION 09
AHA!

Two Repair Shops, One Decision

The masterpiece clock's glass face cracked in the chaos. Two repair shops in town can fix it before the unveiling:

  • Shop A: a flat fee of €180, plus a €15 delivery charge.
  • Shop B: "20% off our usual €250 price!" — but with a €10 rush fee added on top, since it's urgent.

Work out the TOTAL price at each shop, including every fee. Which shop actually costs less — and what is that lower total, in euros? Type just the number (for example: 199).

Hint 1

Work out each shop's total separately, and don't stop at the discount percentage — keep going until every single fee has been added in.

Hint 2

20% off €250 is not Shop B's final price — there's still one more fee to add after the discount.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Vault Behind the Grandfather Clock

Behind the tallest grandfather clock, the team finds a small vault with a four-digit dial and one final card from Meneer Verhoeven:

"My vault's code has two parts, added together. Part one: the year carved into my workshop's brass plaque — the one you noted the day you arrived. Part two: the exact number of seconds from the very start of the first chime to the very end of the last chime, when the tower clock strikes its 12-chime noon salute — where each chime itself lasts 2 seconds, and each silence between chimes lasts 3 seconds. Add the two parts together for my vault's code."

Hint 1

First work out the total time, in seconds, for all 12 chimes and the silences between them. Then add a year you noted down earlier in this case.

Hint 2

There are 12 chimes, but only 11 silent gaps between them — it's easy to accidentally count 12 gaps instead of 11. Work out the seconds first, then add the year.

Case closed?

The vault holds no gold — only a small card in Meneer Verhoeven's handwriting: "Every gear, every flower, every chime rewards the detective who looks closer before leaping to an answer. That patient, careful way of seeing the world has a name: WISDOM. Well done, Agent — the workshop, and its clockmaker, are found."
