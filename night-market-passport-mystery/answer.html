Mission 01 · Easy · Geography & World Knowledge

The Postcard from the Donation Box

Answer: Switzerland
How to get there

The flag described (and drawn) is a red field with a plain white cross in the center, and — the key detail — it is a perfect square, not a rectangle. This is the flag of Switzerland. Searching "only square national flag in the world" or "red flag white cross square" leads directly to Switzerland.

Why this is the only answer

Switzerland and Vatican City are the only two countries with square national flags, and Vatican City's flag (yellow and white, with a papal tiara and crossed keys) looks nothing like a red field with a plain white cross — so the description given is unambiguous.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against multiple sources (Wikipedia's "Flag of Switzerland," Britannica, swissinfo.ch) confirming: red field, white cross, square proportions, and that Switzerland and Vatican City are the only two countries with square flags. Also verified: the Red Cross emblem was created by inverting the colors of the Swiss flag, in honor of Swiss founder Henry Dunant and the first Geneva Convention.

Mission 02 · Easy · Dutch Language

Uncle Theo's Word Cart

Answer: muntje
How to get there

"Muntje" is the diminutive ("little coin/token") form of "munt" (coin). Dutch diminutives — words ending in -je, -tje, -pje, -etje, etc. — always take "het" in the singular, regardless of the gender of the original word. The other four words (paspoort, kraam, markt, stempel) are ordinary nouns, not diminutives.

Why this is the only answer

Of the five words, only "muntje" carries a diminutive ending (-je). The rule that diminutives are always "het" is absolute in standard Dutch, so it's the one word guaranteed to be "het" no matter what.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search on Dutch grammar references (Dutch Grammar Forum, Dutchdiction, and general Dutch grammar guides), all confirming: singular diminutive nouns always take "het," regardless of the base noun's normal article.

Mission 04 · Medium · Food & Health (Quick-Learn)

Aunty Bronwyn's Welsh Cakes

Answer: Sugar
How to get there

The EU's official 14 major allergens are: cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame seeds, sulphites, lupin, and molluscs. Checking the five bolded label words — Wheat flour, Sugar, Egg, Milk, Sesame seeds — against that list: Wheat flour (gluten), Egg, Milk, and Sesame seeds are all genuinely on the list. Sugar is not.

Why this is the only answer

Sugar has no allergen classification under the official 14-allergen list — it's the one bolded ingredient with no match, making it the single, unambiguous mistake.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against EU food-labeling sources summarizing Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, which lists the 14 major allergens that must be typographically emphasized (bold, italic, underlined, etc.) within a packaged food's ingredients list.

Mission 06 · Hard · Logical Reasoning (Intermediate — logic grid)

Four Vendors, Four Passport Stamps

Answer: Romania
How to get there

Full solved grid: Chidi — Kenya — Stall 3 — Coffee. Anna — Ecuador — Stall 6 — Chocolate. Dorota — Romania — Stall 9 — Honey. Bilal — Oman — Stall 12 — Dates.

Step-by-step: Clue 1 forces Chidi = stall 3 (the only single-digit option under 6). Clue 3 then gives Anna = 6 (double of 3). Clue 5 gives Bilal = 12 (the highest). By elimination, Dorota = stall 9. Clue 2 then says stall 9 sells honey, so Dorota sells honey. Clue 6 says the Ecuador vendor is at stall 6, so Anna = Ecuador. Clue 8 says Chidi sells coffee; clue 4 says the Kenya vendor sells coffee and isn't at the highest stall (ruling out Bilal for Kenya) — so Chidi = Kenya. Of the two countries left (Oman, Romania) for Bilal and Dorota: clue 7 says the Oman vendor sells dates; Dorota already sells honey, so Dorota can't be Oman — Dorota = Romania, Bilal = Oman (and indeed sells dates, confirming clue 7). Anna gets the one remaining item, chocolate.

Why this is the only answer

Solved independently from only the 8 given clues, with every value forced by elimination at each step — no branch ever required a guess, confirming a single solution.

Source / fact used

Self-contained logic grid; no outside lookup required or intended.

Mission 07 · Hard · Cryptography

The Loyalty Stamp Cipher

Answer: check the drum
How to get there

The shift number is 9 (Booth 9, planted in Mission 02). The ciphertext "LQNLT CQN MADV" was created by shifting the plaintext "CHECK THE DRUM" forward by 9 letters each. Shifting each ciphertext letter BACKWARD by 9 recovers: L→C, Q→H, N→E, L→C, T→K (word: CHECK); C→T, Q→H, N→E (word: THE); M→D, A→R, D→U, V→M (word: DRUM). Full message: CHECK THE DRUM.

Why this is the only answer

A Caesar shift has exactly one correct decode for a given shift amount; since the shift amount (9) was fixed by the planted detail, there is only one valid plaintext.

Source / fact used

Self-contained cipher, verified by encoding "CHECK THE DRUM" with a +9 shift programmatically and confirming it reproduces "LQNLT CQN MADV," then decoding back to the original with a -9 shift.

Mission 09 · Hard · Big Aha! (Real-World Decision-Making — Financial Literacy)

Two Booths, One Better Deal

Answer: 20
How to get there

Golden Falcon Exchange: €40 × 3 tokens/€ = 120 tokens (no fee). River Booth Exchange: since €40 is under the €50 fee-free threshold, subtract the €5 fee first: €40 − €5 = €35, then €35 × 4 tokens/€ = 140 tokens. River Booth's 140 tokens is the better deal, beating Golden Falcon's 120 by exactly 140 − 120 = 20 tokens.

Why this is the only answer

Both totals are fixed by the stated rates, fee, and threshold — there's no ambiguity in which booth is better or by how much. The point of the puzzle is that the higher-rate booth is still better even after its fee, which only shows up by computing both totals in full rather than assuming the fee makes it worse.

Source / fact used

Self-contained financial-literacy comparison with fixed, invented in-story rates (not real, time-sensitive exchange rates). Flat service fees alongside a per-unit exchange rate are a genuine, common real-world currency-exchange practice, which was verified via web search.

Grand Finale · Logical Reasoning (Advanced — working backward)

The Drum, the Rounds, and the Truth

Answer: teamwork
How to get there

Let Round 1 = R. Round 2 = R + 15, Round 3 = R + 25. The equation is R + (R+15) + (R+25) + 80 = 300, which simplifies to 3R + 120 = 300, so 3R = 180 and R = 60. Since 60 is more than 50, the flags are read top to bottom as given: Turkey, Ecuador, Argentina, Morocco, Wales, Oman, Romania, Kenya. Taking the first letter of each — T, E, A, M, W, O, R, K — spells TEAMWORK.

Why this is the only answer

The equation R + (R+15) + (R+25) + 80 = 300 is linear with exactly one solution (R = 60), independently re-derived and checked (60 + 75 + 85 + 80 = 300 ✓). Because 60 > 50, the reading direction is unambiguously top-to-bottom, which is the only order that spells a real English word — reading bottom-to-top would give the nonsense string "KROWMAET," which itself confirms to the child that they chose the right direction.

Source / fact used

Self-contained arithmetic and acrostic puzzle. The eight flags used across the mission set (Turkey, Ecuador, Argentina, Morocco, Wales, Oman, Romania, Kenya) were each introduced naturally in earlier missions (Missions 1, 3, 5, 6, and 4).

Reward revealed

The reward value is teamwork. It's revealed by an acrostic built from the first letters of the closing ceremony's eight flags, read in the order determined by the finale's working-backward math — literally spelling out the value that solved the whole case: no single clue, stall, or volunteer solved it alone.
