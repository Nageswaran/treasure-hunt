Mission 01 · Easy · Geography

The Flooded Province

Answer: Zeeland
How to get there

Search "1953 North Sea flood Netherlands province." Zeeland suffered the most deaths (865) and the worst flooding of any Dutch province in the disaster of 31 January–1 February 1953, though South Holland and North Brabant also flooded.

Why this is the only answer

Every reputable source (Zeeuws Archief, Britannica, Wikipedia) names Zeeland as hardest-hit by both casualties and flooded area, so no other province is a defensible answer.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against Wikipedia: North Sea flood of 1953 and the Zeeuws Archief (Zeeland: 865 deaths vs. 677 in South Holland and 247 in North Brabant).

Mission 02 · Easy · Cryptography

The Caesar's Note

Answer: PILLARS
How to get there

TMPPEVW shifted backward by 4: T→P, M→I, P→L, P→L, E→A, V→R, W→S = PILLARS.

Why this is the only answer

A Caesar shift is a fixed, reversible substitution — there is exactly one decoding for a given shift amount.

Source / fact used

Pure cipher math, computed and double-checked with a script (encode PILLARS +4 → TMPPEVW; decode TMPPEVW −4 → PILLARS).

Mission 03 · Medium · Mathematics (geometry)

The Control Room's Missing Floor Space

Answer: 58 (square meters)
How to get there

Split the L-shape into two rectangles: the full outer rectangle is 10 m × 7 m = 70 m². The missing notch, cut from the top-right corner, is 4 m × 3 m = 12 m². Subtract: 70 − 12 = 58 m².

Why this is the only answer

The diagram's labeled sides are self-consistent: the top splits into 6 m + 4 m = 10 m (matching the bottom), and the right side splits into 3 m + 4 m = 7 m (matching the left side), so there is exactly one way to read the shape and exactly one resulting area.

Source / fact used

Self-drawn fictional diagram (SVG), area recomputed by hand and by script to confirm 70 − 12 = 58.

Mission 07 · Hard · Dutch Language

The Soggy Logbook Page

Answer: water
How to get there

"De dijk" (the dike), "de sluis" (the lock/sluice), and "de storm" (the storm) all take "de." Only "het water" (the water) takes "het" — it's the odd one out.

Why this is the only answer

Dutch grammatical gender is fixed per word; "dijk," "sluis," and "storm" are all de-woorden, and "water" is the only het-woord in the set, confirmed against Dutch reference sources.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against Dutch lidwoord (article) reference sites for "de sluis," "de dijk," "de storm," and "het water."

Forty-Some Years of Struggle

Answer: 44 (years)
How to get there

Search "Delta Works completed year Maeslantkering" — the Delta Works were officially finished in 1997, with the completion of the Maeslantkering near Rotterdam. 1997 − 1953 = 44 years.

Why this is the only answer

1953 (the flood) is fixed and stated in the puzzle; 1997 is the one year multiple reputable sources give for the Delta Works' completion, so the subtraction has exactly one correct result.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against the Delta Works and Maeslantkering articles, and the Watersnoodmuseum's history of the Delta Works (construction 1991–1997, finished 1997).

Mission 09 · Big "Aha!" Moment · Critical Thinking + Logical Reasoning (Advanced — working backward)

Two Claims and a Vanishing Hour

Stage 1 answer: 237
How to get there (Stage 1)

Search "Maeslantkering truss length meters." Each steel truss really is 237 meters long — that part of Website B is accurate. The often-repeated claim that this makes the arm "as long as the Eiffel Tower is tall" (Website A) does not hold up: the Eiffel Tower is 330 meters tall, noticeably taller than 237 meters. The true, verifiable number is 237.

Why this is the only answer

237 meters is the figure given by engineering sources for the truss length itself, independent of the flawed size comparison — it's a real measurement, not an estimate open to interpretation.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: Maeslantkering trusses are 237 m long (multiple engineering sources); Eiffel Tower height is 330 m (official Eiffel Tower site). The "as tall as the Eiffel Tower" comparison is a commonly repeated but inaccurate claim — a deliberate red herring for this mission's critical-thinking check.

Stage 2 (final) answer: 20 (minutes)
How to get there (Stage 2)

Count backward 237 minutes (3 hours 57 minutes) from the reboot time, 23:41: 23:41 − 3:57 = 19:44. That's when the power went out. The storm warning was issued at 19:24. 19:44 − 19:24 = 20 minutes.

Why this is the only answer

Every value in the chain (reboot time, the 237-minute reboot delay from Stage 1, and the storm-warning time) is fixed and stated, so working backward through the chain produces exactly one power-loss time and exactly one gap in minutes.

Source / fact used

In-story arithmetic chain; double-checked by script: 23:41 minus 237 minutes = 19:44; 19:44 minus 19:24 = 20 minutes.

Why this is the only answer

A Caesar shift of a fixed amount has exactly one valid decoding; the badge number (7) is the only number planted earlier in the case for this purpose, so no other shift produces a sensible English word.

Source / fact used

Pure cipher math, computed and verified with a script (encode PERSEVERANCE +7 → WLYZLCLYHUJL; decode −7 → PERSEVERANCE back).
