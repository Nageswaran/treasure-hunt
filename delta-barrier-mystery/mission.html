Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Delta Barrier Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-95 minutes

Last night, the storm alarm at the giant Oosterscheldekering barrier went silent — hours before the biggest storm of the year. The barrier's oldest keeper, Engineer Dekker, has vanished from the control building, leaving only strange notes behind. The Zeeland Water Authority needs a sharp detective to find out what happened before the next high tide hits. Solve every clue Dekker left, and you'll uncover the value that has protected this land for generations.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Flooded Province

Before you can find Engineer Dekker, you need to know where his control room is. It stands behind a huge storm barrier, in a province (a big region of the Netherlands, like a state) that was hit hardest by a terrible flood on the night of 31 January 1953.

Search online to find which Dutch province suffered the worst damage and the most lives lost in that flood. Type the name of the province.

Hint 1

Try searching "1953 North Sea flood Netherlands" and look for the province that is named as the hardest-hit, again and again.

💡 Did you know? About 26% of the Netherlands lies below sea level. That's one big reason storm barriers like the Oosterscheldekering matter so much.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Caesar's Note

Taped to the control room door, you find a note in code, next to a torn staff badge. Dekker always uses a "Caesar cipher" — a secret code where every letter is shifted forward through the alphabet by the same number of steps.

Dekker's shift is always 4. Shift each letter of this code backward by 4 to read his message:

TMPPEVW

Type the decoded word — it's the thing you should look at closely next.

Agent's Notebook Tucked beside the note is Dekker's torn staff badge. Only one number survived the tear: 7. This feels like something worth remembering...
Hint 1

For each letter, count backward 4 letters in the alphabet. If you go past A, wrap around to the end (Z, Y, ...).

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Control Room's Missing Floor Space

The control room floor is shaped like an L, with a smaller room cut out of one corner for equipment. Look at the marked measurements below and find the total floor area, in square meters, of the whole L-shaped room.

Split the L-shape into two simple rectangles, find each area, then add them together.
Hint 1

Draw one straight line across the shape to turn it into two rectangles.

Hint 2

The whole outer rectangle would be 10 m by 7 m. Now subtract the small notch that's missing (4 m by 3 m).

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Which Control Room?

The silent alarm sensor is hiding in one of four rooms: Room A, B, C, or D. Use these clues to cross off the wrong rooms, one by one, until only one is left.

1. The sensor is not in Room A — that room sits closest to the water and is too damp for electronics.
2. Rooms B and D both face west. The sensor's room faces east.
3. Room B is used only for storing tools, never sensors.

Which room is it? Type just the letter.

Hint 1

Start by crossing off the room closest to the water — clue 1 rules it out completely.

Hint 2

Of the rooms still left, cross off the ones that face the wrong direction.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Barrier's Trigger Point

The barrier's gates don't stay closed all the time — they only close when the sea gets dangerously high. Search for how the Oosterscheldekering decides when to close its gates. Use a trustworthy source, like Rijkswaterstaat (the government water agency) or the Watersnoodmuseum (flood museum).

Find out: at how many meters above NAP (the Netherlands' official sea-level reference height) does the barrier's computer decide the gates must close? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Search "Oosterscheldekering closes water level NAP" and look for the automatic closing rule.

Hint 2

The trigger height is a single small whole number of meters — not a decimal, not a big number.

💡 Did you know? Each of the barrier's 65 concrete pillars weighs about 18,000 tonnes — roughly as much as 3,000 elephants standing on top of each other!

MISSION 06
HARD

Four Inspectors, One Missing Notebook

Four inspectors checked the barrier's gates last night: Anke, Bram, Cor, and Dana. Each one checked a different gate section (1, 2, 3, or 4) and carried a different tool (flashlight, clipboard, radio, or key ring).

Grab a pencil for this one — draw a small grid with the four names down the side, and "gate" and "tool" across the top. Use these clues to fill it in:

1. Bram carried the flashlight.
2. The inspector with the flashlight checked Gate Section 1.
3. Dana did not carry the radio or the key ring.
4. The inspector with the clipboard checked Gate Section 2.
5. Anke did not check Gate Section 4.
6. Cor carried the key ring.

Which inspector checked Gate Section 2 — the section nearest the silent alarm?

Hint 1

Start with the flashlight clues — they pin down one full match right away (name, tool, and gate).

Hint 2

Once you know who's left without the radio or key ring, one clue tells you their gate directly.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Soggy Logbook Page

Dekker's logbook fell in a puddle. Most of the ink smudged away, but four Dutch words survived: dijk, sluis, storm, water.

Three of these words use "de" (like "de dijk"). One of them is different — it uses "het" instead. Find the odd one out and type it (in Dutch).

Hint 1

In Dutch, most nouns use "de" — only a smaller group uses "het". Say "de" and "het" out loud before each word.

Hint 2

Three of the four words name things you can build or things that happen (a wall, a gate, a weather event). The fourth names something natural that flows — that one is different.

MISSION 08
HARD

Forty-Some Years of Struggle

After the 1953 flood, the Netherlands built a huge system of barriers and dams to make sure it never happened again — the Delta Works. Search online to find the year the Delta Works were officially declared finished, when the very last piece (the Maeslantkering, near Rotterdam) was completed.

Now calculate: how many years passed between the 1953 flood and the year the Delta Works were finished? Type just the number of years.

Hint 1

Search "Delta Works completed year Maeslantkering" to find the finishing year.

Hint 2

Subtract 1953 from the finishing year you found. Grab a pencil if the subtraction feels tricky.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

Two Claims and a Vanishing Hour

Stage 1 of 2

While searching, you find two websites about a different, even bigger barrier near Rotterdam, called the Maeslantkering.

Website A says: "Each giant arm of the Maeslantkering is as long as the Eiffel Tower is tall."
Website B says: "Each arm's steel truss measures 237 meters."

The real Eiffel Tower stands 330 meters tall. Search to check both claims for yourself. One of them doesn't hold up once you compare the real numbers — but it does contain one true number hiding inside it. Type that true number (just the number of meters).

Hint 1

Search "Maeslantkering truss length meters" to find the real measurement, then compare it to the Eiffel Tower's real height.

Stage 2 of 2 (final)

That number turns out to matter more than you thought. The control room's backup system automatically reboots itself exactly that many minutes after any power loss.

Investigators found the system rebooted, and the log shows the exact time: 23:41. They also know the storm warning was first issued at 19:24.

Work backward from the reboot time — using the number from Stage 1 as the number of minutes — to find the exact time the power actually went out. Then figure out: how many minutes after the storm warning did the power go out? Type just the number of minutes.

Hint 1

First find the power-loss time: count backward from 23:41 by the number of minutes you found in Stage 1 (that's a few hours and some minutes).

Hint 2

Once you have the power-loss time, compare it to when the storm warning went out at 19:24 — the gap between them, in minutes, is your answer.

💡 Did you know? Zeeland's official motto is "Luctor et Emergo" — Latin for "I struggle and emerge." It's been part of the province's coat of arms since 1602.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Vault of Forty Years

You've traced everything. Dekker never vanished — he was in the deepest server room the whole time, chasing the real fault, and the barrier closed just in time.

Before he reappears, he leaves one last coded message, meant only for the detective sharp enough to finish the case:

WLYZLCLYHUJL

Shift every letter backward using the one number you should have written down back when you first read his torn badge. Decode the message to reveal the value this whole case was really about.

Hint 1

This is another Caesar cipher. You already found the shift number earlier — it was on Dekker's badge.

Hint 2

Shift each letter backward by that number. If you go past A, wrap around to the end of the alphabet (Z, Y, ...).

Case closed?

Just like the engineers who spent over 40 years building the Delta Works after 1953, and just like Zeeland's old motto promises, this land survives every storm through perseverance — struggling on, and always emerging. Case closed, Detective.
