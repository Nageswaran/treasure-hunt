Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Midnight Metro Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 70-90 minutes
Tonight, London's Underground is running a special after-hours heritage tour through its oldest tunnels — but just before the doors open, the vintage signal room falls eerily silent, and a brass lever from the very first opening-day ceremony has vanished from its display case. The station master needs a sharp mind before the midnight train departs. Solve all ten mysteries hidden along the platforms, and the final signal will reveal the quality that's kept this railway running for over a century.
MISSION 01
EASY

The World's First

A brass plaque near the entrance is worn smooth, but you can still make out: "...the world's very first underground railway, opened right here in London." Search online to find the exact year London's Underground first opened to passengers.

Hint 1

Try searching "London Underground first opened year." It happened in the 1860s.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Old Departure Board

An old mechanical departure board is stuck mid-countdown, clicking down the minutes until the next train: 15, 12, 9, 6, ?. What number comes next?

Agent's notebook: while you watch the board, you notice the night conductor's badge, stamped with the number 23. Worth remembering.

Hint 1

Look at how much smaller each number gets compared to the one before it.

💡 Did you know? On its very first day of service in 1863, London's Underground carried more than 30,000 passengers — even though nobody had ever ridden an underground train before!

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Racing the Rails

Two heritage trains leave the station tonight. Train 1 departs at 11:47 and takes 38 minutes to reach the final stop. Train 2 departs 12 minutes after Train 1, and takes 51 minutes to reach the same final stop. Work out each train's exact arrival time, then find out: by how many minutes does the earlier train arrive before the other one? Grab a pencil for this one!

Hint 1

Work out each arrival time one at a time. For Train 1: 11:47 plus 38 minutes — carry over into the next hour once you pass 60 minutes.

Hint 2

Train 2 departs at 11:59. Add 51 minutes to that to find its arrival time.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Ticket Hall Floor

The old ticket hall floor is a rectangle. A restoration note says its total perimeter is 34 meters, and the diagram shows one side is 9 meters. What is the length of the other side, in meters?

Remember: a rectangle's perimeter is the sum of all four sides — two of one length, two of the other.
Hint 1

Half the perimeter equals one width plus one length.

Hint 2

Half of 34 is 17. Subtract the known side (9) from that.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Signal Room Tapper

The old telegraph tapper in the signal room left one last message in Morse code before it fell silent. Use the key below to decode it.

G = --.A = .-P = .--.
--.   .-   .--.

What word does it spell?

Hint 1

There are three groups of dots and dashes, separated by gaps — one group per letter.

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Engineers, Three Lines

Three night engineers — Priya, Tom, and Aisha — each maintain one line (Central, Circle, or District) and each found one fault tonight (a broken signal light, a cracked rail, or a jammed door). Use these clues to work out who maintains the Central line:

  1. The cracked rail was not found on the Circle or District line.
  2. The jammed door was found by the engineer on the District line.
  3. Tom does not work on the Central line.
  4. Aisha found the broken signal light.

No searching needed — work it out from the clues alone.

Hint 1

Clue 1 tells you exactly which line the cracked rail is on, even without naming it directly.

Hint 2

Once you know which line has the cracked rail and which has the jammed door, the third line's fault is forced. Then use clues 3 and 4 to place each engineer.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Odd One Out

A Dutch exchange student left a note listing four words she learned at the train station: perron (platform), spoor (track), wolk (cloud), and kaartje (ticket). Three of these belong together as things you'd find at a train station. Which one doesn't belong?

Hint 1

Think about which of these four things you would never actually see inside a train station.

💡 Did you know? London's famous colorful subway map — the one with straight lines and evenly spaced stations instead of a real geographic layout — wasn't drawn by a mapmaker at all. It was designed by an electrical draughtsman, who based its look on electrical circuit diagrams!

MISSION 08
HARD

The Signal Room Clock

Stage 1 of 2

Search online to find the year the famous diagram-style Underground map (the one described above) was first designed by its inventor, Harry Beck. Subtract 1863 — the year the Underground itself first opened — from that year. How many years passed between the two events?

Hint 1

Search "Harry Beck Tube map year designed."

Stage 2 of 2

Add the night conductor's badge number you noted a few missions back to your Stage 1 answer. Treat the total as a number of minutes counted from midnight (00:00), and write the resulting clock time in 24-hour format as HH:MM (for example, a time like 02:47 would be written just like that).

Hint 1

What number was stamped on the night conductor's badge, back at the departure board?

Hint 2

60 minutes past midnight is 01:00. Count onward from there.

MISSION 09
AHA!

Mind the Gap

Every visitor hears the famous warning: "Mind the gap." Two theories are floating around the tour group. Theory 1: it warns about the horizontal gap between the train doors and the curved platform edge. Theory 2: it warns about a height difference between the train floor and the platform.

Search online using a trustworthy source (like a transport museum) to find out which one is actually correct.

Type "horizontal" or "vertical" depending on which kind of gap it really means.

Hint 1

Search "mind the gap meaning London Underground" and look for why some platforms are more dangerous than others — it has to do with the shape of the tunnel.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Signal

The recovered brass lever controls an old bell-signal system. A faded instruction card reads:

"If the lever is RED: sound one bell when the platform is clear, or sound two bells when the platform is NOT clear.
If the lever is GREEN: always sound three bells, no matter what."

Tonight, the lever is RED, and the platform is NOT clear. How many bells should sound?

Hint 1

First check which color the lever is tonight — that decides which whole rule applies.

Hint 2

Once you're inside the RED rule, check whether the platform is clear or not clear to pick the right bell count.

Case closed?

The bell sounds twice, right on cue, and the signal lights flicker back to life just as the midnight tour doors open. It took an engineer, a conductor, a translator, and a detective — that's you — all working from different clues to get here. TEAMWORK is what's kept this railway's gears turning for over 160 years, and tonight, it kept the mystery from staying buried in the tunnels.
