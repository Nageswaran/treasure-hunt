Internet Detective Academy · Case File

Operation Current Catcher

Junior Engineer briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-95 minutes

In five days, the Blue Current Project launches CC-4 — a brand-new, V-shaped floating boom that gathers drifting plastic at Meridian Delta, where the Kelan River meets the sea, and guides it into a solar-powered collection tank. It works like real ocean-cleanup machines used in rivers and coastlines around the world today.

You've just been added to the crew as a Junior Engineer for Launch Week. Ten jobs stand between now and Launch Day: budgets, sensors, charts, contracts, and one very tricky sponsor wall. Every number has to be exactly right — get one wrong out here, and the whole system could fail once it's out on the water.

Grab a notebook. Some of what you find today, you'll need again later — without anyone reminding you.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Launch Budget

The crew's budget folder is a mess of receipts. Total launch budget: €18,500. So far, the team has spent money on:

Solar panel replacement: €4,250
Conveyor belt lubricant & maintenance: €1,180
Safety inspection fee: €950
Dock rental for Launch Day: €2,300
Crew t-shirts & banners: €640

Add up every expense, then subtract that total from the launch budget. How many euros are left over? Grab a pencil for this one — five numbers is a lot to hold in your head at once.

📓 Field notebook: pinned above the budget folder is CC-4's spec sheet — Standard collection rate: 50,000 kg/day. Optimal-conditions collection rate: 100,000 kg/day. Not needed for this job, but worth jotting down.
Hint 1

Add up every expense first, then subtract that total from the whole budget. Do it in stages on paper rather than all at once.

MISSION 02
EASY

Two Ways Plastic Hurts

The crew's marine biologist is building a poster about why CC-4 matters. She's sorting ocean debris into two groups, drawn here as two overlapping circles:

Here are three things CC-4 pulls out of the water: an abandoned fishing net, a plastic bottle cap, and a length of fishing line.

Search to check the real reports: which one of these three actually belongs in the middle of the diagram — the one item that both tangles animals up and gets swallowed by mistake?

Hint 1

Search for real reports on how a net harms animals, how a bottle cap harms animals, and how this third item harms animals. Two of the three only cause one kind of harm.

💡 Did you know? More than half of the world's sea turtles have eaten plastic at some point, usually because a floating plastic bag looks just like their favorite food: jellyfish.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Which Claim Is Right?

Two draft sentences for tomorrow's press release are sitting on the project lead's desk. Only one can go out — the other is misleading.

Claim A: "CC-4 will remove all the plastic from the ocean and solve the problem completely, forever, all by itself."

Claim B: "CC-4 can remove huge amounts of plastic, but stopping new plastic from entering the water in the first place matters just as much."

Search what real ocean-cleanup organizations actually say when people ask whether cleanup machines alone can fix ocean plastic pollution. Which claim matches what the experts say?

Hint 1

Search something like: can ocean cleanup machines solve plastic pollution alone. Read what an official cleanup organization's own website says.

Hint 2

Look for a sentence that treats cleaning up existing plastic and stopping new plastic from getting in as two separate jobs that are both needed. That idea matches only one of the two claims above.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Best Bid Wins

CC-4 needs a new mooring anchor and chain set. Two suppliers sent quotes. The anchor must be installed and pressure-tested by Thursday morning, or CC-4 cannot be certified safe for Launch Day — no exceptions.

Ironhold Marine Supply: €640, ships and arrives Wednesday.
Tidebound Rigging Co.: €575, ships and arrives Friday.

Which supplier should the crew actually order from?

Hint 1

Line up both delivery days against the Thursday-morning deadline before you even look at the prices.

Hint 2

One shipment arrives after the deadline has already passed. No price is good enough if the anchor isn't there in time to be tested.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Morning Blink Code

Every morning, CC-4's solar-powered status light blinks a short binary code to report itself ready. Each letter is sent as a 5-digit binary number, where the number simply says which letter of the alphabet it is (A is the 1st letter, so A = 00001; B is the 2nd, so B = 00010; and so on).

ABCDEFGHIJKLM
00001000100001100100001010011000111010000100101010010110110001101
NOPQRSTUVWXYZ
01110011111000010001100101001110100101011011010111110001100111010

This morning's blink pattern was:

10010   00101   00001   00100   11001

Use the table to decode the five letters into one word.

Hint 1

Match each 5-digit group to its column in the table, one group at a time, left to right.

Hint 2

If you'd rather calculate it: each digit stands for a place value of 16, 8, 4, 2, then 1. Add up the place values where the digit is a 1.

💡 Did you know? A single plastic bottle can take about 450 years to fully break down in the ocean — nearly six times longer than anyone alive today has been alive.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Harbor Permit

The harbor office won't sign CC-4's mooring permit until they know its total footprint on the water: the V-shaped catch corridor out front (a triangle) plus the rectangular collection tank behind it. Grab a pencil — this one has two shapes to work through.

The dashed line is the triangle's height. The triangle's base and the rectangle's top edge are the same 12 m — they're the same seam where the two pieces join.

What is the total footprint area of CC-4, in square meters?

Hint 1

Split the shape into the two pieces you already know how to measure separately: a triangle and a rectangle.

Hint 2

Triangle area = ½ × base × height. Rectangle area = length × width. Add both areas together for the total.

MISSION 07
HARD

Which Sensor Do We Trust?

CC-4 has two current-speed sensors, one on each arm of the V: Sensor North and Sensor South. Exactly one of them is broken. A broken sensor's statements are always false. A working sensor's statements are always true.

The crew already knows one fact for certain, straight from the dock's own hand-checked instrument: today's current is NOT flowing at exactly 4 knots — it's flowing faster than that.

This morning, the two sensors reported:

Sensor North: "Sensor South is broken."
Sensor South: "The current is flowing at exactly 4 knots today."

Which sensor is actually broken?

Hint 1

Check one sensor's claim against the fact the crew already knows for certain — don't start with the claim about the other sensor.

Hint 2

If a sensor's own words don't match a fact you already know for certain, that sensor must be the broken one — a working sensor never says something false.

💡 Did you know? The middle of a big ocean current system (called a gyre) is calm and slow-moving. That's exactly why floating plastic gets trapped there for years instead of washing back to shore.

MISSION 08
HARD

Chart the Mooring Spot

Stage 1 of 2

The harbor pilot radios instructions in compass bearings, not plain words. She says the support buoy needs to be set on a bearing of "due Southeast" from the dock.

Search to find: how many degrees is the compass bearing for due Southeast?

Hint 1

Southeast sits exactly halfway between two of the four main compass points. Search for a compass bearing chart to find the exact number.

Stage 2 of 2

Here's the harbor chart. The dock (marked H) sits at grid square C2. On this chart, moving east means moving one square right, and moving south means moving one square down (south is toward the bottom, like on any map).

Using the bearing you found in Stage 1, sail exactly 3 diagonal steps from the dock along that heading. Each step moves one row and one column. Which grid square do you land on?

Hint 1

A bearing exactly halfway between East and South moves you diagonally — one column right and one row down for every step.

Hint 2

Starting from C2, take three of those diagonal steps. Each step advances the letter by one row and the number by one column.

MISSION 09
BIG AHA!

The Sponsor Wall

Three sponsors are getting a decal each on CC-4, and each decal goes in exactly one of three zones: the Left Arm, the Right Arm, or the Tank. No zone gets two decals, and no sponsor gets two zones. The contracts say:

1. BlueWave Bank's decal goes on the Left Arm or the Tank — never both, and never the Right Arm.
2. Coastal Foods Co.'s decal is on the Right Arm and is not on the Tank.
3. Nautix Batteries' decal is not on the Left Arm.

Which zone gets Nautix Batteries' decal?

Hint 1

Start with the clue that pins down an exact zone for one sponsor by itself. That's your first solid piece.

Hint 2

Once one sponsor's zone is settled, only two zones are left for the other two sponsors. One remaining clue rules out a zone for one of them — leaving just one place it can go.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Season Countdown

Launch Day has arrived — but the review board needs one last proof before they'll sign off. Together, Meridian Delta and the drift zone offshore still hold about 900,000 kilograms of plastic that needs clearing before the autumn storms roll in.

If CC-4 runs at its optimal collection rate every single day from here on, exactly how many full days will it take to clear all 900,000 kilograms?

Hint 1

You need two numbers: the kilograms still left to collect, and how fast CC-4 can collect on its very best days. One of those numbers was written down earlier in your notes, not here.

Hint 2

Divide the total kilograms left by CC-4's optimal daily rate. If it divides evenly, that clean number is your answer, in days.

Case closed?

Budgets, sensors, charts, contracts, and one stubborn diagram — no single job would have gotten CC-4 to the water on its own. It took every teammate's piece fitting with every other teammate's piece. That's teamwork, and it's exactly what got CC-4 launched on schedule.
