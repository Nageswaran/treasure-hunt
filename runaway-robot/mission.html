Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Case of the Runaway Robot

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 65-80 minutes

Sanne runs a small robotics workshop in Eindhoven, a Dutch city famous for its robot-builders and engineers. Her newest creation, a helper-robot named PIP-7, was halfway through a software update last night when something went wrong — and PIP-7 rolled straight out the workshop door into the dark streets.

All Sanne has left is a trail of error logs, sticky notes, and a battery counting down toward zero. She needs a sharp young detective to follow PIP-7's digital trail and bring it home before the battery dies for good.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Boot-Screen Tribute

Sanne restarts the workshop's main computer to check the last thing PIP-7 did before it vanished. Every time this computer boots up, it flashes a short tribute message honoring someone Sanne admires:

"In honor of the woman historians call the world's first computer programmer — who wrote a step-by-step algorithm for Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine in the 1840s, long before real computers even existed."

Search online to find out: what is this person's full name?

Hint 1

Try searching "first computer programmer Analytical Engine 1840s" and look for the name on a trustworthy history or museum site.

MISSION 02
EASY

PIP-7's Last Note

Taped to the workshop door is a sticky note in PIP-7's own handwriting-font, written half in Dutch, the way it always practiced:

"Verbinding verbroken met ___ netwerk. Ik ga zoeken." ("Connection lost with the network. I am going to search.")

Every Dutch noun needs the right little word in front of it — either "de" or "het". Search online to find out which article belongs with the Dutch word "netwerk" (network).

Type just the article: de or het.

Hint 1

Try searching "netwerk de of het" and check a Dutch grammar or dictionary site for the answer.

💡 Did you know? The word "robot" comes from an old Slavic word, "robota," meaning forced labor. Writer Karel Čapek first used it in his 1920 play about machines that rebel against their makers!

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Charging Dock Blueprint

PIP-7's charging dock is built into the corner of the workshop, in an L-shape, so the storage closet door doesn't bump into the charger. Sanne sketched the floor plan with every measurement marked.

Using the measurements on the sketch below, calculate the total floor area of the charging dock, in square meters.

Split the L-shape into two simple rectangles before you calculate — or think of it as one big rectangle with a corner missing.
Hint 1

Try drawing one straight line across the shape to turn it into two ordinary rectangles.

Hint 2

One way: find the area of the whole outer rectangle (10 m × 6 m) as if the corner wasn't missing, then subtract the missing notch (4 m × 2 m).

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The New Lock Code

Before it glitched, PIP-7 was choosing a new password to protect its charging station. Its system log shows four passwords it was considering:

Password A: PIP2024

Password B: 123456789

Password C: hunter2

Password D: Qm7!vLxT2#dRwZp9

Which ONE of these four passwords is the strongest? Think about length, whether it uses a real word or personal information, and how many different kinds of characters it mixes together.

Type your answer like this: "Password A", "Password B", "Password C", or "Password D".

Hint 1

Look at length first — a short password is easy to crack no matter what characters it uses.

Hint 2

A strong password avoids real words, avoids anything personal (like a name or a year), and mixes uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols unpredictably.

💡 Did you know? The very first computer mouse, built in 1964, had a carved wooden case and just one button!

MISSION 05
HARD

The Sensor Logs

The workshop has four rooms: the Workshop, the Server Closet, the Break Room, and the Garage. Each room has a different type of sensor: Motion, Light, Sound, and Door — one sensor type per room.

PIP-7 tripped exactly one sensor in each room on its way out, but the software glitch scrambled which sensor belongs to which room in the printout. Use these clues to work it out:

1. The Light sensor triggered in the Server Closet.

2. The Motion sensor did not trigger in the Server Closet or the Break Room.

3. The Door sensor did not trigger in the Workshop or the Garage.

4. The Sound sensor did not trigger in the Garage.

Which sensor triggered in the Garage — the room PIP-7 used to escape into the street?

Hint 1

Try making a table: the four rooms across the top, the four sensor types down the side. Cross off a box every time a clue rules it out.

Hint 2

Clue 1 gives you one exact match right away. Then use clue 3 to figure out where the Door sensor must be, since the Server Closet is already taken.

MISSION 06
HARD

Bug in the System

PIP-7's error log ends with a strange line: "ERROR: BUG DETECTED." Underneath it, someone has scribbled two competing explanations for where the word "bug" (meaning a computer glitch) actually came from. Only one of them is true — the other is a popular myth.

Claim A: In September 1947, engineers found a real moth stuck inside a relay of the Harvard Mark II computer, taped it into their logbook, and joked it was the "first actual case of bug being found."
Claim B: The word "bug" for a computer problem was invented by Bill Gates in the 1980s.

Search online to find out which claim is actually true. Type: Claim A or Claim B.

Hint 1

Try searching "first computer bug moth story" and see what a trusted computer-history site says really happened.

Hint 2

Look for a museum or computer-history website — does it credit Bill Gates, or someone else entirely, decades earlier?

💡 Did you know? A robot soccer team from Eindhoven, called Tech United, has been crowned world champion multiple times at the RoboCup competition — right in PIP-7's home city!

MISSION 07
BIG AHA

The Battery Safe

Behind a loose panel in the workshop wall, Sanne finds PIP-7's emergency spare battery locked inside a small safe. The keypad doesn't use number buttons — it uses PIP-7's favorite binary status lights instead.

Look closely at the panel below. A glowing (filled) light means 1, a dark (empty) light means 0. Reading left to right, add up the place values of only the glowing lights to find the code.

The number under each light is what it's worth if it's glowing. Add up only the glowing ones.
Hint 1

Binary place values double each time, starting from 1 on the right: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128. Add up the place values of only the glowing lights.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

PIP-7 Comes Home

Sanne finds PIP-7 powered down behind a recycling bin, two streets from the workshop, its battery at zero. Right before shutting off, it printed one last coded line — using the same shifting code its programmers taught it to hide secret messages: every letter is pushed forward through the alphabet by a fixed number of steps.

To read the message, shift each letter backward through the alphabet by the same number as the lights that were glowing in Mission 7's battery safe.

TIVWIZIVERGI

Hint 1

Go back and count how many lights were actually glowing in Mission 7's panel. That number is your shift.

Hint 2

For example, with a shift of 4: the letter E shifted back becomes A. Try that pattern on the first few letters and see what starts to appear.

Case closed?

Every glitch you traced — the boot screen, the sensor logs, the coded shutdown message — you never gave up, even when the trail went cold. That's real perseverance, the same quality every great troubleshooter and every great detective shares. Sanne reboots PIP-7 right there on the sidewalk, and its very first word is "thank you."
