Mission 01 · Easy · Technology & Computers / Internet Research

The Boot-Screen Tribute

Answer: Ada Lovelace
How to get there

Search "first computer programmer Analytical Engine 1840s". Ada Lovelace wrote notes (including "Note G") describing a step-by-step algorithm for Charles Babbage's proposed Analytical Engine to compute Bernoulli numbers — widely regarded as the first published computer program, making her the person historians call the first computer programmer.

Why this is the only answer

Only one historical figure is consistently credited with this specific achievement (a published algorithm for the Analytical Engine in the 1840s) across encyclopedic and museum sources. The mission text asks for her full name, so the app expects "Ada Lovelace" typed in full — "Lovelace" alone will not match the hash.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against Wikipedia ("Ada Lovelace"), the Computer History Museum, and NIST's biography page, all of which credit her as the first computer programmer for her algorithm in Note G.

Mission 03 · Medium · Mathematics (Geometry / Measurement)

The Charging Dock Blueprint

Answer: 52 (square meters)
How to get there

The floor plan is an L-shape: a 10 m × 6 m outer rectangle with a 4 m × 2 m notch cut from the top-right corner. Whole rectangle: 10 × 6 = 60 m². Subtract the missing notch: 4 × 2 = 8 m². Total area = 60 − 8 = 52 m².

Cross-check by splitting into two rectangles instead: a 6 m × 6 m left rectangle (36 m²) plus a 4 m × 4 m right rectangle (16 m²) = 52 m². Both methods agree.

Why this is the only answer

The six labeled side lengths (6 m, 2 m, 4 m, 4 m, 10 m, 6 m) are mutually consistent with the drawn polygon points (20,20 → 140,20 → 140,60 → 220,60 → 220,140 → 20,140 at a 1 m = 20 px scale), so there is exactly one shape and exactly one area that matches every labeled measurement.

Source / fact used

Original diagram authored for this mission; area recomputed by hand two independent ways (subtraction method and split-rectangle method) to confirm 52 m² both times.

The New Lock Code

How to get there

B. "123456789" — purely sequential digits; one of the most commonly used and fastest-cracked passwords in the world.

If a phrasing variant is typed (e.g. just "D" or "Password d"), it must match "password d" exactly after lowercasing — note that in the app for the parent if the child gets an unexpected "not quite."

Mission 06 · Hard · Critical Thinking & Media Literacy / Internet Research

Bug in the System

Answer: Claim A
How to get there

Search "first computer bug moth story" or "Harvard Mark II moth 1947." On September 9, 1947, engineers working on the Harvard Mark II found a moth wedged in Relay #70 (Panel F), taped it into the operations logbook, and wrote "First actual case of bug being found." That logbook page is preserved at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History.

Claim B is false: engineers had already been using "bug" as slang for equipment faults for decades before 1947 (and long before Bill Gates, born 1955, was old enough to coin anything in the 1980s) — the term did not originate with Gates.

Why this is the only answer

Claim A is directly documented by a preserved physical artifact (the logbook page with the actual moth) held at the Smithsonian, described consistently by the Computer History Museum and the Smithsonian itself. Claim B has no supporting historical evidence and is contradicted by the fact that "bug" was already standard engineering slang decades earlier.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against the Computer History Museum's "This Day in History" entry and the Smithsonian National Museum of American History's catalog record for the logbook page.

Grand Finale · Logical Reasoning / Cryptography (Caesar Shift)

PIP-7 Comes Home

Answer: perseverance
How to get there

The shift number is 4 — the count of glowing lights in Mission 7's panel (32, 8, 4, and 1 were lit — four lights total). The coded message "TIVWIZIVERGI" was created by shifting the word PERSEVERANCE forward by 4 letters (P→T, E→I, R→V, S→W, E→I, V→Z, E→I, R→V, A→E, N→R, C→G, E→I).

Shifting each coded letter backward by 4 reverses this: T→P, I→E, V→R, W→S, I→E, Z→V, I→E, V→R, E→A, R→N, G→C, I→E, spelling out P-E-R-S-E-V-E-R-A-N-C-E.

Why this is the only answer

The shift amount (4) is uniquely determined by Mission 7's diagram, and a Caesar shift is fully reversible with the correct shift — decoding "TIVWIZIVERGI" backward by exactly 4 produces exactly one 12-letter English word, "PERSEVERANCE," with no other shift amount producing a real word from this ciphertext.

Source / fact used

Original cipher authored for this mission; encoding and decoding both verified letter-by-letter by hand.
