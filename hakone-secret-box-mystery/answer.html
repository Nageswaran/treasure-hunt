The Workshop Floor Pattern

The tiles repeat in a strict 3-tile cycle: square, triangle, diamond. Position 1=square, 2=triangle, 3=diamond, 4=square, 5=triangle, 6=diamond, 7=square, 8=triangle. Tile 9 is the 3rd tile in the 3rd repeat of the cycle (9 ÷ 3 = 3 exactly), so it must be diamond.

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle states the cycle is strict and unbreaking, so position 9 is forced by the pattern alone — no outside information is needed or used.

Source / fact used

Self-contained logic puzzle; no external source. Solved by hand: 9 mod 3 = 0, which maps to the 3rd (diamond) position in the cycle.

Mission 03 · Medium · History + Geography + Internet Research

The Master's History Lesson

Answer: Netherlands
How to get there

Searching "Dejima island Nagasaki only European country allowed to trade with Japan" leads to the history of Dejima, a fan-shaped artificial island built in Nagasaki harbor. From 1641 to 1854, the Dutch (Netherlands) were the only Western nation permitted to trade with Japan, operating out of a trading post on Dejima, during Japan's sakoku ("closed country") period.

Why this is the only answer

Every reputable source (encyclopedic and historical) agrees the Dutch/Netherlands held sole Western trading rights at Dejima for this period — Portugal was expelled in the 1630s, and other Western powers were barred entirely, leaving only the Netherlands. The mission expects the child to type the country name ("Netherlands"), not the nationality adjective ("Dutch") — this is spelled out in the puzzle text and Hint 1 to avoid ambiguity.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against multiple sources describing Dejima's history (Artelino, Japan Experience, Japanistry). The Dutch trading post operated on Dejima from 1641 until Japan's ports reopened in the 1850s (Convention of Kanagawa, 1854) — a span of roughly 213 years, commonly rounded to "more than two centuries."

Mission 04 · Medium · Mathematics (Geometry / Measurement — box net)

Measuring the Display Case

Answer: 432 (cm²)
How to get there

The box is 12 cm x 8 cm x 6 cm. The six faces are: Front & Back = 12 x 6 = 72 cm² each (144 total); Left & Right = 8 x 6 = 48 cm² each (96 total); Top & Bottom = 12 x 8 = 96 cm² each (192 total). Total surface area = 144 + 96 + 192 = 432 cm². (Equivalently: 2 x (12x8 + 12x6 + 8x6) = 2 x (96+72+48) = 2 x 216 = 432.)

Why this is the only answer

All three needed dimensions (12 cm, 8 cm, 6 cm) are explicitly labeled on the diagram, and the puzzle text states opposite faces are identical — the calculation is fully determined, no estimation needed.

Source / fact used

Self-authored diagram, verified for internal consistency: the net is a valid "cross with tail" cuboid net (Left-Front-Right-Back in a row, Top above Front, Bottom below Front), with every shared edge between adjacent faces matching in length (checked by hand against the 12/8/6 dimensions) — it folds into a closed box.

Mission 05 · Medium · Dutch Language (de/het)

Goods Off the Dejima Boats

Answer: specerij
How to get there

Standard Dutch grammar: raw materials and metals overwhelmingly take "het" — het hout (wood), het koper (copper), het zout (salt), het zilver (silver). "Specerij" (spice) is not a raw material/metal noun and takes "de": de specerij.

Why this is the only answer

Of the five words given, four are "het" words (hout, koper, zout, zilver) and only specerij is a "de" word — it is the single odd one out by the stated 4-vs-1 split.

Source / fact used

Standard Dutch de/het vocabulary (native-speaker grammar knowledge): het hout, het koper, het zout, het zilver, de specerij. These are common, unambiguous Dutch nouns, not a matter of regional variation.

Mission 07 · Hard · Logical Reasoning (Intermediate — knights and knaves)

Who Stayed Late?

Answer: Ren
How to get there

Let S = who stayed late. Aiko's statement ("Ren stayed late") is true iff S=Ren. Ren's statement ("I did not stay late") is true iff S≠Ren. Yuki's statement ("Aiko is lying") is true iff Aiko's statement is false, i.e. iff S≠Ren — so Ren's and Yuki's statements are always true or false together. Since exactly one of the three apprentices always tells the truth, exactly one of the three statements must be true. Testing S=Ren: Aiko's statement is true, Ren's and Yuki's are both false — exactly 1 true statement. ✓ Testing S=Aiko or S=Yuki: Aiko's statement is false, but Ren's and Yuki's are both true — 2 true statements, which contradicts "exactly one truth-teller." So S=Ren is the only consistent solution.

Why this is the only answer

Solved from only the three statements plus the "exactly one truth-teller" constraint, with no guessing: only S=Ren produces exactly one true statement among the three, satisfying the puzzle's stated premise. The other two candidates both fail that premise (they'd require two truth-tellers).

Source / fact used

Self-contained logic puzzle (knights-and-knaves mechanic); worked by hand through all three cases (S=Aiko, S=Ren, S=Yuki) to confirm a unique forced solution.

Mission 09 · Big "Aha!" · Logical Reasoning (Advanced — working backward / backward induction)

Reading the Master's Notebook

Answer: left right front lid
How to get there

Work backward from the fully-open goal state. Last move: Rule A is the only rule whose condition depends on all three other parts already being open — so the Lid must be the last (4th) move. First move: Rule C requires Front, Right, and Lid to all still be in their original untouched positions — true only before any move has happened at all — so the Left panel must be the very first (1st) move, and only the Left panel's rule has this property. Middle moves: Rule D requires both Left and Right already open before Front can move, so Front cannot be 2nd (Right would need to already be open, but Right's own rule (B) requires Left already open first, and Left is fixed as move 1, so Right cannot also be move 1). That forces Right = 2nd move, then Front = 3rd move (now that Left and Right are both open, satisfying Rule D), then Lid = 4th. Full forced sequence: Left, Right, Front, Lid.

Why this is the only answer

Verified by hand: Rule C pins Left uniquely to move 1 (the only moment all three other parts are untouched); Rule A pins Lid uniquely to move 4 (the only moment all three other parts are already open); Rule B then forces Right before Front in the remaining two slots, since Right's rule requires Left already open (satisfied at move 2 but not move 1) while Front's rule (D) requires both Left AND Right already open, which is only true at move 3. No branch or alternative ordering satisfies all four rules simultaneously.

Source / fact used

Self-authored puzzle mechanism; solved and double-checked by hand against all four rules for each of the 24 possible orderings' worth of constraints (via the elimination argument above) to confirm a single forced solution.
