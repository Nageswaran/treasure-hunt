Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Cartographer's Lost Map

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 55-70 minutes
A Dutch maritime museum is about to auction off a battered 17th-century sea chart linked to the explorer Abel Tasman. But the museum's old conservator — before she retired — quietly hid the chart's final torn corner as a private puzzle trail, believing only a truly curious detective should ever piece it back together. The auction is tomorrow morning. You have until then to follow her trail and find the missing corner.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Explorer's Year

A plaque beside the chart names the Dutch explorer Abel Tasman, but the year has worn away. Search online: in what year did Abel Tasman become the first European known to reach both Tasmania and New Zealand? Type just the year.

Hint 1

Try searching "Abel Tasman Tasmania New Zealand year discovered" and check it against more than one trustworthy history site.

💡 Did you know? Tasman's two ships were called the Heemskerck and the Zeehaen. The Zeehaen was a "fluyt" — a Dutch cargo ship built to carry lots of goods with only a small crew.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Torn Compass Rose

The chart's compass rose is torn, but North is still clearly marked at the top. Beside it, a captain's note reads: "We sailed from the cove in the direction opposite of Southwest." Which compass direction did they actually sail?

Hint 1

Picture a compass: N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW, all the way around in a circle. The "opposite" direction is exactly halfway around from Southwest.

🗒️ Agent's Notebook: Squeezed into the corner of the torn compass rose is a faint scrawled number: 36. You don't know what it means yet, but it feels like something worth remembering.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Reading the Chart's Scale

This part of the chart still has its scale marked: each grid square stands for 40 nautical miles. Count the squares in a straight line from the Cove to Tasman's ship, then work out the real distance.

Count only whole squares between the two dots — they sit on the same row.
Hint 1

Count the squares between the Cove dot and the Ship dot, moving straight across.

Hint 2

There are 6 squares between them. Multiply that by 40 nautical miles.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Conservator's Note

Tucked behind the chart's frame is a note in a simple code: every letter has been shifted forward by 3 places in the alphabet. Shift each letter of this message BACK by 3 to read the real word:

EHDFRQ

Hint 1

Write the alphabet out. To undo a "shift forward by 3," move each letter 3 places earlier (back) instead.

Hint 2

The first letter, E, shifted back 3 is B. The word describes a signal fire sailors watched for along the coast.

💡 Did you know? The Dutch East India Company (VOC), founded in 1602, is often called the world's first true multinational company — it was also one of the first companies ever to sell shares of stock to ordinary investors.

MISSION 05
HARD

The Shape of the Island

Grab a pencil for this one — it takes two separate calculations added together. The chart shows an island shaped like a rectangle with a triangular point jutting into the sea. Using the marked measurements, find the island's total area in square kilometers.

The dashed line down the middle is the rectangle's own right edge — it's also the triangle's base.
Hint 1

Split the island into a rectangle and a triangle at the dashed line.

Hint 2

Rectangle area = 12 km × 8 km. Triangle area = ½ × base × height, using the same 8 km edge as the base and the 5 km point as the height. Add both areas together.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Map Room Drawers

The museum's map room has four drawers — A, B, C, and D. Each holds a chart of a different place: Tasmania, New Zealand, Java, and Mauritius — one chart per drawer. Use these clues to work out which drawer holds which chart:

1. Drawer D holds the New Zealand chart.

2. The Tasmania chart is not in Drawer A, and not in Drawer C.

3. The Java chart is in a drawer that comes alphabetically before the Mauritius chart's drawer.

4. The Mauritius chart is not in Drawer B.

Which drawer holds the Tasmania chart? Type your answer like "Drawer A", "Drawer B", "Drawer C", or "Drawer D".

Hint 1

Clue 1 gives you Drawer D right away. Cross that whole drawer off your other guesses.

Hint 2

Clue 2 leaves only one drawer free for Tasmania once Drawer D is already taken. Clues 3 and 4 then settle Java and Mauritius between the two remaining drawers.

MISSION 07
BIG AHA

The Depth Mark

Stage 1 of 2

Recall the faint number scrawled in the corner of the torn compass rose, back in Mission 2. Subtract it from the real distance you worked out between the Cove and the ship in Mission 3. What number do you get?

Hint 1

Your Mission 2 notebook had a number in it. Your Mission 3 answer was a distance in nautical miles. Subtract the smaller from the larger.

Stage 2 of 2

That number is a faint depth mark on the chart, right where an old X was inked over a cabinet number in the museum's back room. Divide it by 4, then subtract 3. Which cabinet number does the X mark?

Hint 1

Take your Stage 1 answer, divide it by 4 first, then subtract 3 from what's left.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Chart's Last Corner

Inside Cabinet 48, wrapped in oilcloth, is the chart's missing corner — and one last note from the conservator. Every letter in it has been swapped for its mirror opposite in the alphabet (A becomes Z, B becomes Y, C becomes X, and so on, all the way to the middle). Swap each letter back to read her final word:

XLFIZTV

Hint 1

Line up the alphabet forwards and backwards side by side: A pairs with Z, B pairs with Y, C pairs with X... keep going until every letter has a partner.

Hint 2

The first letter, X, pairs with C. The whole word is something every explorer needs before setting sail into the unknown.

Case closed?

The conservator never wanted the chart's corner found by just anyone — only by someone willing to sail into the unknown, one puzzle at a time, without knowing what came next. That's exactly what Abel Tasman needed to cross uncharted seas 400 years ago, and it's exactly what you needed tonight: COURAGE. The chart goes back on display whole, corner and all.
