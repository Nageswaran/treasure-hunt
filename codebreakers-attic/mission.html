Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Case of the Locked Attic Trunk

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60-75 minutes
Your great-aunt Elsie kept one big secret her whole life: during World War II, she worked as a codebreaker at a secret countryside estate in England, helping crack enemy messages. Now her old steamer trunk has turned up in the attic — locked tight, and covered in strange notes. The family is clearing the attic for an estate sale this weekend, so you have until then to follow Elsie's trail and discover what she hid inside, one clue at a time.
MISSION 01
EASY

Elsie's Postcard

Taped inside the trunk's lid is an old postcard. On the back, in Elsie's handwriting: "Worked here, 1943. Told everyone it was just a boring old country house. It wasn't."

Search online to find the name of the real English countryside estate where WWII codebreakers secretly worked cracking enemy messages. What was it called?

Hint 1

Try searching for "WWII secret codebreakers England country estate." One name comes up again and again.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Codebreaker's Word List

Under the postcard is a list of Dutch words Elsie must have used to practice, each with a blank where the little word "de" or "het" should go: geheim (secret), wachtwoord (password), bericht (message), and sleutel (key).

Three of these words take "het." One of them is the odd one out and takes "de" instead. Which word is the odd one out?

📓 Agent's Notebook: Tucked between the pages is a torn photo of a cat. On the back, Elsie wrote: "Vier keeps my secrets." Worth remembering!

Hint 1

Try saying "het" in front of each word out loud — three of them will sound right straight away.

💡 Did you know? The Enigma machine, used to scramble WWII enemy messages, looked a lot like a typewriter — except instead of printing letters onto paper, it lit up one of 26 little lamps to show the scrambled letter.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Locker Ticket

A cloakroom ticket falls out of the trunk. Instead of a normal number, Elsie wrote the locker number in binary — the code of 1s and 0s that computers use: 101101.

Each position in a binary number stands for a power of 2, and you read it from right to left: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Add up the positions that have a 1 in them. What ordinary number is locker 101101?

Hint 1

Write the six place values above the digits, left to right: 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Then only add up the ones sitting under a "1".

Hint 2

The digits are 1-0-1-1-0-1, sitting under 32-16-8-4-2-1. That means you add 32 + 8 + 4 + 1.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Mathematician's Machine

Inside locker 45 is a photo of a serious-looking young man next to a wardrobe-sized machine full of wheels and wires. A caption underneath reads: "He designed the machine that finally beat the enemy's fastest code — years before computers like ours existed."

Search online for the mathematician who designed this code-cracking machine at the same estate you found in Mission 1, and find the machine's name. What was it called?

Hint 1

The mathematician's name is Alan Turing. Search for "Alan Turing machine that cracked Enigma."

Hint 2

The answer is a short, ordinary English word — not a person's name. Type just that one word.

💡 Did you know? By 1944, almost 9,000 people worked at that secret English estate cracking codes around the clock — and about three out of every four of them were women.

MISSION 05
HARD

Three Clerks, Three Huts

A staff list names three codebreakers — Fox, Hart, and Wren — each working in a different hut (Hut 3, Hut 6, or Hut 8) on a different kind of enemy code (Army, Navy, or Air Force). Water damage smudged most of the sheet, but four lines are still readable:

1. The clerk in Hut 6 worked on the Army code.

2. Fox did not work in Hut 8.

3. Wren worked on the Navy code.

4. Hart worked in Hut 3.

Work out who worked where, on what — then tell me which code Hart worked on.

Hint 1

Start with clue 4 — it pins one clerk to one hut right away. Then use clue 2 to place Fox.

Hint 2

Once you know Fox is in Hut 6, clue 1 tells you Fox's code directly. Wren's code is given in clue 3. Whatever's left over belongs to Hart.

MISSION 06
HARD

Elsie's Own Cipher

Stage 1 of 2

Folded under the staff list is a strip of paper in Elsie's own handwriting: ORRVH IORRUERDUG. A note beside it says: "Shift every letter back 3 places in the alphabet." (So a shifted "D" would really be an "A".)

Hint 1

Go letter by letter. For "O," count back 3 places: O, N, M, L — so "O" decodes to "L."

Stage 2 of 2

You did it — there's a second, shorter strip hidden in the spot Stage 1 pointed you to: XVYRO OIC. This time Elsie left no shift number. Instead, her note says: "Use the number only my cat would know."

Hint 1

Think back to an earlier page in this case file — did any of the Dutch words you sorted have anything to do with a number?

Hint 2

"Vier" is Dutch for a number. Use that number as your shift, the same way you did in Stage 1.

💡 Did you know? Some historians believe that cracking enemy codes at that secret English estate helped end World War II about two years sooner than it otherwise would have.

MISSION 07
BIG AHA

The Rotor Puzzle

Inside the trunk key's hiding spot is one last note: "The enemy's Enigma machine held 3 rotors at a time, slotted in side by side — and the order you put them in changed the whole code. Search to find out: how many different rotors could an operator choose from in total?" (Grab a pencil for this one.)

Once you know that number, work out how many different ways 3 rotors could be chosen from that set and arranged in order in the machine. Remember: once a rotor is used in one slot, it can't be used again in another slot.

Hint 1

First figure out how many choices you have for the very first slot. Then figure out how many rotors are left for the second slot, and the third.

Hint 2

Multiply your three numbers of choices together: (choices for slot 1) × (choices for slot 2) × (choices for slot 3).

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

What Elsie Left You

The trunk key finally turns. Inside is a small wooden box and one last note in Elsie's handwriting:

"It took us years — trying, failing, and trying again — to crack a code everyone said was impossible. We didn't give up, even when it felt hopeless. What's the one word for never giving up, no matter how many times you fail?"

Hint 1

Think of a word that describes someone who keeps trying after failing again and again, instead of quitting.

Hint 2

It starts with "per" and ends the same way "endurance" and "patience" both feel.

Case closed?

Inside the wooden box is Elsie's own medal and a note: "Codebreaking wasn't magic — it was perseverance, one wrong guess at a time." You cracked her whole trail. Well done, Agent.
