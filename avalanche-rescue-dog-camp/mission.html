Internet Detective Academy · Case File

Avalanche Rescue Dog Camp

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes (across all 10 missions)

High in the Swiss Alps, the Rothorn Alpine Rescue Dog Camp is one night away from demonstrating its avalanche search dogs for a group of visiting government officials. But head trainer Elin Baumann just discovered two problems: her training logbook, with tomorrow's whole running order, has vanished from her desk — and Nera, one of the four demonstration dogs, is missing her tracking tag, which must have come loose sometime during evening training.

Nobody's in trouble. Nobody's a villain. Something ordinary happened, and Elin needs a sharp pair of eyes to retrace the evening before the officials arrive at dawn. That's you. Grab a notebook — real handlers always do.

MISSION 01
EASY

Three Staff, One Muddy Footprint

Elin's logbook was on her desk in the office cabin yesterday evening. It isn't anymore. You start with the easiest question a detective always asks first: who was actually in that cabin?

The cabin floor shows one fresh trail of muddy footprints — dry, crumbly dirt, not wet snow-slush. Here's what you know from the duty roster:

1. Senior handler Marta was out on the practice slope from 4 PM until dinner, wearing snow boots the whole time.
2. Vet Pieter spent that same stretch in the kennel building doing health checks — its floor is bare concrete, and the roster confirms he never left.
3. Rookie volunteer Jonas drove back from a supply run to the village around 5 PM. The village road was muddy from that morning's rain, and he went straight to the cabin to drop off new lead ropes before dinner.

Only one of these three could possibly have left that muddy trail. Who was it?

Hint 1

Match each person's boots and location at that hour to what the cabin floor actually shows — mud, not snow-slush, and bare concrete leaves no mud at all.

MISSION 02
EASY

Barry, the Dog Who Started It All

While you wait for Jonas to come back from evening rounds, you notice an old framed print on the common-room wall: a huge dog wearing a small barrel on its collar, standing over a traveler in the snow. The caption reads "Barry, hero of the Great St Bernard Pass."

Marta glances over. "Everyone assumes that barrel part is true," she says. "It isn't. Go look up the real history — you'll be surprised."

Search online for the true story of Barry the rescue dog and the monks of the Great St Bernard Hospice. Fill in the blank: according to real historical records, Barry never actually carried a barrel of ___ around his neck.

Hint 1

Search "Barry St Bernard dog true story" and look for what the hospice's own records say, not just the famous old painting everyone remembers.

🕵️ Agent's Notebook — worth remembering

Elin taps the radio clipped to her jacket. "Tomorrow's demonstration runs on Channel 5. Every handler needs to know that number by heart — so should you." Jot it down. You won't need it right away.

💡 Did you know? Switzerland's rescue-dog association, REDOG, was founded in 1971 — and just five years later, in 1976, its dog teams helped find 16 survivors buried after a major earthquake in Italy's Friuli region.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Shape of the Slide

Grab a pencil for this one. This afternoon's search-training zone was marked out with flags on the practice slope, in the L-shaped patch shown below. Every corner is a right angle. Elin wants today's zone size written down for tomorrow's records, but with the logbook missing, you'll have to work it out yourself.

Find the total area of the marked zone, in square meters.

Every measurement you need is labeled right on the flags — no guessing required.
Hint 1

Try splitting the L-shape into two rectangles — or start from the full outer rectangle and subtract the missing corner.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

A Dutch Family's Word Card

A Dutch family visited the camp last week and left behind a laminated card their kids used to practice camp words. Five words are listed, each needing "de" or "het" in front of it in Dutch:

hond · berg · lawine · sneeuw · touw

Four of these five words all take the same article. One doesn't belong — it takes the other article. Which ONE word is the odd one out?

Hint 1

Four of the five words use "de." Test each word out loud with both articles and listen for the one that sounds wrong with "de."

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

How Long Will the Demonstration Take?

Here's something real Elin explained earlier tonight, worth knowing: a trained avalanche rescue dog can search about one hectare of snow (10,000 square meters) by scent in roughly 30 minutes — while a line of 20 human searchers using probes would take about 4 hours to search that same ground.

Tomorrow's demonstration search area is a rectangle 150 m by 100 m. Using the rate above, about how many minutes should the dogs' demonstration search take? (Remember: 1 hectare = 10,000 square meters.)

Hint 1

First find the area of the rectangle in square meters, then work out how many hectares that is — then scale the 30-minutes-per-hectare rate to match.

One more thing pinned above the office desk: a faded thank-you certificate. The camp once lent its dogs to a real Swiss institute for a winter snow study. Elin can't remember the exact name. "Something about snow and avalanches, up near Davos," she says, "but everyone just calls it by its short letters."

Search online to find this real Swiss institute — it's one of the world's leading centers for snow and avalanche research, with a famous measuring station high on a mountain called Weissfluhjoch. What is its short, 3-letter abbreviation?

Hint 1

Try searching "Switzerland snow avalanche research institute Davos."

💡 Did you know? A dog's nose has up to 300 million scent receptors, compared to only about 6 million in a human nose — that's part of why dogs, not machines, are still the fastest way to find someone buried in snow.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Whistle Code From Base

That evening, a short message crackles over the camp radio. It's spoken entirely in the phonetic alphabet that pilots and rescue teams use to spell out letters clearly over a crackly signal:

"X-ray. Mike. Juliett. India."

Search online for that phonetic alphabet, and turn each of those four words into a single letter. Then remember the channel number you noted earlier tonight — shift each of your four letters that many places backward through the alphabet (A, B, C...) to reveal a real four-letter word.

Hint 1

Search "NATO phonetic alphabet" — every word on that list always stands for exactly one letter, in every language.

Hint 2

Once you have four letters, shift each one backward by the channel number from your notebook. For example, shifting the letter "F" backward by 2 places gives "D."

MISSION 07
HARD

Four Handlers, Four Dogs, One Kennel Card

You now know something is happening near the shed. Before you head there, Elin wants Nera's actual handler warned in person — but the kennel cards are as scattered as everything else tonight. Four handlers (Anke, Bruno, Celine, and Felix) each look after one of four demonstration dogs (Nera the Border Collie, Ari the German Shepherd, Momo the Belgian Malinois, and Fara the Golden Retriever), each in their own numbered kennel (1, 2, 3, or 4). Work it out from these five clues:

1. The handler in Kennel 1 trains the Border Collie.
2. Bruno's kennel number is exactly three more than Celine's kennel number.
3. Anke does not train the Golden Retriever or the Malinois.
4. Anke's kennel number is even.
5. The Malinois's kennel number is odd.

Which handler will you need to warn about Nera's missing tracking tag?

Hint 1

Start with clue 2 — with only kennels 1 to 4 to work with, there's only one pair of numbers that fits "three more than."

Hint 2

Once all four kennel numbers are pinned down, clues 1, 3, 4, and 5 each force exactly one dog onto one handler.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Budget That Saves the Demo

Before the officials arrive, the equipment shed still needs restocking. There's 150 CHF left in the gear budget — but 15 CHF of that must stay untouched, because Marta already promised to buy a replacement whistle with it.

Camp rule: any new gear kit must include at least 3 collapsible probes AND at least 8 marker flags. Each probe costs 24 CHF. Each flag costs 3 CHF. Three suppliers offer ready-made kits:

Mountain Set: 3 probes + 8 flags
Value Pack: 2 probes + 12 flags
Pro Bundle: 5 probes + 10 flags

Only one of these three kits meets the camp rule AND fits inside the real spending limit. Which kit should the shed buy?

Hint 1

Check each kit against the camp rule FIRST — if it fails the minimum probes or flags, its price doesn't matter at all.

Hint 2

Remember: 15 CHF must stay untouched, so the real spending limit isn't the full 150 CHF.

💡 Did you know? If someone is completely buried in an avalanche, their chances of survival are still above 90% if they're dug out within about the first 10 to 15 minutes — after that, the odds drop fast. That's exactly why a fast search dog can matter more than almost anything else on the mountain.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

The Bearing to the Shed

Stage 1 of 2

Grab your pencil again. Elin's assistant remembers roughly how Jonas walked earlier: starting at the camp flagpole, he walked 40 paces heading exactly East, then turned and walked 30 paces heading exactly North, arriving at the shed.

Using the Pythagorean theorem (a real formula: a² + b² = c²), what is the straight-line distance from the flagpole to the shed, in paces?

Hint 1

This is the Pythagorean theorem — a² + b² = c². The paces East and the paces North are the two shorter sides of a right triangle; the straight-line distance is the longest side.

Stage 2 of 2

You reach the shed — its door has a wheel lock with a 2-digit code. Two trainees, Sofie and Milo, are standing nearby. One of them always tells the truth. The other always lies.

Sofie says: "Exactly one of us is telling the truth right now. Also, the real code is your Stage 1 distance minus Nera's kennel number."
Milo says: "I'm telling the truth, and so is Sofie."

Work out who is lying, then use the truth-teller's rule — with your Stage 1 answer and the kennel number you already worked out for Nera — to find the code.

Hint 1

Try assuming Milo is the truth-teller and see what that would force to be true about Sofie — does it match what "exactly one of us" would mean?

Hint 2

If assuming Milo is truthful leads to a contradiction, he must be the liar — which tells you Sofie's whole statement, including her rule for the code, is true.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

What Made Tonight Work

The wheel lock clicks open. Inside a storage crate: Elin's logbook, and Nera's tracking tag, tangled in a coil of spare leashes. Jonas comes jogging up, red-faced — he'd been quietly tidying the shed all evening so it would look sharp for the officials, and swept both items into the crate without ever checking what was inside it.

Nobody's angry. Elin laughs with relief and calls everyone over — you, Jonas, Marta, Pieter, Celine, Sofie, and Milo — to reset for tomorrow. She looks around at all of them and says only one word could really describe how a scattered pile of clues turned into a solved case by morning, with no single person ever holding the whole picture alone.

Type that word. It's also the exact reason a real rescue dog is never sent onto a slope by itself.

Hint 1

Think about how many different people's small pieces you had to combine tonight — the footprint, the history search, the shape of the slope, the radio code, the kennel card, the budget, the bearing, the truth-teller. No one of them solved the case alone.

Hint 2

It's the same reason a search dog always works roped to a handler, and a handler always works as part of a team — never solo, on a real mountain.

Case closed?

Jonas's honest mistake, Sofie and Milo's sharp eyes, Celine's steady hands with Nera, and your own detective work all needed each other tonight — exactly like a rescue dog and handler need each other on a real mountain. The value that cracked this whole case is teamwork. At dawn, the officials watch four dogs and four handlers work as one. Case closed, Agent.
