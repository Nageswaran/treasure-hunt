Mission 02 · Easy · History + Internet Research

Barry, the Dog Who Started It All

Answer: brandy
How to get there

Barry (1800-1814) was a real rescue dog at the Great St Bernard Hospice in the Swiss Alps, credited with saving dozens of travelers. The famous image of a St Bernard wearing a small brandy barrel on its collar comes from an 1820 painting by the artist Sir Edwin Landseer — it was never confirmed by the monks who actually ran the hospice, and giving alcohol to someone with hypothermia would in fact be dangerous. Search results for "Barry St Bernard true story" consistently explain that the barrel-of-brandy detail is a 19th-century artistic invention, not a historical fact.

Why this is the only answer

The question asks specifically what the real records say Barry never carried — every reputable source traces the myth to the same single 1820 painting and confirms real hospice dogs of that era were never documented carrying alcohol barrels.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search across multiple sources on Barry's real history and the origin of the barrel myth (the 1820 Edwin Landseer painting), cross-checked against historical accounts of the Great St Bernard Hospice dogs' actual rescue methods (barking for help, leading rescuers back, providing body warmth).

Mission 03 · Medium · Mathematics (geometry/measurement diagram, pen-and-paper)

The Shape of the Slide

Answer: 222 (square meters)
How to get there

Split the L-shape into two rectangles. One way: a big rectangle 18 m (bottom) × 14 m (left side) = 252 m², minus the missing notch of 6 m × 5 m = 30 m², taken out of the top-right corner. 252 - 30 = 222 m². (Equivalently: a 12 m × 14 m left rectangle = 168 m², plus a 6 m × 9 m right rectangle = 54 m²; 168 + 54 = 222 m².)

Why this is the only answer

The diagram's labeled dimensions are self-consistent (top 12 m + notch 6 m = bottom 18 m; notch 5 m + lower-right 9 m = left side 14 m), so there is exactly one correct total area regardless of which way the shape is split.

The Bearing to the Shed

Stage 1 answer: 50 (paces)
Stage 2 (final) answer: 49
How to get there

Stage 1: The walk (40 paces East, then 30 paces North) forms a right triangle with legs 40 and 30. By the Pythagorean theorem, distance² = 40² + 30² = 1600 + 900 = 2500, so distance = √2500 = 50 paces.

Stage 2: Assume Milo is the truth-teller. His statement ("I'm telling the truth, and so is Sofie") would then have to be entirely true, meaning Sofie is also a truth-teller — but the setup says exactly one of them always tells the truth, so having both be truth-tellers is a contradiction. Therefore Milo must be the liar, and Sofie is the truth-teller. Since Sofie is truthful, both parts of her statement are true: "exactly one of us is telling the truth" (checks out: Sofie true, Milo false) and "the code is the Stage 1 distance minus Nera's kennel number." Nera's kennel number is 1 (from Mission 7's solved grid: Celine, Kennel 1, Nera). Code = 50 - 1 = 49.

Why this is the only answer

Stage 1 is a direct, single-answer application of a fixed formula. Stage 2's contradiction (assuming Milo truthful breaks the "exactly one truth-teller" rule) forces Sofie to be the sole truth-teller with no alternative branch, and her rule then produces one specific number given Stage 1's result and Mission 7's kennel number — genuinely dependent on both earlier answers, re-solved independently to confirm.
