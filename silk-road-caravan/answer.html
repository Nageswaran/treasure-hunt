Mission 01 · Easy · Geography & History / Internet Research

The City Where the Road Begins

Answer: Xi'an (typed as "xian")
How to get there

Search "terracotta army city China." Every result names Xi'an (in Shaanxi province) as the city where the Terracotta Army was found, and as the traditional eastern starting point of the Silk Road (the city was called Chang'an in ancient times).

Why this is the only answer

Xi'an/Chang'an is the standard, universally cited starting city for the Silk Road across encyclopedic and educational sources — it's not a matter of local opinion or a changeable ranking. The mission text tells the child to type it as one plain word ("xian") to sidestep the apostrophe.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: Britannica's Silk Road overview and multiple China-travel/history sources agree the road "originated from the historical capital of Chang'an (now Xi'an)." Terracotta Army: discovered 1974 by farmers digging a well, roughly 8,000 statues (Guinness World Records, Discover Magazine, Artnet News).

Loading the Caravan

Answer: 47
How to get there

Silk camels: 72 bales ÷ 2 bales per camel = 36 camels. Spice camels: 33 sacks ÷ 3 sacks per camel = 11 camels. Total: 36 + 11 = 47 camels.

Why this is the only answer

Both divisions come out even (no leftover camels to argue about), and the "never a mix" rule stops the child from combining silk and spice on one camel, so there's exactly one total.

Mission 04 · Medium · Dutch Language (de/het)

Grandpa's Dutch Footnote

Answer: papier
How to get there

De zijde, de koffie, de thee all take "de." Papier takes "het" ("het papier") — it's the odd one out.

Why this is the only answer

Dutch nouns are fixed as "de" or "het" words (not a matter of preference), and three of the four listed words are confirmed "de" words, leaving exactly one "het" word.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against welklidwoord.nl and woorden.org: de zijde, de koffie, de thee, het papier all confirmed individually.

Mission 05 · Medium · Mathematics (geometry/measurement — self-drawn diagram)

The Caravanserai Courtyard

Answer: 50 (meters)
How to get there

Add all six labeled sides going around the L-shape once: 9 + 4 + 6 + 6 + 15 + 10 = 50 meters.

Why this is the only answer

The diagram is built to these exact numbers: a 15m × 10m rectangle with a 6m × 4m notch cut from the top-right corner. The remaining top edge is 15−6=9m and the remaining right edge above the notch is 10−4=6m, which is exactly what's labeled — so the six labeled sides are consistent with each other and sum to a single forced perimeter. (For reference, the enclosed area is 15×10 − 6×4 = 150 − 24 = 126 m², though the mission only asks for perimeter.)

Mission 09 · Hard · Big "Aha!" · Logical Reasoning (Advanced — working backward)

The Gatekeeper's Riddle

Answer: 110
How to get there

Work backward from the end. The rule per gate is: coins_after = coins_before ÷ 2 − 1, so reversed: coins_before = (coins_after + 1) × 2.
Undo Gate 3: (12 + 1) × 2 = 26 (coins before Gate 3, i.e. after Gate 2).
Undo Gate 2: (26 + 1) × 2 = 54 (coins before Gate 2, i.e. after Gate 1).
Undo Gate 1: (54 + 1) × 2 = 110 (coins at the very start).
Check forward: start 110 → Gate 1 takes 55+1=56, leaving 54 → Gate 2 takes 27+1=28, leaving 26 → Gate 3 takes 13+1=14, leaving 12. ✔

Why this is the only answer

Each "undo" step is a fixed, reversible arithmetic operation with no branching, so working backward from the one given ending value (12) produces exactly one possible starting value.

Source / fact used

None needed — solved purely by working backward from the given rule and ending amount, no lookup. Solved independently and checked forward before publishing to confirm it's forced and self-consistent.

Why this is the only answer
