Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Silk Road Caravan Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes (10 missions — grab a notebook and a pencil)

Grandpa Daan Jansen once walked part of the ancient Silk Road, the ancient trade road that once linked China to Europe. He kept a field journal of everything he found along the way.

Last week, his old computer scrambled the scanned pages into a jumble of clues. Grandpa needs a sharp detective to rebuild his journey, mission by mission.

Solve all ten missions, and you'll find what he hid at the very end: an old bronze caravan bell, engraved with one word that says exactly what it took to walk that road.

MISSION 01
EASY

The City Where the Road Begins

Grandpa's first journal page describes an incredible discovery. In 1974, farmers in China were digging a well. They found thousands of life-sized clay soldiers, buried underground for over 2,000 years, guarding an emperor's tomb.

These clay soldiers stand near the Chinese city where the ancient Silk Road began. Search to find that city's name.

Type the city's name as one plain word, with no apostrophe — for example: xian.

Hint 1

Try searching "terracotta army city China." The buried soldiers and the start of the Silk Road are in the very same city.

💡 Did you know? Those clay soldiers are called the Terracotta Army. Archaeologists think there are close to 8,000 of them, and each one has a slightly different face.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Rope of Knots

Camel drivers on the Silk Road tied knots in a rope to count their rest stops. Grandpa copied down the knot pattern from one driver's rope:

1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ?

Each number is made from the two knots before it. What is the next knot count in the pattern?

Hint 1

Look at how each number connects to the two numbers right before it. What do you get if you add them together?

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Loading the Caravan

Grab a pencil for this one — it's easier on paper than in your head.

Grandpa's caravan is carrying 72 bales of silk and 33 sacks of spice. Each camel can carry either 2 bales of silk, or 3 sacks of spice — never a mix of both.

How many camels in total does the whole caravan need?

Hint 1

Work out the silk camels and the spice camels separately, then add the two totals together.

Hint 2

72 bales of silk, 2 bales per camel. 33 sacks of spice, 3 sacks per camel. Divide each, then add.

Agent's Notebook: Write down the total number of camels somewhere safe. This isn't the last time you'll need it.
MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Grandpa's Dutch Footnote

Grandpa scribbled a footnote in Dutch, listing the goods his caravan traded. Three of these words use "de", and one uses "het".

zijde   koffie   thee   papier

(zijde = silk, koffie = coffee, thee = tea, papier = paper)

Find the odd one out: which word takes "het" instead of "de"?

Hint 1

If you're not sure, try each word in a sentence: "de ___ is mooi" or "het ___ is mooi." One will sound wrong for the other three.

Hint 2

Materials you write or draw on in Dutch are very often "het" words — think of "het potlood" (the pencil).

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Caravanserai Courtyard

A caravanserai is a walled inn where caravans slept safely at night. Grandpa sketched the shape of one courtyard, where the camels rested, with every side measured.

The caravan master needs to know how far the wall stretches all the way around, to order enough rope fencing.

What is the total distance around the outside of this courtyard, in meters?

Hint 1

Add up every labeled side of the shape, going all the way around once.

Hint 2

There are six labeled sides in total: 9, 4, 6, 6, 15, and 10 meters.

💡 Did you know? A single silkworm cocoon is made from one continuous thread. Unwound, it can stretch several hundred meters long — sometimes over 900 meters, longer than nine football fields!

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Merchants, Three Roads

Three merchants — Aisha, Bram, and Chen — are each carrying one good, to one city: Samarkand, Kashgar, or Baghdad. Grandpa's notes give four clues. Use them to work out where Chen is heading.

  1. Aisha sells silk.
  2. Whoever is heading to Kashgar sells jade.
  3. Chen is not heading to Samarkand.
  4. Bram does not sell spice.

Which city is Chen traveling to?

Hint 1

Start with clue 1 and clue 4. If Aisha already sells silk, and Bram doesn't sell spice, what must Bram sell instead?

Hint 2

Once you know Bram sells jade, clue 2 tells you exactly which city Bram is heading to. That leaves only one good for Chen, and clue 3 rules out one of the two remaining cities.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Name on the Map

Stage 1 of 2

For most of history, this trade road had no single name. Then a German geographer studying old trade maps gave it the name we still use today.

Search to find the year this geographer first used that name.

Hint 1

Try searching "who coined the term Silk Road." Look for a German geographer's name and a year in the 1800s.

Stage 2 of 2

A locked crate in the journal needs a number code. Add together all four digits of the year from Stage 1. Then double that total. That doubled number is the code.

Hint 1

Add the digits one at a time first, then multiply the sum by 2.

MISSION 08
HARD

Water for the Desert Crossing

Bactrian camels — the two-humped kind used on the Silk Road — have a hidden body part that shields their eyes from blowing desert sand.

Search to find how many eyelids a camel has.

Now multiply that number by the total camels from your notebook. Then multiply the result by 10. That's how many liters of water the caravan must pack for the rest of the crossing.

Hint 1

Try searching "how many eyelids does a camel have." It's more than the usual two.

Hint 2

Eyelid count × your camel total from your notebook × 10. Grab your pencil for the multiplication.

💡 Did you know? A camel's extra eyelid is thin and see-through. It works like a windshield wiper, closing sideways to clear away sand while the camel can still see where it's walking.

MISSION 09
BIG AHA!

The Gatekeeper's Riddle

The caravan must pass through 3 toll gates before reaching the last city. At every gate, the guard takes exactly half of the caravan's coins, then 1 extra coin on top.

After paying all 3 gates, in order, the caravan has exactly 12 coins left.

This one only works backward: start from 12 coins at the end, and undo each gate one at a time, working your way back to Gate 1. How many coins did the caravan start with, before Gate 1?

Hint 1

To undo a gate, reverse what it did. If a gate took half your coins plus 1, then before that gate you had: add 1 to what's left, then double it.

Hint 2

Undo Gate 3 first (starting from 12), then use that result to undo Gate 2, then undo Gate 1 last.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Grandpa's Last Page

The very last journal page is written in a simple code. Every letter has been shifted forward by 4 places in the alphabet (A becomes E, B becomes F, and so on).

Decode Grandpa's final word, shifting every letter back by 4 to find it:

TIVWIZIVERGI

Hint 1

Shift every letter backward by 4. T becomes P, I becomes E. Try the rest the same way.

Hint 2

Write the alphabet out on paper, then count back 4 letters from each coded letter, one at a time.

Case closed?

Knots, camels, coins, and codes — you rebuilt Grandpa's whole journey, one scrambled page at a time. That's real perseverance, Agent: the Silk Road took months to cross on foot, and you crossed it too, one stubborn puzzle after another.
