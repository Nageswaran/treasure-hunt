Mission 01 · Easy

Confirming the Masterpiece

Answer: blue
How to get there

The painting is Johannes Vermeer's "Girl with a Pearl Earring" (c. 1665), oil on canvas, 44.5 x 39 cm, held by the Mauritshuis museum in The Hague. In the downloaded reference photo, the front part of her headscarf wrapped around her head is a clear blue, while only the long piece of cloth draped down over her shoulder is gold/yellow. The gallery's claim that it is "gold from top to bottom" is false.

Why this is the only answer

The image is unambiguous once the child compares the two parts of the headscarf the puzzle explicitly separates ("the part wrapped snugly around her head" vs. "the piece hanging down"). Any other single-word color reading of the front wrap is not defensible from the photo.

Source / fact used

Verified via web_search: title, artist, date (c. 1665), and dimensions (44.5 x 39 cm) confirmed against the Mauritshuis's own collection page and Wikipedia. Image downloaded from a GitHub-hosted copy (IO-Studio/FirstGallery repo) whose embedded JPEG comment cites "commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Meisje_met_de_parel.jpg" as its source, and the image was viewed directly to confirm the blue-vs-gold headscarf detail before writing the puzzle. Mission 02 · Easy

The Forger's Portfolio

Answer: Monet
How to get there

Johannes Vermeer (1632-1675), Rembrandt van Rijn (1606-1669), and Frans Hals (c. 1582-1666) all worked during the Dutch Golden Age in the 17th century. Claude Monet (1840-1926) was a French Impressionist painter working almost two centuries later, in a completely different country and style. He is the odd one out.

Why this is the only answer

The other three painters share both the era (1600s) and movement (Dutch Golden Age); Monet fails both tests at once, so there's no reasonable way to pick a different name from the list.

Source / fact used

Verified via web_search against Britannica/Wikipedia-sourced biography summaries: Rembrandt 1606-1669, Frans Hals c.1582-1666 (both Dutch Golden Age); Claude Monet 1840-1926 (French Impressionism, founder of the movement).

Mission 04 · Medium

The Canvas Ratio

Answer: 114
How to get there

Real canvas: 445 mm wide × 390 mm tall. To keep the same proportions at a mini-print height of 100 mm: mini width = 445 × (100 ÷ 390) = 44,500 ÷ 390 = 114.1025... mm, which rounds to 114 mm.

Why this is the only answer

This is a direct proportion (cross-multiplication) problem with one correct numeric result once rounded to the nearest whole millimeter as instructed. Recomputed independently: 390 × 114 = 44,460 (remainder 40 of 390, i.e. ≈0.10), confirming 114.10 rounds down to 114, not up to 115.

Source / fact used

Real canvas dimensions (44.5 × 39 cm / 445 × 390 mm) verified via web_search against the Mauritshuis collection page and Wikipedia's "Girl with a Pearl Earring" article.

Mission 05 · Hard

The Coded Lab Note

Answer: viridian
How to get there

Using the pigpen key shown in the mission (three grids: A-I plain, J-R with a dot, and S-Z as X-shaped quadrants, plain then dotted), the eight coded symbols decode letter-by-letter to V-I-R-I-D-I-A-N, spelling "VIRIDIAN." This is the name of a bright green pigment. This is the delayed-recall plant: the mission text tells the child to "jot it down" — it is needed again, unprompted, in the Grand Finale's Stage 1.

Why this is the only answer

Each pigpen symbol has exactly one matching cell in the key (matched by side-count and dot), so the decoding is fully determined, letter by letter, with no alternative reading.

Source / fact used

Pigpen cipher is a standard, well-documented substitution cipher; the key and message were authored directly for this mission and are self-consistent (each glyph's shape was checked by hand against the key before publishing). Viridian's invention date (1838, chemist Pannetier in Paris; commercialized 1859) verified via web_search against Jackson's Art Blog and Winsor & Newton's pigment history pages — this is the fact the Finale relies on, since Vermeer died in 1675, over 150 years earlier.
