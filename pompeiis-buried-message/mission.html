Internet Detective Academy · Case File

Pompeii's Buried Message

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 70-90 minutes
A museum in Naples has lost track of its most important artifact: a sealed bronze box, dug up near Pompeii and never opened. The old excavation ledger that explains where it was found — and how to open it — has been scattered across ten pages, mixed up and half-coded. You've been called in to read the ruins like a real archaeologist, rebuild the ledger page by page, and finally crack the box. Solve every mission and you'll uncover the quality that let the original dig team keep going for centuries: buried deep at the very end of this case file.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Mountain That Woke Up

The ledger's first page is torn, but one line survives: "...buried by the mountain, in the year 79." Search online to find the name of the volcano near the Italian city of Naples that erupted in the year 79 AD and buried the city of Pompeii.

Type just the volcano's name (no "Mount" needed).

Hint 1

Try searching "volcano Pompeii 79 AD" — the answer is one famous word.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Lamp Layers

Every layer of ash the diggers remove reveals more oil lamps than the layer above it — always exactly double. Layer 1 had 3 lamps. Layer 2 had 6. Layer 3 had 12. Layer 4 had 24. If the pattern keeps going, how many lamps are in Layer 5?

Agent's notebook: while you count, you spot the dig supervisor's ledger, stamped with her badge number: 14. Worth remembering.

Hint 1

Each layer's number is the one before it, multiplied by 2.

💡 Did you know? Pompeii stayed buried and forgotten for so long that nobody knew exactly where it was — serious digging only began in 1748, nearly 1,700 years after the eruption.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Amphora Count

Two trench tags use Roman numerals. Trench A's tag reads XLVII amphorae (clay storage jars). Trench B's tag reads XXXVIII amphorae. Convert both to ordinary numbers, then add them together. Grab a pencil for this one!

Hint 1

XLVII means "5 less than 50, plus 2" — work out each Roman numeral separately before adding.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Broken Mosaic Floor

A Roman mosaic floor was found with one corner broken away by a fallen column. The diagram below shows its shape and every measurement you need. What is the total area of the mosaic floor that's left, in square meters?

The broken corner is missing — try splitting the shape into two rectangles first.
Hint 1

Imagine the floor as a full rectangle, then subtract the missing corner piece.

Hint 2

The full rectangle would be 9 m by 5 m. The missing corner piece is 3 m by 2 m.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Museum Placard

Next to the ledger, someone left a note in Dutch with the letters all jumbled up. Unscramble them to find the Dutch word for the very thing that destroyed Pompeii:

N A A K L U V

Hint 1

It's a 7-letter Dutch word. In English, this kind of mountain can erupt.

💡 Did you know? A Roman writer named Pliny the Younger watched the eruption from across the bay and wrote it all down. He said the ash cloud looked exactly like an umbrella pine tree — a tall trunk that spread out into branches at the top. His letter is one of the only eyewitness accounts of the disaster that still exists today.

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Diggers, Three Trenches

Three archaeologists — Elena, Marco, and Sofia — each dug one trench (North Wall, Forum, or Bakery) and each found one item (bronze coin, oil lamp, or glass bead). Use these clues to work out who dug the Forum trench and found the oil lamp there:

  1. The oil lamp was not found at the Bakery or the North Wall.
  2. The bronze coin was found by the person who dug at the Bakery.
  3. Marco did not dig at the Forum.
  4. Sofia found the glass bead.

No searching needed — just work it out from the clues above, crossing off options as you go.

Hint 1

Clue 1 tells you exactly which trench the oil lamp came from, even though it doesn't name it directly. Which trench is left over?

Hint 2

Once you know the oil lamp's trench, use clue 4 to place Sofia, then clue 3 to sort out Marco and Elena.

MISSION 07
HARD

The General's Secret Trick

Julius Caesar himself used a simple trick to hide messages from enemies: he shifted every letter forward by 3 places in the alphabet (A becomes D, B becomes E, and so on). Someone used his trick on the next clue. Shift every letter of this message backward by 3 places to decode it:

WKH EDNHUB

Hint 1

W shifted back 3 letters (V, U, T...) becomes T. Try the same for every letter.

Hint 2

The decoded message is two words and names a place you've already read about in this case file.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Sealed Trench Number

Stage 1 of 2

Search online to find the year archaeologists began the first serious excavation of Pompeii (it was in the 1700s, ordered by a King of Naples). Once you have that year, subtract 79 — the year of the eruption. How many years was the city buried before digging began?

Hint 1

Try searching "when did excavations of Pompeii begin."

Stage 2 of 2

The ledger's final note: "This trench's seal number equals the digit-sum of your Stage 1 answer (add up every digit in that number), plus the badge number you spotted earlier in this case. Write the seal number in Roman numerals."

Hint 1

Add the digits of your Stage 1 answer together first. Then remember: whose badge number did you jot down in your notebook a few missions back?

MISSION 09
AHA!

Lava, or Something Else?

Two students are arguing. Alex says, "Pompeii was buried under a river of hot, flowing lava." Priya says, "No — Pompeii was buried by ash and pumice falling from the sky, not lava." Search online to check which one is actually correct, using a trustworthy source like a museum, encyclopedia, or history site.

Type what really buried the city: "lava" or "ash and pumice"?

Hint 1

Search "did lava reach Pompeii" and read a reputable site's answer carefully.

💡 Did you know? In 1863, an archaeologist named Giuseppe Fiorelli found hollow spaces in the hardened ash — the exact shapes left behind by bodies and objects that had rotted away. He poured in plaster to fill the hollows, revealing lifelike casts of people and even doors and furniture from the moment of the eruption.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Supervisor's Diary

Inside the bronze box is one last page — the dig supervisor's diary, working backward from the final count of coins in her offering box:

"I doubled the coins in the box. Then I paid 5 coins to a guide. I doubled what remained. Then I paid 8 coins for tools. I ended with exactly 10 coins. How many coins were in the box at the very start?"

Work backward from the end, undoing each step in reverse order.

Hint 1

Start from the last number, 10, and undo the very last thing that happened first: she paid 8 coins, so before that she had 10 + 8.

Hint 2

Undo each step in this order: add back the 8, undo the doubling by halving, add back the 5, then undo the first doubling by halving again.

Case closed?

The box finally creaks open. Inside is a small bronze plaque, worn smooth by centuries underground, engraved with one word: PERSEVERANCE. The dig team who worked this trench in 1748 didn't have computers, flashlights, or search engines — just patience, one layer of ash at a time. You just proved you have that same quality, agent.
