Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Class of 2001 Time Capsule

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 70-90 minutes
Maple Street Elementary is throwing its biggest party of the year today: the 25-year reunion of the Class of 2001! Twenty-five years ago, a group of soon-to-graduate fourth graders sealed a time capsule and buried it somewhere on the school grounds. Now the grown-up alumni are back — but nobody quite remembers the details, and some of the old notes inside are going to need real detective work to crack. The reunion committee has hired YOU, Junior Detective, to verify the old facts, solve the class's leftover puzzles, and help reveal the one word the Class of 2001 left behind for whoever found this box. Ready? Let's dig in.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Sock Full of Secrets

Right at the top of the capsule, wrapped in an old gym sock, is a small handheld game console — still in decent shape. Nobody on the reunion committee can remember exactly when the class got these; they just recall it was "brand new that year."

Take a look at the photo below. Then find out: what month and day did this exact console officially go on sale in the United States? (Same year the capsule was sealed — 2001.)

Look closely at its shape and button layout — this isn't the original, brick-shaped Game Boy. It's the console that came after it.
Hint 1

Search for the console's full name (it's a Nintendo handheld) plus "US release date 2001." Reputable gaming-history sites will agree on the exact day.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Class Letter

Inside a plastic sleeve, a little yellowed at the edges, is a handwritten letter:

"Dear Future Detective, We are Mrs. Alcott's 4th grade class of 2001! We wrapped up our very last day of school by burying this box under the flagpole on a warm, sunny afternoon. All 24 of us signed our names below before we sealed the lid. (Psst — that number might matter again someday. Worth jotting down!) We hope whoever finds this had just as much fun in school as we did. — Class of 2001"

Question: according to the letter, exactly where did the class bury the capsule?

Hint 1

Read the letter again slowly — the answer is a single object standing on the school grounds, named right in the second sentence.

💡 Did you know? Wikipedia — the encyclopedia website millions of people use today — first went online on January 15, 2001, the very same year this capsule was sealed. Back then it only existed in English!

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Order of the Afternoon

Five things happened at today's reunion — but everyone's retelling has the order mixed up! Using only the clues below (no guessing from "what usually happens"), figure out the true order of the afternoon.

The five events:

Code N — Alumni signed in and got name tags in the gym.
Code P — The principal gave a welcome speech.
Code R — A ribbon was cut at the edge of the schoolyard.
Code D — Shovels came out and the capsule was dug up.
Code L — The old class letter was read aloud to everyone.

The clues:

1. Signing in happened before the principal said a single word.
2. The ribbon was cut after the speech ended, but before any shovel touched the ground.
3. Digging happened right after the ribbon was cut.
4. The letter wasn't read until after the box was fully dug up.
5. The letter reading was the grand finale of the whole afternoon.

Type the five codes in the correct order, all together with no spaces or commas — like ABCDE.

Hint 1

Start with the clue that only involves two events and no "before/after" ambiguity — which one absolutely has to come first?

Hint 2

Clue 1 fixes the very first event. Clue 2 then fixes the next two, in order. Clues 3-5 lock in the rest — there's only one order left that fits everything.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Tiny Square That Held (Almost) Nothing

Deeper in the capsule is a hard little plastic square with a metal slider on one edge — see the photo below. Classrooms in 2001 used these to save their work, because USB flash drives barely existed yet.

Research: how much data could ONE of these actually hold, in megabytes (MB)?

Now the real test: let's say a single photo from a phone today takes up about 4 MB. If you HAD to save just one modern phone photo using only disks like this one, how many of these old disks would you need in total?

This exact size and shape of disk was standard in almost every school computer lab in 2001.
Hint 1

Search "3.5 inch floppy disk capacity" — the exact number of megabytes might surprise you.

Hint 2

Once you know how many MB fit on one disk, divide 4 by that number — then round UP, since you can't use part of a disk to save part of a photo.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Fleur's Postcard

Folded inside an old comic book is a postcard from a pen pal named Fleur, who lived in the Netherlands and wrote to the class back in 2001. Part of her note reads:

"Hallo vrienden! Bij mij op de school is het heel gezellig. Na de klas spelen we buiten op het grote ___."
(Translation: "Hello friends! At my school it's very cozy. After class we play outside on the big ___.")

The missing Dutch word is plein (it means "square" or "yard" — think of a big open space kids play on).

Every Dutch noun needs either de or het in front of it — never both, and you can't guess just by the sound of the word. Fleur already used "de school" and "de klas" correctly earlier in her note. But "plein" is one of the tricky ones that breaks that pattern — it needs the OTHER article.

Which article goes with "plein"? Type the article and the word together, lowercase, exactly how Fleur would write it (for example: de klas).

Hint 1

Most Dutch nouns take "de" — but a smaller group of words take "het" instead, and there's no shortcut for knowing which without checking.

Hint 2

Search an online Dutch dictionary (like woorden.org) for the word "plein." Dutch dictionaries always mark whether a noun is a de-word or a het-word.

💡 Did you know? The first-ever iPod was announced on October 23, 2001, and went on sale a few weeks later that November. It could hold about 1,000 songs on just 5 gigabytes of storage — which felt like an impossibly huge number of songs to carry around back then!

MISSION 06
HARD

Whose Treasure Was Whose?

Four former classmates — Jordan, Casey, Priya, and Mateo — each tucked one personal item into the capsule: a mixtape cassette, a Beanie Baby bear, a stack of Pogs, and a friendship bracelet. A water-stained tag names all four kids and all four items, but the connecting lines have worn away completely. Use these five clues to figure out exactly who buried what.

1. Priya's item was not the Beanie Baby bear.
2. Either Jordan or Mateo buried the cassette tape.
3. Casey did not bury the Pogs.
4. Mateo's item is something you could listen to.
5. Casey did not bury the Beanie Baby bear, either.

Question: what item did Priya bury?

Hint 1

Clue 4 tells you exactly what Mateo buried — start there, then clue 2 falls into place on its own.

Hint 2

Once you know Mateo's item, clues 1 and 5 leave only one person who could have the Beanie Baby bear. Everything else follows after that.

MISSION 07
HARD

Four Memories, One Truth

The reunion committee asked four grown-up alumni to describe the burial day from memory. Only ONE of them remembers it with zero mistakes. Read carefully:

Jordan: "It was pouring rain, and all 24 of us signed the log before we buried the box under the flagpole."
Casey: "It was a beautiful sunny day, all 25 of us signed the log, and we buried the box under the flagpole."
Priya: "It was pouring rain, all 24 of us signed the log, and we buried the box beside the big oak tree."
Mateo: "It was a beautiful sunny day, all 24 of us signed the log, and we buried the box under the flagpole."

School records confirm two things for certain: it was a sunny day, and the box went under the flagpole (not any tree). Whose memory is completely accurate — matching every single detail?

Hint 1

Two of the four alumni already get both the weather AND the location right — so weather and location alone won't be enough to pick a winner. What's the third detail each of them mentions?

Hint 2

Scroll back up to the very first letter the Class of 2001 left behind — one number in there matters a lot right now.

MISSION 08
HARD

Twenty-Five Years, Give or Take a Few Days

The class letter is dated the very last day of school: June 15, 2001. Today's reunion is happening on August 14, 2026.

Grab a pencil for this one — you'll want scratch paper. Exactly how many days have passed between those two dates? Don't forget: some of those 25 years were leap years with an extra day tucked into February!

Hint 1

First count the leap years between 2001 and 2026 (years divisible by 4). Each one adds exactly one extra day to your total.

Hint 2

Try this order: (25 years × 365 days) + (your leap-day count) gets you all the way to June 15, 2026. Then add the handful of extra days from June 15 to August 14, 2026 on top of that.

💡 Did you know? In 2001, most homes connected to the internet using "dial-up" — a screechy, beeping connection that could take over a minute just to load a single web page, and it tied up the phone line the entire time it was connected!

MISSION 09
BIG AHA

The Box Inside the Box

Stage 1 of 2

Beneath the letter, wrapped in a plastic bag, is a real five-dollar bill from 2001 — the class's leftover fundraiser money. A note beside it asks: was leaving this $5 bill untouched for 25 years actually a smart move, money-wise?

To answer that, you need to know what $5 from 2001 is really worth today. Search for a real online inflation calculator (one that mentions the U.S. government's official CPI data) and look up: how many of TODAY'S dollars would you need to match the buying power of $5.00 from 2001? Round to the nearest cent, and type just the number (like 9.43 — no dollar sign).

Hint 1

Try searching "inflation calculator $5 in 2001" — look for a calculator that explains it's using the CPI (Consumer Price Index).

Hint 2

An accurate calculator should land you somewhere between $9 and $10. If you're way outside that range, double-check you searched for the year 2001, not some other year.

Stage 2 of 2

Now you know: it would take roughly your Stage 1 answer, in today's dollars, to buy what that old $5 bill could buy back in 2001 — proof that money left sitting still doesn't stay "still" in value!

But here's where it gets interesting: tucked underneath the $5 bill is a TINY inner box with a 2-digit combination lock. To crack it, take your Stage 1 answer apart and add its two halves together: the whole-dollar part, plus the cents part. (For example, if your Stage 1 answer had been $12.07, you'd add 12 + 07 = 19.)

What 2-digit combination opens the inner box?

Hint 1

Split your Stage 1 answer at the decimal point into two whole numbers, then simply add them.

Hint 2

If Stage 1's answer was $9.43, that's 9 and 43 — what do those two numbers add up to?

The lock clicks open. Inside the tiny box is a single index card: "Congratulations on solving this, Junior Detective. We hope you have as much fun today as we did in 2001. — The Class of 2001, buried with love." One part of this case is still waiting for you...

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Code

Taped to the very bottom of the capsule is a strip of paper with six shifted letters: U G Q B M K.

A note beside it reads: "Shift each letter backward through the alphabet by the number of classmates who signed our very first letter to you."

Work out the shift amount, decode the message, and type the word you find.

Hint 1

Remember that number of classmates from the very first class letter you read? That's your shift amount. "Shift backward" means each letter moves earlier in the alphabet (Z becomes Y, Y becomes X, and so on).

Hint 2

Shifting backward by a big number like 24 is the same as shifting FORWARD by a much smaller one, since the alphabet only has 26 letters (24 back = 2 forward). Try it: U forward 2 becomes W.

Case closed?

Case closed, Junior Detective! The word WISDOM was the Class of 2001's gift to you — because every clue you cracked today, from decades-old floppy disks to inflation math to four competing memories, took real thinking, real patience, and real wisdom. Twenty-five years from now, maybe it'll be YOUR time capsule someone else digs up.
