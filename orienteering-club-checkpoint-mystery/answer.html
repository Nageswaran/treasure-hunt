Mission 01 · Easy · Navigation & Orientation

The Smudged Bearing

Answer: Northwest
How to get there

A bearing is measured clockwise from North (0°). Using the compass rose in the mission, find the tick mark labeled 315°. It sits between West (270°) and North (360°/0°), and it's marked "NW" — Northwest.

Why this is the only answer

Each of the eight labeled directions on the rose has one, and only one, matching degree value. 315° corresponds to exactly one label: Northwest.

Source / fact used

Standard compass-bearing convention (bearings measured clockwise from North, 0°-360°). No external source needed — solvable entirely from the diagram in the mission.

Counting the Course by Foot

Answer: 400 (meters)
How to get there

Add the double-paces: 54 + 108 + 78 = 240 double-paces total. Using the given rate of 60 double-paces = 100 meters: 240 ÷ 60 = 4, and 4 × 100 = 400 meters.

Why this is the only answer

The addition and the conversion rate are both fixed numbers stated in the puzzle, so there is exactly one correct total.

Source / fact used

Arithmetic recomputed directly from the numbers given in the puzzle: 54+108+78=240; 240/60×100=400.

Mission 04 · Medium · Science (magnetism) · Internet research

The Needle That Never Lies

Answer: Magnetic field
How to get there

Searching "why does a compass point north" quickly leads to kid-friendly explanations: Earth acts like a giant magnet because of its molten iron core, and this creates a magnetic field that pulls the needle's north end toward magnetic north.

Why this is the only answer

Multiple reputable sources (Live Science, Britannica Kids, NOAA) agree on this single term; there's no competing explanation a search would surface.

Source / fact used

Live Science — How does a compass work?

Between missions · Did you know?

Fact check: The magnetic north pole drifts

Verified: Earth's magnetic north pole is not fixed — it moves roughly 55 km (about 34 miles) per year due to changes deep in Earth's outer core.

Searching "history of orienteering" leads straight to the sport's origin: it began in Sweden in the late 1800s as military training for soldiers, with the term first used in 1886 and the first competition in 1893.

Why this is the only answer

Mission 08 · Hard · Mathematics (geometry / measurement diagram)

The Rope for the Assembly Field

Answer: 56 (meters)
How to get there

The two unlabeled sides are found by subtraction: the right side = 10 − 4 = 6 m, and the bottom side = 18 − 6 = 12 m. The perimeter is the sum of all six sides: 18 + 6 + 6 + 4 + 12 + 10 = 56 meters.

Why this is the only answer

The four labeled dimensions (18, 10, 6, 4) fully determine the two missing sides through simple subtraction, leaving exactly one possible perimeter.

Source / fact used

Recomputed directly from the diagram's own labeled dimensions. Diagram check: outer rectangle 18×10 with a 6×4 notch removed from the top-right corner; area would be 18×10 − 6×4 = 156 m² (not asked, but consistent), and perimeter = 56 m either by adding all six sides or by the shortcut that a corner notch doesn't change a rectangle's perimeter (2×(18+10)=56).
