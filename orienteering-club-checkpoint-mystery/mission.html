Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Fernwood Checkpoint Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 80-100 minutes
Tomorrow at dawn, Fernwood Trail Runners race for the Fernwood Cup — the club's biggest day. Last night, course-setter Jonas slipped near Otter Creek and dropped his control folder in the water. Half his notes are smudged, and the final checkpoint flag is completely missing. You're the club's sharpest new member. Solve each clue to rebuild the course before the runners arrive.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Smudged Bearing

One line in Jonas's soggy notebook is still readable: "From the Old Oak, walk on a bearing of 315° to find Flag 7."

A bearing is the direction you face, measured in degrees. It's counted clockwise starting from North (0°).

Use the compass rose to match the bearing to its direction.
🖊️ Agent's Notebook: This is worth remembering — at Fernwood, west-side controls always use EVEN numbers, and east-side controls always use ODD numbers.
Hint 1

Start at North (0°) and count clockwise around the circle. Each turn between labels is 45°.

MISSION 02
EASY

Whose Punch Was First?

This morning, four practice controls were punched. Each punch stamps the exact time on a card.

The printer jammed and printed the times in the wrong order. Here's what came out:

Control B — 09:07  ·  Control D — 09:11  ·  Control A — 09:14  ·  Control C — 09:22

Sort the times from earliest to latest to find the real order.
Hint 1

Line the four times up from smallest to largest, like sorting numbers on a ruler.

💡 Did you know? A compass needle doesn't point at the exact North Pole. It points at magnetic north, which drifts a little from true north — the gap is called declination.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Counting the Course by Foot

Orienteers measure distance by counting "double-paces" — one count every time your LEFT foot lands.

Your own double-pace count is steady: 60 double-paces cover 100 meters on flat ground.

Grab a pencil for this one. To rebuild three missing legs of the course, you counted:

Leg 4: 54 double-paces  ·  Leg 5: 108 double-paces  ·  Leg 6: 78 double-paces

Add the double-paces together, then use your 60-double-paces-per-100-meters count to find the total distance.
Hint 1

First add all three double-pace counts into one total.

Hint 2

Divide that total by 60, then multiply the result by 100.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Needle That Never Lies

Every compass in the gear box points the exact same way, all on its own. No batteries, no strings.

Search online to find out: what invisible field surrounds our whole planet and pulls every compass needle north?

Type the two-word answer you find.
Hint 1

Try searching "why does a compass point north."

Hint 2

The answer is two words, and the first word starts with "magnetic."

💡 Did you know? Earth's magnetic north pole doesn't sit still. It drifts about 55 kilometers (34 miles) every single year.

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Volunteers, Three Flags

Three volunteers set up practice flags tonight: Mia, Leo, and Zara.

Each carried one flag color (Orange, Red, or Yellow) and one control number (14, 22, or 35).

Here's what you know:

1. The Orange flag bundle is marked with control number 22.

2. Zara's control number is not 14.

3. Mia is not carrying the Orange flag bundle.

4. The volunteer carrying the Yellow flag bundle has a smaller control number than Leo.

5. Leo set up his control on the west side of the field.

Work through the clues one at a time to find each volunteer's exact color and number.
Hint 1

Start with clue 1 — it tells you exactly which number belongs with Orange.

Hint 2

Clue 3 rules out one person for Orange. That leaves only two possible owners for it — test each one.

MISSION 07
HARD

Where It All Began

A torn scrap in Jonas's folder says: "the sport started as army training, over 100 years ago, in one Nordic country."

Search online to find out which country that was.

Type just the country's name.
Hint 1

Try searching "history of orienteering."

Hint 2

This country's flag is blue and yellow.

💡 Did you know? The first real orienteering competition was held in Sweden on 28 May 1893, at a military games day.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Rope for the Assembly Field

Overnight rain flattened the boundary tape around the assembly field, shaped like a giant letter L.

You need to know exactly how much rope to buy to re-mark the whole boundary.

Every side you need is either labeled or can be worked out from the labels.
Hint 1

Two sides aren't labeled. Find them first by comparing the outer rectangle to the notch.

Hint 2

The missing right side is 10 − 4 meters. The missing bottom side is 18 − 6 meters. Now add up all six sides.

MISSION 09
AHA!

Two Volunteers, One Truth

At the trail junction, two volunteers are waiting: Ravi and Tessa. Their directions don't match.

In this club's tradition, one volunteer at the junction ALWAYS tells the truth, and the other ALWAYS lies. You don't know which is which.

Ravi says: "The last checkpoint is at the Stone Bridge, and Tessa is telling the truth."

Tessa says: "The last checkpoint is at the Beech Tree."

Remember: in an "and" statement, if even one part is false, the whole statement is false.

Figure out who must be lying, then trust the other one completely.
Hint 1

Try assuming Ravi always tells the truth. Does that force Tessa to also be truthful — and is that allowed?

Hint 2

Since only one of them lies, Ravi being the liar means Tessa must be the truth-teller. What does that make true?

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Control

Stage 1 of 2

You've rebuilt almost the whole course. Only the very last control is missing — the one Jonas never placed before his fall.

Search online to find the year the International Orienteering Federation (IOF) was founded. Then subtract 1900 from that year.

Hint 1

Try searching "International Orienteering Federation founded."

Stage 2 of 2

Every control on Fernwood's master list has a nickname. Here's the page Jonas pinned to the shed wall:

58 — Fox Hollow  ·  59 — Beaver Dam  ·  60 — Crow's Nest  ·  61 — Whispering Pines  ·  62 — Otter Bend  ·  63 — Badger Set

Find the control number that matches your Stage 1 answer.

Type its nickname to reveal the last checkpoint.
Hint 1

Use the exact number from Stage 1 — it's one of the six listed here.

Hint 2

Match your number to its row in the list, then read the nickname next to it.

Case closed?

A soaked notebook, a missing flag, two volunteers arguing at a fork — none of it stopped you. You kept going until every control was back in place. That steady effort has a name: perseverance. Tomorrow's Fernwood Cup is saved. Case closed, Agent.
