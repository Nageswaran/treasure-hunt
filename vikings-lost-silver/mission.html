Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Vikings' Lost Silver

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 90–120 minutes (this is a big case — take breaks!)
A metal detectorist just pulled a corroded silver disc from a riverbank near Wijk bij Duurstede in the Netherlands — and it's over a thousand years old. Wrapped around it was a torn, soggy scrap of a Viking captain's log. The Internet Detective Academy has been called in to reconstruct the last voyage of the longship Sea Serpent: where she raided, what she carried, and the port she was racing to reach. Ten clues remain. Follow every one, Agent — the river doesn't give up its secrets easily.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Wonder of the Age

The log's first readable line says: "Year 834. We struck the great trading town again — richest port on the river, they call it the wonder of the age." Historians know this town well. It stood on a branch of the Rhine, and today the Dutch town of Wijk bij Duurstede stands near its ruins.

Search to find the old name of this trading town — the name the Vikings actually used for it.

Hint 1

Try searching "Wijk bij Duurstede Viking trading town old name."

💡 Did you know? Vikings first raided this town in the year 834 — and kept coming back for decades, because it was one of the wealthiest trading ports in northern Europe.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Quartermaster's List

Sea Serpent's quartermaster scratched a list of everything in the ship's hold. But something's wrong — one item on this list could not possibly have been there in the year 834. It hadn't been invented yet!

Which item doesn't belong?

  • A) A folding bronze balance scale, for weighing silver
  • B) A bundle of walrus ivory tusks
  • C) A small glass jar of honey
  • D) A wind-up mechanical pocket watch
  • E) A coil of rope made from walrus hide

Type just the odd item out (for example: "the scale").

Hint 1

Vikings lived over a thousand years ago. Think about which item needs tiny gears and springs to work at all.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

A River With Two Names

Dorestad stood beside a mighty river that still flows through the Netherlands today. In Dutch, rivers always get a little word in front of their name.

Is it "de Rijn" or "het Rijn"? Type your answer as two words.

Hint 1

Nearly every Dutch river uses the same little word before its name — think of "de Maas" or "de Waal."

Hint 2

It's the same little word you'd use for "de kat" or "de auto" — not "het."

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Scratched Into the Tiller

The captain scratched a word into the ship's tiller using runes — real letters Vikings carved over a thousand years ago. Use the key below to decode it.

ᚱ = R (reið)
ᛅ = A (ár)
ᛏ = T (týr)
ᛋ = S (sól)
ᛋ ᛏ ᛅ ᚱ

Match each symbol to its letter, in order, to read the captain's word.

Hint 1

Match each rune shape to its letter in the key, one at a time — don't skip any.

Hint 2

Once you have all four letters in order, they spell something you'd look for in the night sky.

📓 Agent's Notebook: Right next to the captain's rune-carving is a tally of hash-marks — the crew roster. Sea Serpent sailed with 32 crew. This is worth remembering...
MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Shipwright's Plan

A page from the shipwright's plans shows the deck of Sea Serpent — a long deck with a small notch cut out of one corner for the raised prow platform. Every measurement is marked on the drawing.

Find the total area of the usable deck, in square meters. Type just the number.
Hint 1

Try splitting the deck into two rectangles — the notch cuts one small rectangle out of a corner.

Hint 2

Find the area of the big rectangle first, as if there were no notch at all. Then subtract the missing piece.

💡 Did you know? Real Viking traders carried their own small, folding weighing scales, so they could check on the spot that silver they were paid was really solid silver, and not a fake.

MISSION 06
HARD

Four Traders, Four Ports

A torn trade ledger names four traders — Ingrid, Bjorn, Astrid, and Leif — each sailing from a different port (Birka, Hedeby, Dublin, Kaupang) and each selling a different good (amber, walrus ivory, honey, wool). Water damage destroyed most of the ledger, but six clues survived:

  • 1. The amber trader did not sail from Birka or Dublin.
  • 2. Bjorn sailed from Hedeby.
  • 3. Astrid did not sell wool or honey.
  • 4. The trader from Kaupang sold walrus ivory.
  • 5. Ingrid sold honey.
  • 6. Leif did not sail from Birka.

Figure out which trader sold walrus ivory — then tell us which port they sailed from.

Hint 1

Start with the clues that name a trader directly — Bjorn and Ingrid are pinned down right away.

Hint 2

Once you know Bjorn sells amber and the Kaupang trader sells ivory, clue 3 tells you exactly what Astrid sells.

MISSION 07
HARD

Two Trade Offers

Grab a pencil for this one. Vikings didn't use coins the way we do — they paid with "hacksilver," pieces of silver weighed on a scale. Two traders offer silver for the same haul of walrus ivory:

Trader A offers three broken arm-ring pieces (18 g, 25 g, and 14 g) plus two silver coins (6 g each).

Trader B offers one silver bar (71 g) plus one coin (9 g).

How many more grams of silver does Trader B's offer weigh than Trader A's?

Hint 1

Add up all of Trader A's pieces first, then all of Trader B's pieces, before comparing them.

Hint 2

Trader A: 18 + 25 + 14 + 6 + 6. Trader B: 71 + 9. Then subtract the smaller total from the bigger one.

MISSION 08
HARD

Working Backward From the Dock

By the time Sea Serpent reached port, her silver had passed through three stops. Work backward from the end to find where it started.

  • At the last stop, the crew paid a 12-gram river toll — leaving exactly 45 grams.
  • Before that toll, they had paid a shipwright 18 grams for a mast repair.
  • Before that repair, they had split their silver exactly in half with a rival crew, as agreed.

How many grams of silver did they have at the very start, before any of this happened?

Hint 1

Try working from the end backward — undo each step one at a time, starting with the very last one.

Hint 2

Undo the river toll first (add 12), then undo the shipwright's payment (add 18), then undo the halving (multiply by 2).

💡 Did you know? Real Viking sailors found their way across open water without a magnetic compass — using the sun, and maybe even sunlight-splitting crystals called sunstones, to figure out direction on cloudy days.

MISSION 09
HARD

The Sun Compass

Stage 1 of 2

Vikings crossing open water still found their way, even on cloudy days. In 1948, archaeologists found a mysterious wooden disc under the ruins of a monastery in Greenland. Today, many believe Norse sailors used it as a sun compass.

Search to find the one-word place-name this disc is called after (it's known as the "___ disc"). Type just that one word.

Hint 1

This disc was found in Greenland, in the ruins of a Benedictine convent, by an archaeologist named Christen Vebæk. Try searching "Viking sun compass disc Greenland 1948."

Stage 2 of 2

Count the letters in your Stage 1 answer. Multiply that by the number of crew you noted in your Agent's Notebook earlier in the case. That total is how many silver pennies the harbor toll-keeper demands before Sea Serpent can dock.

Hint 1

First count every letter in your Stage 1 word carefully — don't forget repeated letters count twice.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Port at the End of the Log

Case file complete. Sea Serpent paid her toll and sailed on. Every clue points to one legendary port: a Viking Age trading town on a small island in a Swedish lake, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Search to find its name — the final entry in your case file.

Hint 1

The island is called Björkö, about 30 km from Stockholm.

Hint 2

The lake is called Mälaren. Try searching "Viking trading town Lake Mälaren UNESCO."

Case closed?

From a soggy scrap of log to a lost port on a Swedish island — you followed runes, ledgers, silver, and the sun itself to bring a thousand-year-old voyage back to light. That's real discovery, Agent: the thrill of turning scattered clues into something true.
