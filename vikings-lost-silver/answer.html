Mission 01 · Easy

The Wonder of the Age

Answer: Dorestad
How to get there

Search "Wijk bij Duurstede Viking trading town." Dorestad was a major early-medieval trading emporium in the southeast of the province of Utrecht, near a bend of the Rhine (where it splits into the Lek and the Kromme Rijn). It was repeatedly raided by Vikings starting in 834 CE. The modern town of Wijk bij Duurstede stands near where it once stood — the town's name even means "settlement/river-bend near Dorestad."

Why this is the only answer

Only one historic trading town is associated with this location and this raid date — Dorestad is the name used in every historical source for the settlement.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search, cross-checked against Wikipedia: Dorestad and The Viking Herald's article on Dorestad and the Viking raids on the Rhine.

Mission 02 · Easy · Logical Reasoning (Foundational — classification)

The Quartermaster's List

Answer: the pocket watch
How to get there

Mechanical clocks weren't invented until several centuries after the Viking Age (roughly the 13th–14th century for early mechanical clocks, and portable "pocket" watches came even later, in the 16th century). Every other item is a genuine, well-documented Viking-age object: folding balance scales for weighing hacksilver, traded walrus ivory, honey, and walrus-hide rope were all real Viking-age goods and tools.

Why this is the only answer

It's the only item that requires technology (gears, springs) that didn't exist yet in 834 CE — every other item is independently verifiable as authentic to the era.

Source / fact used

General history of mechanical clocks and watches; Viking hacksilver/scale-weighing practice verified via Wikipedia: Hacksilver and The Viking Herald.

Mission 03 · Medium · Dutch Language

A River With Two Names

Answer: de Rijn
How to get there

In Dutch, rivers (and mountain ranges) consistently take the article "de," never "het" — for example de Rijn, de Maas, de Waal, de IJssel. This is a standard, well-documented rule of Dutch grammar.

Why this is the only answer

"Het Rijn" is grammatically incorrect Dutch; native sources and grammar guides confirm "de Rijn" is the only correct form.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search of Dutch grammar sources and usage examples (Wikipedia's "Rijnland" and "Kromme Rijn" articles, and standard Dutch de/het grammar guides).

Two Trade Offers

Answer: 11 (grams)
How to get there

Trader A: 18 + 25 + 14 + 6 + 6 = 69 g. Trader B: 71 + 9 = 80 g. Difference: 80 − 69 = 11 g.

Why this is the only answer

Both totals are fixed by the numbers given in the puzzle; there's only one correct sum for each trader and one correct difference.

Mission 08 · Hard · Logical Reasoning (Advanced — working backward)

Working Backward From the Dock

Answer: 150 (grams)
How to get there

Start at the end (45 g) and undo each step in reverse order: undo the 12-gram toll → 45 + 12 = 57 g. Undo the 18-gram shipwright payment → 57 + 18 = 75 g. Undo the 50/50 split with the rival crew → 75 g was half of the original, so the original was 75 × 2 = 150 g.

Why this is the only answer

Each operation (subtract a toll, subtract a payment, split in half) has exactly one correct inverse, and working backward through all three in order gives a single forced starting amount. Checking forward confirms it: 150 ÷ 2 = 75, − 18 = 57, − 12 = 45. ✓

Source / fact used

Mission 09 · Hard · Big "Aha!" · Multi-stage (Science + Internet Research + Math)

The Sun Compass

Stage 1 answer: Uunartoq

The Uunartoq Disc is a wooden fragment found in 1948 in the ruins of a Benedictine convent in Greenland (excavated by archaeologist Christen Vebæk), believed to be a Norse sun compass or "twilight board" used for navigation. It is named after Uunartoq, the location in Greenland where it was found.

Stage 2 (final) answer: 256

"Uunartoq" has 8 letters (U-U-N-A-R-T-O-Q). Multiplied by the 32 crew planted in Mission 04's Agent's Notebook: 8 × 32 = 256.

Why this is the only answer

Stage 1 has one verifiable name attached to this specific artifact. Stage 2 is a fixed multiplication of two fixed numbers (the letter count from Stage 1, and the crew number planted earlier) — there's exactly one correct product.

Grand Finale

The Port at the End of the Log

Answer: Birka
How to get there

Search "Viking trading town Lake Mälaren UNESCO." Birka was one of the most important Viking Age trading towns in northern Europe (active roughly 750–980 CE), located on the island of Björkö in Lake Mälaren, about 30 km west of Stockholm, Sweden. Birka and the neighboring site of Hovgården were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

Why this is the only answer

Only one Viking Age trading town matches all three clues at once: an island in a Swedish lake, and UNESCO World Heritage status.

Source / fact used

Verified via UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Birka and Hovgården and the World History Encyclopedia's article on Birka.
