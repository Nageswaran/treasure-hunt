Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Eclipse Chaser's Notebook

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes (across all 10 missions)

Professor Marieke Bos is an "umbraphile" — a person who chases the Moon's shadow around the whole world just to stand inside it for a few minutes. She's picking one young detective to join her next expedition, and she's left her old field notebook at the Academy as the test.

Ten case files are hidden inside: maps, codes, sums, and one very stubborn locked trunk. Crack them all in order, and the trunk will open — revealing the one thing every eclipse chaser needs most.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Shadow's First Footstep

Page one of the notebook shows a rough sketch: a dark stripe racing across a map of the United States, from the Pacific coast to the Atlantic coast. A note reads: "August 21, 2017 — the day the Moon's shadow crossed an entire country. Find out where it first touched land."

Search the internet for the path of the 2017 total solar eclipse. In which U.S. state did the Moon's shadow first reach land that day?

Hint 1

Try searching "path of totality 2017 map". Look at the far west end of the path, where it meets the Pacific coastline — which state is that?

MISSION 02
EASY

Professor Bos's Sticky Note

A yellow sticky note is stuck to page two, in Professor Bos's own handwriting — in Dutch. She jotted down six words about the night sky:

de zon · de maan · de ster · de hemel · de planeet · het heelal

Five of these words use "de". Only one uses "het". Type that one word (with its article) — the odd one out.

Hint 1

Say each word out loud with "de" in front of it instead. Five will sound completely normal in Dutch — one will sound wrong.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Eclipse-Glasses Ledger

Grab a pencil for this one. Five base camps along the 2017 path each need eclipse-viewing glasses. Here's the ledger page:

CampGlasses needed
Salem34
Casper58
Carbondale71
Nashville46
Charleston29

Two suppliers can provide them:

Supplier A: €2 per pair, buy any amount you like.
Supplier B: only sells packs of 10 pairs, for €17 per pack. You must buy whole packs, even if you don't need every pair in the last one.

First, add up the total number of glasses needed across all five camps. Then work out the total cost from each supplier for that many glasses. Type how many EUROS you would save by choosing the cheaper supplier over the more expensive one (just the number, no € sign).

Hint 1

Add the five camp numbers first — write it as a column addition so you don't lose track of a digit.

Hint 2

Supplier B only sells full packs of 10. If the total isn't an exact multiple of 10, you still have to round UP to the next whole pack.

💡 Did you know? The Netherlands won't see another total solar eclipse until October 7, 2135 — so nobody alive today will see one there twice!

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Fenced Observation Field

Professor Bos fenced off a field for her telescopes, shaped like an "L" because one corner had to be left open for the supply shed. Here's her sketch, with a few measurements marked:

The notch (top-right) is 6 m wide and 4 m tall. Find the total area of the fenced "L" shape, in square meters.
Hint 1

Try splitting the L-shape into two simple rectangles — a big one and a smaller one — then add their areas.

Hint 2

The big rectangle can be 12 m wide by 10 m tall (the full height, up to where the notch starts). What's left over on the right forms the second rectangle — work out its width and height from the labels, then add both areas.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Four Snapshots From Totality

Four torn photo captions fell out of the notebook, all mixed up. Put them back in the order they actually happen during a total solar eclipse:

A — "The Moon has fully slid away; the Sun is a whole bright circle again, and the sky is completely light."

B — "A blazing white ring called the corona glows around a perfectly black Sun. For a few minutes, it's dark enough to see stars in the daytime sky."

C — "The last sliver of sunlight shines through a valley on the Moon's edge, making the Sun look like a glittering diamond on a ring — right before the sky goes dark."

D — "The Moon starts sliding in front of the Sun. Bit by bit, a growing bite disappears from one side of the Sun."

Type the four letters in the correct time order, like ABCD.

Hint 1

Which caption describes the Moon just starting to cover the Sun? That one has to come first.

Hint 2

The "diamond ring" only shows up here once, and the caption says it happens "right before the sky goes dark" — so it comes right before the darkest stage, not after it.

💡 Did you know? The Sun's corona — the glowing ring you can only see during totality — can reach over 1 million °C, way hotter than the Sun's visible surface, which is "only" about 5,500 °C!

MISSION 06
HARD

Four Astronomers, Four Towns

Four of Professor Bos's astronomer friends — Anke, Bram, Chiara, and Dexter — each watched the 2017 eclipse from a different town on the path: Salem, Casper, Carbondale, and Charleston. Each one brought a different instrument: a telescope, a solar-filter camera, a radio antenna, or a weather balloon.

Use these six clues to work out who was where, and with what:

1. The astronomer in Casper did not bring the telescope or the camera.
2. Chiara was not in Casper or in Charleston.
3. The astronomer with the radio antenna was in the only one of the four towns that touches the Atlantic Ocean.
4. Dexter's instrument listens to radio waves, not light.
5. Anke brought the telescope.
6. The camera traveled with the astronomer in Carbondale.

Which astronomer brought the radio antenna?

Hint 1

Start with clue 6 — it tells you exactly which town has the camera. Then use clue 1 to figure out which town is left for the telescope.

Hint 2

Clue 3 tells you which town has the antenna. Once you know that, clue 4 tells you which astronomer is there.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Coded Postscript

A torn page has a place name written in a Caesar cipher — every letter shifted forward by 5 places in the alphabet (A becomes F, B becomes G, and so on). In the margin, Professor Bos scribbled a note to whoever finds this: "I always use 5 for these — remember that number, Agent. It comes in handy again."

Decode this word: XTGWFQ

Hint 1

To undo a "shift forward by 5" cipher, shift each letter backward by 5 instead. X shifted back 5 places is S.

Hint 2

Write the alphabet out on paper and count backward 5 letters for each coded letter. If you go past A, wrap around to Z.

MISSION 08
HARD

Two Claims About 1919

Two old newspaper clippings are pasted into the notebook, both about a total solar eclipse on May 29, 1919, watched by scientists on the island of Príncipe (off the west coast of Africa) and in Sobral, Brazil. Only one clipping tells the truth:

Claim 1: "The expedition proved that starlight bends as it passes near the Sun's gravity — confirming a famous new theory about the universe."

Claim 2: "The expedition proved that the Sun is much smaller and closer to Earth than scientists had believed."

Search the internet to find out which claim is true — and which famous scientist's theory it confirmed. Type that scientist's last name.

Hint 1

Try searching "1919 solar eclipse expedition results". An astronomer named Arthur Eddington led one of the two expeditions, to test a brand-new theory by a famous physicist.

Hint 2

The scientists were measuring exactly how much starlight bent as it passed close to the Sun — search for whose prediction that measurement matched.

💡 Did you know? The tiny beads of sunlight around the Moon's edge just before totality are called "Baily's beads" — named after Francis Baily, the British astronomer who first explained them back in 1836.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

Back to Base Camp

Stage 1 of 2

Professor Bos's note: "The shadow doesn't stop at one state, Agent — it just keeps going." Search the internet to find out: how many different U.S. states did the 2017 path of totality cross through, coast to coast? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Try searching "2017 total solar eclipse how many states" — or look at an official path map and count every state the dark stripe touches, including the small corners.

Stage 2 of 2

Recall the number Professor Bos told you to remember from her coded postscript earlier in your case file. Add that number to the number of states from Stage 1. Use the total as a new Caesar shift — forward this time — to decode her final campsite name:

MHMTEBMR

Hint 1

Add the two numbers together first: the number of states from Stage 1, plus the number Professor Bos said to always remember. That total is your shift.

Hint 2

Shift each letter of MHMTEBMR backward by your total shift number. M shifted back 19 places is T.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Trunk's Final Lock

You've reached Professor Bos's locked trunk. Taped to the lid is her very last note:

"Eclipse chasers like me spend our lives hunting the umbra — the small, dark patch of true totality that the Moon's shadow drags across the Earth. This trunk guards the last thing I want to hand you. The lock has three dials, each showing a different digit from 1 to 9. Arrange your three digits from biggest to smallest to read the code. Added together, all three digits make exactly 23."

Hint 1

The three biggest different digits you could possibly pick are 9, 8, and 7 — but added together, those make 24. That's one too many.

Hint 2

Keep 9 and 8. What's the one digit — different from both of those — that you'd need to add to land on exactly 23?

Case closed?

The trunk clicks open. Inside is a spare pair of eclipse glasses and a note: "Eclipse chasers cross oceans and wait years for a few minutes of darkness, Agent, and we never stop trying, rain or shine. That's perseverance — and you've just proven you've got it too." Case closed — pack your bags. The next eclipse is waiting.
