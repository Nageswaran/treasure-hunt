Internet Detective Academy · Case File

Master Corvo's Trunk

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 70-90 minutes
Once a year, on Founder's Day, the puppeteers of the old touring theatre unlock Master Corvo's antique trunk — a tradition started over a century ago. Inside is this year's set of trials, and whoever solves them all gets to choose tonight's role in the show. This year, you're the guest apprentice who gets to try. Open the trunk, and let's see what Master Corvo left behind.
MISSION 01
EASY

Sorting the Puppets

A yellowed card is taped inside the trunk's lid, in Master Corvo's handwriting:

"If a puppet has red string, it is a Villain puppet. If a puppet is a Villain puppet, it is kept in the LEFT drawer."

Today's puppet on the workbench has red string. Which drawer is it kept in — LEFT or RIGHT?

Hint 1

Follow the chain one step at a time: red string tells you what KIND of puppet it is. Then the second sentence tells you where that kind is kept.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Faded Ticket Stub

Tucked in the trunk is an old ticket stub from a real marionette theatre in Austria, founded by a sculptor named Anton Aicher. It's one of the most famous puppet theatres in the world, and it's still open today.

Search the internet to find the year this theatre first opened its curtain. Type the year.

Hint 1

Try searching "Salzburg Marionette Theatre founded" — Anton Aicher opened it in a city famous for Mozart.

💡 Did you know? The Salzburg Marionette Theatre's very first show, in 1913, was a Mozart opera called "Bastien and Bastienne" — performed entirely by puppets.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Dot-Pattern Note

Master Corvo used her own version of an old code called a Baconian cipher — every letter is written as five A's and B's. She made sure every letter got its own unique pattern (no sharing, unlike some old versions). Here's the part of her key you'll need:

LetterEFIV
CodeAABAAAABABABAAABABAB

Decode this note, tucked under the trunk's tray:

AABAB  ABAAA  BABAB  AABAA

It tells you how many acts are in tonight's show. Type the number as a word.

Hint 1

Match each 5-letter group to the key table above, one group at a time.

Hint 2

The first group, AABAB, is the letter F. What common number-word starts with F?

📓 Agent's Notebook: Tonight's show has five acts. Worth remembering...
MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Heritage Certificate

A framed certificate hangs backstage: the puppetry traditions of Slovakia and Czechia were added to a very special worldwide list — UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Search the internet to find the exact year this happened. Type the year.

Hint 1

Try searching "Puppetry Slovakia Czechia UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage."

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Stage Valance

The fabric valance above the puppet stage is shaped like a parallelogram — a slanted rectangle. The seamstress needs its total area to order fabric. Careful: one of the measurements below is a distraction!

Find the total area of the valance in square centimeters.
Hint 1

A parallelogram's area uses its BASE and its straight-up-and-down HEIGHT — not the slanted side.

Hint 2

50 × 20 = ?

MISSION 06
HARD

Two Programme Notes

Backstage law, written in the troupe's oldest rulebook: "Only the FIRST show of any season is ever held on the museum's anniversary — never the fourth, never any other."

Two old programme notes both claim to describe the same night:

Note 1: "Opening Night fell on the museum's 100th anniversary, a Friday, and it was the FOURTH show of the season."
Note 2: "Opening Night fell on the museum's 100th anniversary, a Friday, and it was the FIRST show of the season."

They can't both be right. Using the backstage law above, which note is accurate — Note 1 or Note 2? Type "note 1" or "note 2".

Hint 1

Re-read the backstage law carefully. One of the two notes directly breaks that law.

Hint 2

The law says the anniversary is ONLY ever the first show — so any note claiming a different show number on the anniversary must be the wrong one.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Candle Game

Backstage tradition: before opening night, two puppeteers play a game with 21 candle stubs in a row. Taking turns, each player removes 1, 2, or 3 candles. Whoever takes the very LAST candle wins the right to open the show.

Master Corvo always lets the newest apprentice go first. If both players play perfectly, how many candles should YOU take on your very first turn to GUARANTEE a win?

Hint 1

Think in groups of 4. If you can always make sure that the total taken in each "round" (your turn plus your opponent's turn) equals 4, you control the game.

Hint 2

21 is 1 more than a multiple of 4 (20 = 5 × 4). Take just enough to leave a multiple of 4 for your opponent.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Box Office Ledger

Stage 1 of 2

Master Corvo's old programme is written partly in Dutch. It says this week has "acht voorstellingen" — Dutch for a number of shows. What number does the Dutch word "acht" mean? Type the number.

Hint 1

In Dutch, "twee" is two and "zes" is six. "Acht" is a bit more than "zes."

Stage 2 of 2

Grab a pencil for this one. Here is the box office ledger for this week's shows. The Saturday evening show sold exactly as many MORE tickets than Friday evening as the Dutch number you just found in Stage 1 — so Saturday evening's ticket count is Friday evening's count plus that number.

ShowTicketsPrice each (€)
Friday evening426
Saturday matinee555
Saturday evening42 + Stage 1 number7
Sunday matinee385

Multiply each row (tickets × price), then add all four totals together. What was the total box office revenue for the week, in euros?

Hint 1

First work out the Saturday evening ticket count using your Stage 1 answer. Then multiply each row and write down all four answers before adding.

Hint 2

Friday evening, Saturday matinee, and Sunday matinee give you 252, 275, and 190. Work out Saturday evening's total and add all four together.

💡 Did you know? Marionette puppets are traditionally controlled by strings tied to a wooden crossbar — puppeteers can spend years learning to make a puppet walk, bow, or dance convincingly.

MISSION 09
BIG AHA!

The Opera on the Stub

Remember that ticket stub from Mission 2? Master Corvo's notes say the Salzburg Marionette Theatre's very FIRST-EVER performance, back when it opened, was a famous opera called "Bastien and Bastienne" — performed entirely by puppets.

Search the internet to find which world-famous composer wrote that opera. Type the composer's last name.

Hint 1

Search "Bastien and Bastienne composer" — he was a child prodigy from Salzburg himself.

Hint 2

His full name is often shortened to just two names — his first name means "beloved" in several languages.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Latch

Master Corvo's trunk has one final latch, marked with a blank number dial. Take the number of acts in tonight's show — the one you were told to remember, right at the start — and add the winning first move from the candle game.

Turn the dial to that number to open the last latch and claim tonight's role.

Hint 1

The acts number came from the dot-pattern note. The candle-game number came from the game where you go first.

Hint 2

Five acts, plus the one candle you should take first — add them together.

Case closed?

The last latch clicks open. Inside is a single carved puppet, unfinished, with a note in Master Corvo's hand: "Finish it however you like — that's the whole point." Tonight, for the first time in a hundred years, the Founder's Day role goes to someone who solved it with a bit of their own creativity, not just by following the old rules.
