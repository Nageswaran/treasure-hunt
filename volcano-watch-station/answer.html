Mission 01 · Easy

Four Rocks on the Windowsill

Answer: Limestone
How to get there

Search "is basalt igneous", "is andesite igneous", "is obsidian igneous", and "is limestone igneous or sedimentary." Basalt, andesite, and obsidian all form when lava or magma cools — they're true volcanic (igneous) rocks. Limestone forms very differently: it's a sedimentary rock, built up over long stretches of time from shells, coral, and other ocean sediment, or from minerals settling out of water. It has nothing to do with volcanoes.

Why this is the only answer

Basalt, andesite, and obsidian are textbook igneous/volcanic rocks with no ambiguity in any geology source. Limestone is just as clearly sedimentary — there is no reasonable reading where it counts as volcanic.

Source / fact used

Verified via geology.com, the U.S. National Park Service geology pages, and standard rock-classification sources: basalt, andesite, and obsidian are extrusive igneous rocks; limestone is a sedimentary rock composed mainly of calcium carbonate.

Mission 08 · Hard · Multi-Stage

Two Eruptions, One Viral Post

Stage 1 answer: 259

The 1669 eruption (March–July 1669) reached and partly buried the outskirts of Catania — Etna's most destructive historical eruption. The November 1928 eruption destroyed the village of Mascali on Etna's eastern flank. 1928 − 1669 = 259 years.

Stage 2 (final) answer: smaller

The viral post's claim plugs in the child's Stage 1 number (259) as if Etna only erupts "once every 259 years." In reality, INGV (the official Italian institute that monitors Etna) and the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program both document that Etna is one of the most active volcanoes on Earth: at least 186 recorded eruptions since roughly 1500 BC (an average of about once every 19 years over that very long span), and in recent decades often several eruptive episodes per year — sometimes a dozen or more in a single year. Either way you measure it, the real gap between eruptions is dramatically smaller than 259 years, so the correct answer is "smaller."

Why this is the only answer

Stage 2 cannot be answered without Stage 1's exact number, since the viral post's claim is written in terms of "the number you just calculated" — a different Stage 1 result would describe a different (though still false, in this case) claim. With 259 specifically, real eruption records leave no doubt the true frequency is smaller, not bigger or equal.

Source / fact used

1669 eruption: Wikipedia "1669 eruption of Mount Etna" and Britannica. 1928 Mascali eruption: University of Edinburgh Research Explorer and multiple peer-reviewed sources on the 1928 eruption. Eruption frequency: INGV (ct.ingv.it, Italy's Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia, confirmed as the real official monitoring institute) and the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program (186 eruptions since ~1500 BC; frequent multi-eruption years in recent decades, e.g. 11 eruptions in three weeks during 2021).
