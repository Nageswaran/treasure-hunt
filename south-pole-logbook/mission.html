Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The South Pole Logbook

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60-90 minutes
A maritime museum just found a sealed metal tube wedged inside an old ship's storage crate. Inside: a torn logbook page from a polar expedition in 1911, the ink barely holding on. The Internet Detective Academy has been called in to piece together exactly what happened out on the ice, clue by clue, before the page crumbles for good. Crack all ten entries, Agent, and the logbook will reveal the one quality that got the explorers through it alive.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Empty Place on the Map

In December 1911, a small team of explorers stood somewhere on Earth that no human had ever reached before. To mark the moment, they planted a flag and named the icy, flat land around them after their king.

Search to find which country's flag they planted. Type just the country's name.

Hint 1

Try searching "first explorers to reach the South Pole 1911." One name comes up again and again — search where he was from.

💡 Did you know? A rival British team, led by Robert Falcon Scott, reached the South Pole too — but not until January 17, 1912, just over a month after the first team got there.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Sledging Log

The logbook's next page is a table of daily distances the sledges covered, in kilometers:

Day 1: 15   Day 2: 19   Day 3: 23   Day 4: 27   Day 5: ?

Each day's distance grows by the exact same amount. How far did they travel on Day 5?

Hint 1

Look at how much the distance grows from one day to the next. Is it always the same jump?

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Dutch Curator's Sticky Note

The museum's Dutch curator left a note listing expedition words. Three of these words use "de", and one uses "het" instead.

slee   vlag   tent   kompas

(slee = sled, vlag = flag, tent = tent, kompas = compass)

Find the odd one out: which word takes "het" instead of "de"?

Hint 1

Try each word in a sentence: "de ___ ligt op tafel" or "het ___ ligt op tafel." One will sound wrong for the other three.

Hint 2

Three of these words are things you'd carry or pitch. One is an instrument you'd read — that one often takes "het."

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Hut With a Missing Wall

The expedition's base hut had an L-shaped floor. A builder's plan shows five of its six walls, and the note beside it says the total distance all the way around the hut is exactly 50 meters. One wall's length is missing — marked with a "?".

Grab a pencil: add up the five walls you know, then use the total distance to find the missing one.

How many meters long is the missing wall?

Hint 1

Add together the five labeled walls first. Then think about what's left over from the total of 50 meters.

Hint 2

11 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 16 = 41 meters. The full trip around is 50 meters. What's the difference?

📓 Agent's Notebook: The missing wall measured this many meters. Something tells you this number will matter again before the case is closed.

💡 Did you know? The coldest temperature ever directly measured on Earth's surface is -89.2°C, recorded at Antarctica's Vostok Station in 1983 — cold enough to freeze exhaled breath into a crackling fog.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Bird That Loves Winter

Most animals near the South Pole flee before the brutal Antarctic winter sets in. One bird does the opposite — it stays, and a single egg is balanced on its feet through the very worst of the cold and wind.

Search to find which penguin species does this. Type your answer as two words: the size or rank word, then "penguin" (for example, a made-up guess like giant penguin — but that's not the real one).

Hint 1

Try searching "only penguin that breeds in Antarctic winter." It's also the tallest and heaviest penguin species alive.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Signal Lamp

Ships in this era often signaled to each other across open water using a flashing lamp, blinking out letters in Morse code. The logbook says one word was flashed over and over, to any ship close enough to see it. Use the key to decode it.

R = ·–·
M = ––
F = ··–·
A = ·–
··–·   ·–·   ·–   ––

Match each group of dots and dashes to its letter in the key, in order, to read the flashed word. (It's the name the expedition gave their own ship.)

Hint 1

Match the first group to the key first: it's four symbols long, dot-dot-dash-dot. Only one letter in the key fits that.

Hint 2

The four letters, once matched in order, spell a short Norwegian word meaning "forward."

💡 Did you know? The expedition brought around 97 Greenland dogs, a breed built for pulling heavy sledges through Arctic and Antarctic cold for centuries.

MISSION 07
HARD

Three Navigators, Three Readings

Three navigators — Erling, Torvald, and Sigrid — each used a different instrument that freezing morning (a sextant, a chronometer, or a theodolite), and each recorded a different temperature (-28°C, -34°C, or -41°C). Use the four clues to work out which instrument Sigrid used.

  1. The person using the chronometer recorded a warmer temperature than Torvald.
  2. Sigrid did not use the sextant.
  3. Erling recorded -41°C.
  4. The theodolite user recorded the coldest temperature of the three.

Which instrument did Sigrid use?

Hint 1

Start with clues 3 and 4 together. If Erling recorded the coldest reading, and the theodolite user recorded the coldest reading, what does that tell you about Erling?

Hint 2

Once you know Erling used the theodolite, clue 2 leaves Sigrid only one instrument to choose from.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Depot Ledger

Stage 1 of 2

Search to find the exact date the expedition reached the South Pole (the same event from Mission 1). Type just the day-of-the-month number (a number from 1 to 31).

Hint 1

Try searching "date Amundsen reached the South Pole 1911." It happened in December.

Stage 2 of 2

Grab a pencil. Two supply depots sell paraffin fuel for the return journey:

  • Depot Alpha: charges, per liter, as many kroner as the day-of-month number from Stage 1.
  • Depot Beta: charges a flat total price for a batch of 10 liters — that total is your notebook number (the hut's missing wall, from Mission 4) multiplied by 20 kroner.

Work out the price per liter at each depot. How many kroner per liter cheaper is the less expensive depot?

Hint 1

Depot Alpha's price per liter is just the Stage 1 number. For Depot Beta, first find the total price for 10 liters, then divide by 10 to get the price for one liter.

Hint 2

Notebook number × 20 = Depot Beta's total for 10 liters. Divide that by 10. Then compare both depots' per-liter prices.

MISSION 09
BIG AHA!

Harnessing the Team

Four sled dogs need to be harnessed in a single-file line for tomorrow's push: Lucky, Frost, Storm, and Compass. Only Lucky can be harnessed as the lead dog — she's the only one who reliably obeys voice commands in a whiteout. The other three dogs (Frost, Storm, and Compass) can be arranged in any order for the remaining three positions in the line.

How many different valid sled line-ups are possible in total?

Hint 1

Lucky's spot is already fixed, so only think about arranging the other three dogs in the three spots behind her.

Hint 2

For the first spot behind Lucky, you have 3 choices of dog. For the next spot, only 2 dogs are left to choose from. For the last spot, only 1 is left. Multiply those choices together.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Entry

The logbook's final page is torn and water-stained, but seven short lines are still readable. Look only at the very first letter of each line, top to bottom, and write those letters together to spell one word.

  1. Cold bit through every layer of wool and fur that final morning.
  2. Onward the last few sledges pushed, lighter than ever before.
  3. Under a flag that snapped hard in the wind, they finally stopped.
  4. Reaching this point had cost many long weeks of frozen marching.
  5. A small tent, one flag, and a single victorious photograph — that was all.
  6. Going home again would prove just as dangerous as arriving.
  7. Every explorer who reads this page should remember what it took.
Hint 1

Ignore every word except the very first letter of each of the seven lines.

Hint 2

Write the seven letters down in order, top to bottom, then read them together as one word.

Case closed?

Flags, frozen walls, signal lamps, and one hidden word — you rebuilt the whole expedition from a single torn page. That's real courage, Agent: the same thing it took to keep walking when the ice never seemed to end.
