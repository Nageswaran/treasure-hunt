Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Case of the Missing Trophy Tulip

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 55-70 minutes
Every year, growers from all over the Netherlands bring their finest new tulips to Royal FloraHolland in Aalsmeer — the biggest flower auction on Earth — hoping to win the Golden Trophy Tulip. But an hour before today's winner is announced, the trophy crate has vanished from its usual spot, and nobody can find the grower who was supposed to be standing beside it. You've been called in to follow the trail through the auction halls before the winner's bell rings.
MISSION 01
EASY

Past the Gate

Before the guard at the gate lets you in, she wants to know you understand just how enormous this place really is. Search online: how many football fields could fit inside the whole Royal FloraHolland auction building in Aalsmeer? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Try searching "Royal FloraHolland Aalsmeer football fields size" and look for the number on a couple of trustworthy travel or news sites.

💡 Did you know? Tulips didn't start out in the Netherlands at all. They first grew wild high in the mountains of Central Asia, and only reached Dutch gardens a few hundred years ago.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Grower's Ledger

You find grower Els Bakker's planting ledger open on a crate. It shows how many tulip bulbs she planted each year: Year 1: 3, Year 2: 6, Year 3: 12, Year 4: 24. If the pattern continues exactly, how many bulbs did she plant in Year 5?

Hint 1

Look at what happens to each year's number to turn it into the next year's number — it's the same simple move every time.

🗒️ Agent's Notebook: Clipped to the crate right behind the ledger is a shipping tag, stamped LOT K-247. You don't know why yet, but it feels like something worth remembering.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Sorting the Crates

The crates in the loading bay are labeled with Dutch words: tulp, bloembol, veiling, geld. In Dutch, most nouns use the word "de" in front of them. But one special word on this list uses "het" instead. Which word is the odd one out?

Hint 1

Try saying "de" in front of each word out loud, one at a time — three of them will sound completely normal.

Hint 2

The odd word out isn't about flowers at all — it's about what the winning grower takes home along with the trophy.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Race the Clock

Grab a pencil for this one. At Aalsmeer, the auction clock doesn't count UP like a normal auction — it counts DOWN, and the first buyer to slam the button wins the flowers at whatever price is showing.

A cart of prize roses starts at 80 cents per stem. Every few seconds, the price drops by 5 cents. A florist finally stops the clock on the 9th price shown (so the price has already dropped 8 times). What is the final price, in cents, per stem?

Hint 1

The 1st price shown is 80 cents, with zero drops yet. The 2nd price shown is after 1 drop. So how many drops have happened by the 9th price shown?

Hint 2

Multiply the number of drops by 5 cents, then subtract that from 80.

💡 Did you know? In the 1630s, one rare Dutch tulip variety called the Semper Augustus was so prized that a single bulb sold for about as much as a fine house in Amsterdam. People still call that wild time "Tulip Mania."

MISSION 05
HARD

The Greenhouse Floor Plan

The trophy was last seen in Greenhouse 6, which is L-shaped because of a loading-dock cut-out in one corner. Using the measurements marked on the floor plan, find the total floor area in square meters.

Try splitting the L-shape into two ordinary rectangles before you add anything up.
Hint 1

Draw one straight line to turn the L-shape into two plain rectangles.

Hint 2

One rectangle is 14 m × 14 m. The other is 6 m × 9 m. Find each area, then add them together.

MISSION 06
HARD

Four Growers, Four Colors

Four growers each brought exactly one tulip color to today's auction: Jansen, De Vries, Bakker, and Visser grew Red, Yellow, Purple, and White tulips — one color per grower, but the auction sheet got smudged. Use these clues to work out who grew what:

1. The Red tulip came from Jansen.

2. Visser's tulip is not Purple and not Yellow.

3. The Yellow tulip did not come from Jansen or Bakker.

4. De Vries's tulip is not White.

This year's Golden Trophy always goes to the finest Purple bloom. Which grower's tulip is the Purple one — the trophy winner?

Hint 1

Make a table: growers across the top, colors down the side. Clue 1 gives you one match immediately — cross that box off first.

Hint 2

Once Jansen's color is settled, clue 2 tells you Visser's color must be White. That leaves only Yellow and Purple for De Vries and Bakker — clue 3 picks between them.

MISSION 07
BIG AHA

The Crate Code

Stage 1 of 2

Bakker's trophy crate is missing from Greenhouse 6 — but you remember a shipping tag you spotted earlier, stamped with a lot code. Take the number from that code, and subtract the number of football fields you found could fit inside the whole building (your Mission 1 answer). What number do you get?

Hint 1

Flip back through your notes — what number was stamped on that crate tag in Mission 2? What was your Mission 1 answer?

Stage 2 of 2

That number is too big to be a real aisle number here — aisles only go up to 40. Take your Stage 1 answer, subtract 7, then divide what's left by 2. Which aisle number do you get?

Hint 1

Subtract 7 from your Stage 1 answer first. Then divide whatever is left by 2.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

What Was Really Written on the Crate

In the aisle you found, Bakker's trophy crate sits safe and sound — it was only ever mislabeled in the rush of auction morning. Wedged under the lid is a strip of paper with numbers on it. Use A=1, B=2, C=3, all the way to Z=26, to turn each number into a letter and spell out one word.

20 - 5 - 1 - 13 - 23 - 15 - 18 - 11

Hint 1

Write the alphabet out with a number under each letter, A=1 through Z=26, then match each number to its letter.

Hint 2

The first number, 20, is the letter T. The word has 8 letters in total.

Case closed?

The trophy was never stolen at all — just mislabeled and rushed to the wrong aisle in the morning chaos. It took a ledger, a Dutch word, a race against the clock, a floor plan, and a crate code to bring it back — no single clue solved it alone. That's TEAMWORK. Well done, Agent — the Golden Trophy Tulip makes it to the winner's spotlight just in time.
