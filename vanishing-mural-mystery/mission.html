Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Vanishing Mural

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75–100 minutes

Tomorrow at 10 AM, Riverside Park Community Center unveils its brand-new mural, "Roots & Wings," painted by artist Anke Bloem. But tonight, committee chair Mr. Devries found a mess: the finished wall doesn't match the approved sketch. Colors are swapped. A key symbol is missing. And nobody can agree who painted what, or when.

Anke is on a ferry with no phone signal until morning. The ribbon-cutting can't wait. Mr. Devries has called in the Internet Detective Academy's newest recruit — you — to figure out what went wrong before the crowd arrives.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Sketch on the Corkboard

You arrive at the community center the evening before the big day. Pinned to the corkboard is Anke's approved sketch: a tree with roots reaching into a river, its branches turning into wings.

Next to it, Mr. Devries has pinned a note: "Anke told me she studied one famous mural before designing this one — a giant painted wall by a Mexican artist, showing factory workers, inside a real museum you can actually visit today."

Search online to find out: in which U.S. city can you visit that real, famous mural right now?

Agent's Notebook: Clipped to the corner of the corkboard is a laminated volunteer badge: "Access Badge No. 30." Something about it feels important. Jot that number down — you might need it before this case closes.
Hint 1

Try searching "Diego Rivera factory mural museum" — one city name will show up in almost every result.

💡 Did you know? That real mural has 27 separate painted panels, and the artist, Diego Rivera, called it his greatest work.

MISSION 02
EASY

De Kwast, Het Doek

Anke grew up in the Netherlands, and she labeled the volunteer supply closet in Dutch. Every Dutch word needs either "de" or "het" in front of it — but the labels fell off and got mixed up.

Here are five supply words: kwast (brush), emmer (bucket), ladder (ladder), muur (wall), doek (canvas).

Four of these words use the same article. One doesn't belong. Search "[word] de of het" for each one, then type the ONE word that uses a different article than the other four.

Hint 1

Search each word one at a time, like "kwast de of het" — most of these five words will give you the same article.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Wall That Doesn't Add Up

Before you can sort out the colors, the paint order form needs the exact size of the mural wall. Grab a pencil for this one — it has a few steps.

The wall is shaped like a big rectangle, but the bottom-right corner is missing: that part is a utility box that doesn't get painted.

Use every labeled measurement to find the wall's paintable area.

Find the total area that actually gets painted, in square meters.

Hint 1

Split the L-shape into two rectangles and add their areas — or find the area of the whole big rectangle and subtract the utility box.

Hint 2

The big outer rectangle is 15 m by 9 m. The missing utility box is 4 m by 3 m.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Color Wheel Rule

Quick lesson: Artists arrange colors in a circle called a color wheel. Colors sitting directly opposite each other are called complementary colors. Look at the wheel below: red sits opposite green, blue sits opposite orange, and yellow sits opposite purple.

Volunteer Sam left a note: "Oops — I mixed up the river ribbon's paint. I grabbed the color exactly OPPOSITE the correct one on the wheel by mistake."

A photo of the finished wall shows the river ribbon painted orange. Using the rule above, what color was the river ribbon actually supposed to be?

Hint 1

Find orange's wedge on the wheel above, then look at the wedge directly across the circle from it.

Hint 2

Opposite wedges are exactly halfway around the circle — count three wedges from orange in either direction.

💡 Did you know? The very first color wheel was made by Isaac Newton, who arranged rainbow colors into a circle back in the 1660s.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Urgent Message

The treasurer shows you a text message she got tonight, supposedly from Anke:

"URGENT!! Hi mural comittee, this is Anke!! My card got decline at the paint store!! Please send $500 in Amazon gift cards RIGHT NOW before 6pm or the mural wont be ready for tommorow!! Reply fast, dont call, my phone is broken. – Anke B."

Real scam experts watch for warning signs like these: rushing you, asking for an unusual payment method, spelling mistakes, and discouraging you from double-checking.

Search "FTC gift card scams" and open the official page. According to the FTC, no legitimate business or government agency will ever ask you to pay using what? (Two words.)

Hint 1

Search exactly: FTC gift card scams — look for the official page at consumer.ftc.gov.

Hint 2

The answer is the same unusual payment method mentioned in the message above.

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Cans, One Clue

Three mislabeled paint cans turned up in the wrong supply box. The label maker broke, so someone scratched a binary code into each lid instead of writing the section number.

Can X: 0 1 1 1 1
Can Y: 1 0 1 1 0
Can Z: 1 1 0 0 1

A separate note reads: "The mystery can's section number, once you convert it from binary, is greater than 20 — but it is NOT a multiple of 5."

Convert all three binary codes to decimal numbers, then use the note to find the mystery can. Type just that can's section number.

Hint 1

Binary place values from left to right are 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Add up the place values wherever you see a 1.

Hint 2

Two of the three numbers are bigger than 20. Only one of those two is NOT a multiple of 5 (like 5, 10, 15, 20, 25).

💡 Did you know? Diego Rivera painted another mural at Rockefeller Center in New York — but it was destroyed in 1934 because he refused to remove a painted portrait of Lenin.

MISSION 07
HARD

Two Paint Suppliers, One Decision

The committee needs 8 liters of white primer for touch-ups before tomorrow. Two suppliers can deliver tonight:

TrueBrand Paints: €12 per liter, plus a flat €25 delivery fee, no matter the order size.
ColorWorks Supply: €14 per liter. Delivery is FREE on orders over €90 — otherwise it's a €10 delivery fee.

Work out the total cost of 8 liters from each supplier, delivery included. Type the total cost, in euros, of whichever supplier turns out to be cheaper. (Numbers only, no € sign.)

Hint 1

Work out each supplier's total separately: liters × price per liter, then add the delivery fee.

Hint 2

Before adding ColorWorks Supply's delivery fee, check whether the order total already passes their €90 free-delivery threshold.

MISSION 08
HARD

Who Painted the River?

The volunteer sign-in sheet is a mess, but three volunteers — Mia, Noah, and Zara — each painted one of three sections: Leaves, River, and Sun. Nobody remembers who painted what. You find three clues:

1. Noah did not paint the River section.
2. The Sun section was painted by someone who signed in earlier that morning than Noah did.
3. According to the approved color key, the River section is blue — and Zara refuses to work with blue paint, so she didn't paint it.

Using only these three clues, figure out which volunteer actually painted the River section.

Hint 1

Start with clue 2 — think carefully about what it tells you about who could have painted Sun.

Hint 2

If Noah didn't paint River (clue 1) and couldn't have painted Sun (clue 2), there's only one section left for him.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

Fixing the River, Stage by Stage

Stage 1 of 2

Now you know the river ribbon needs to be completely repainted. It's a rectangle 12 meters long and 1.5 meters wide.

The paint can label says: "1 liter covers 6 square meters with one coat." The plan calls for 2 coats, for full color.

How many liters of paint are needed in total?

Hint 1

First find the river ribbon's area (length × width). Then work out how many liters ONE coat needs before doubling it for two coats.

Stage 2 of 2 (final)

Paint only comes in 2.5-liter cans, costing €19 each — you can't buy part of a can.

Using the number of liters you found in Stage 1, how many EUROS will the team spend on river-repair paint?

Hint 1

Divide the liters from Stage 1 by 2.5 — if it isn't a whole number, round UP to the next whole can.

Hint 2

Once you know how many whole cans are needed, multiply that by €19.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Envelope Behind the Frame

Taped to the back of the mural's frame, you find a sealed envelope from Anke. Inside is a coded word: KZMIBQDQBG, with a note.

The note says: "Take the badge number you noticed on your very first evening here. Subtract the section number you found on that mystery paint can. That difference is your shift number."

Shift every letter of KZMIBQDQBG BACKWARD through the alphabet by that many places (wrapping from A back around to Z) to reveal the one word that best describes how you cracked this whole case.

Type that word.

Hint 1

Badge number minus mystery-can number gives you a small shift — count backward that many letters for each character.

Hint 2

Write out the alphabet A to Z on paper, then count backward from each coded letter by your shift number.

Case closed?

The word is CREATIVITY. You cracked this case by looking at things sideways — art, geometry, a coded paint can, and one lucky Dutch word. Mr. Devries hangs your report right next to the mural, and the missing dove takes flight on the wall just in time for tomorrow's ribbon-cutting.
