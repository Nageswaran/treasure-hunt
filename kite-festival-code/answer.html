Mission 02 · Easy · Science & Nature / Logical Reasoning (Foundational: classification)

The Wind Gauge Log

Answer: 2
How to get there

A kite needs roughly 9-13 km/h (5-7 knots) of wind just to lift off the ground at all; recreational single-line kites fly best from about 13-24 km/h. Of the four logged readings — 3, 6, 15, and 22 km/h — only 15 and 22 km/h clear even the top of that minimum-launch range, so 2 readings were strong enough.

Why this is the only answer

The four readings were deliberately spread so the count doesn't change no matter which exact number (9 or 13 km/h) the child finds cited as "minimum": 3 and 6 km/h fail under any cited minimum, while 15 and 22 km/h clear all of them. The count is robust to normal source variation.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: sources (surfertoday.com, windup.live, cskites.com) agree kites can launch around 5-7 knots (~9-13 km/h) minimum, with single-line kites flying best around 13-24 km/h.

Mission 03 · Medium · Mathematics (geometry/measurement)

The Spare Sail Panel

Answer: 3120
How to get there

For any quadrilateral whose two diagonals cross at a right angle (a kite/diamond shape), area = (diagonal 1 × diagonal 2) ÷ 2. Here: (96 × 65) ÷ 2 = 6240 ÷ 2 = 3120 square centimeters.

Why this is the only answer

The diagram's SVG coordinates were built directly from these two numbers (a 192px-scaled vertical diagonal and a 130px-scaled horizontal diagonal, at a 2px = 1cm scale) and independently re-verified with the shoelace formula: it returns exactly 12,480 px², which divided by the scale-squared (4) gives 3,120 cm² — matching the formula result exactly, so the picture and the math agree.

Source / fact used

Self-contained math puzzle (geometry formula for a quadrilateral with perpendicular diagonals); no external fact needed. Numbers and diagram cross-checked by script.

How to get there

De vlieger, de wind, het touw, het papier, de lucht, de staart. Only "touw" (string/rope) and "papier" (paper) are het-words; the other four are de-words. That is 2 het-words out of the six.

Mission 05 · Hard · Cryptography

The Judge's Coded Note

Answer: storage tent
How to get there

The ciphertext "WXSVEKI XIRX" was produced by shifting each letter of "STORAGE TENT" forward by 4 (a Caesar cipher, shift +4). Shifting each ciphertext letter back by 4 recovers: W→S, X→T, S→O, V→R, E→A, K→G, I→E (= STORAGE), and X→T, I→E, R→N, X→T (= TENT).

Why this is the only answer

A Caesar shift is fully reversible and deterministic — decoding by the stated shift amount (4, backward) produces exactly one plaintext, verified by encoding "storage tent" forward by 4 in a script and confirming it reproduces "WXSVEKI XIRX" exactly.

Source / fact used

Self-contained cipher puzzle; encoding double-checked programmatically (Caesar shift +4 of STORAGE TENT = WXSVEKI XIRX).

Mission 07 · Big "Aha" · Financial Literacy

The Bamboo Stall's Receipts

Answer: Golden Dragon
How to get there

Golden Dragon Line costs a flat ¥60. Bargain Breeze Threads costs ¥40 + a ¥15 rush fee = ¥55 total — technically still ¥5 cheaper. But a search on kite-line materials shows braided Dacron (Golden Dragon's material) is the standard for large, heavy-duty kites, while cotton-blend twine (Bargain Breeze's material) is common on cheap children's kites and is weaker. Bargain Breeze is rated for only 20 kg, far under the dragon kite's required 80 kg, so it is not safe to use no matter its price. Golden Dragon, rated for 80 kg, is the only supplier that actually meets the requirement.

Why this is the only answer

The "aha" is that the cheaper total price is a red herring: the real deciding factor is whether the line can hold the required weight at all, and only one supplier's product is rated high enough. This makes the choice a safety requirement, not a matter of opinion.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: multiple kite-supply sources (my-best-kite.com, allthingskites.com, emmakites.com) agree braided Dacron is stronger than cotton and is the standard material for large/heavy-duty kites, while cotton is typically sold with cheaper kids' kites.
