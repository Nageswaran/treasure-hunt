Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Vanishing Dragon Kite

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 70-90 minutes

It is early morning in Weifang, China — a city so famous for kites that the whole world calls it the "Kite Capital." Today is the final round of the International Kite Festival. But the giant dragon kite meant to open the finals has vanished from its display stand, leaving only an empty pedestal and a small brass number tag.

The Head Judge has called in one detective for the job: you. Follow every clue, and by the last mission you will not only find the dragon kite — you'll uncover the one quality that cracked the whole case.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Empty Pedestal

The judges' pavilion is buzzing with panic. Before you chase suspects, prove you know your case file. Here is a photo pinned to the case board of a famous building in the very city hosting this festival:

Look closely at the roof's shape and color — search those details to identify the building and the city it's in.

This city is so famous for kites that the whole world gave it a nickname. Search online: which Chinese city is nicknamed the "World Kite Capital," and in what year was it officially given that title (at the festival's 5th edition)? Type just the year, as four digits.

Agent's Notebook: The brass tag on the empty pedestal reads "27" — the missing dragon kite's competitor number. Jot that down. You may need it later.
Hint 1

Search for the roof's shape and its blue tile color to identify the building and its city. Then look for when that city's nickname became official — that's a different year from when the festival itself first began.

💡 Did you know? Kites were invented in China over 2,000 years ago. Around 200 BC, a general named Han Xin even flew one over an enemy city's wall — just to measure how far his soldiers would need to tunnel to get underneath it!

MISSION 02
EASY

The Wind Gauge Log

Could the dragon kite have simply blown off its stand in a strong gust? The groundskeeper's wind gauge logged four readings early this morning: 3 km/h, 6 km/h, 15 km/h, and 22 km/h.

Search online: about how many kilometers per hour of wind does a kite need at minimum just to lift off the ground (not to do tricks — just to rise at all)? Then figure out how many of the four logged readings were strong enough to lift a kite off its stand by itself. Type just the number.

Hint 1

Search for the wind speed a kite needs just to lift off, not the speed for fancy stunts. Then sort the four readings into "strong enough" and "too calm."

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Spare Sail Panel

In the workshop tent, you find a folded silk sail panel shaped like a diamond — the same shape as the missing dragon kite's own panels. Its maker labeled both cross-lines (called diagonals) with their exact lengths, shown in the diagram below.

Grab a pencil for this one: what is the area of this diamond-shaped panel, in square centimeters?

The two dashed lines are the diagonals. They cross at a perfect right angle.
Hint 1

For a diamond (kite) shape whose diagonals cross at a right angle, there's a formula that uses both diagonal lengths together — think about how a rectangle split by both diagonals relates to this shape.

Hint 2

Multiply the two diagonal lengths together, then divide the result by 2. Type just the number — no units.

💡 Did you know? Weifang, China calls itself the "World Kite Capital." More than 70% of all kites sold around the world are made there, and the city has hosted an International Kite Festival every year since 1984.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

HQ's Language Check-In

While you wait for the workshop's fingerprint results, Detective Academy HQ radios in a quick language drill — every agent must stay sharp in Dutch, no matter where in the world a case takes them.

In Dutch, every noun pairs with either "de" or "het." Here are six words connected to kites and wind: vlieger (kite), wind (wind), touw (string), papier (paper), lucht (sky/air), staart (tail).

How many of these six words use "het" instead of "de"? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Try each word in a short sentence, like "Ik zie ___ vlieger" (I see ___ kite), first with "de" and then with "het." Most will only sound right one way.

Hint 2

Two of the six often trip people up: the word for "string" and the word for "paper." Test both "de" and "het" in front of each one and listen for what sounds wrong.

MISSION 05
HARD

The Judge's Coded Note

Tucked inside the dragon kite's empty display case, you find a note in a code the judges use to protect competitors' secrets:

WXSVEKI XIRX

Every letter has been shifted forward by 4 places in the alphabet (A becomes E, B becomes F, and so on — spaces stay spaces). Shift each letter back by 4 to read the real message. Type your answer as two words.

Hint 1

Write out the alphabet on scratch paper. For each letter in the note, count backward 4 letters to find the real one.

Hint 2

The first word names a place where things are kept. The second word is a common name for a canvas shelter set up at a fair or festival.

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Stalls Around the Fountain

The coded note points you to the repair stalls near the courtyard fountain. Three stalls stand around it, each in a different compass direction: North, East, and South.

The fountain sits at the center of the courtyard.

Clues from your interviews:

1. The stall to the North sells bamboo repair tape.
2. Mr. Zhao's stall is not to the North.
3. Ms. Li's stall does not sell glue.
4. The stall that sells silk thread is to the East.
5. Mr. Zhao sells silk thread.

The repair ledger for today also shows: competitor #12 got a silk-thread patch, competitor #40 got a glue seal, and competitor #27 got bamboo tape.

Which compass direction is the stall that repaired the kite you're investigating today? Type just the direction (North, East, or South).

Hint 1

Logic grids: start with the clue that names a person AND what they sell directly — that gives you your first solid fact to build from.

Hint 2

Once you work out which direction sells bamboo tape, check the ledger to see which competitor number got that repair. Does that number look familiar from earlier in your case file?

💡 Did you know? The Weifang World Kite Museum is the largest kite museum on Earth. Its roof is shaped like a giant dragon-headed centipede kite and covered in shiny peacock-blue tiles!

MISSION 07
BIG "AHA"

The Bamboo Stall's Receipts

The bamboo-tape vendor's receipts show the team was choosing between two kite-line suppliers to restring the giant dragon kite, which needs a line rated to hold at least 80 kilograms of pull.

Golden Dragon Line: 500 meters for ¥60, delivery included. Woven from a strong material called braided Dacron, rated to hold 80 kg.

Bargain Breeze Threads: 500 meters for ¥40 — but with the festival only 2 days away, it needs a rush-delivery fee of ¥15 to arrive in time. Made from cotton-blend twine, rated to hold only 20 kg.

First, work out the total cost of each supplier once the rush fee is included. Then search online: is braided Dacron line or cotton-blend twine actually strong enough for big, heavy kites?

Which supplier should the team have chosen to fly the dragon kite safely? Type the supplier's two-word name.

Hint 1

Add up Bargain Breeze's price including its rush fee before you compare totals — the sticker price alone doesn't tell the whole story.

Hint 2

Once you've compared the totals, search for which material — braided Dacron or cotton-blend twine — is strong enough for a heavy dragon kite. That fact matters more than the few yuan difference in price.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Judges' Scoreboard

Case cracked: the weak cotton-blend line snapped under the wind, and the dragon kite drifted safely to a soft landing nearby, undamaged. Now the Head Judge hands you the scoreboard used to crown this year's Best Flyer — and challenges you to read it like a true agent.

Stage 1 of 2

The scoring rule: Score = (flight time in minutes × wind speed in km/h) ÷ number of dips.

Today's dragon kite flew for 10 minutes in a 12 km/h wind, and dipped 15 times before landing. What was its score? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Do the multiplication first (flight time × wind speed), then divide that result by the number of dips.

Stage 2 of 2 — Final Answer

The Head Judge keeps a numbered "Virtue Board" listing the eight qualities every great detective needs. Find the score from Stage 1 in the numbered list below, and type the matching word.

  1. curiosity
  2. courage
  3. discovery
  4. creativity
  5. teamwork
  6. kindness
  7. wisdom
  8. ???
Hint 1

Count down the numbered list to the number that matches your Stage 1 score.

Hint 2

Think about what quality kept you going through six earlier missions, even when a clue seemed impossible to crack. The Virtue Board's last word means "never giving up."

Case closed?

You never gave up — not on the coded note, not on the tricky logic grid, not on a single clue. That is exactly what the last word on the Virtue Board means. Case closed, Agent. The dragon kite flies again tomorrow, all because you never quit.
