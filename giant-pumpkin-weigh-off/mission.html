The Great Pumpkin Weigh-Off

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes

It's the final afternoon of the Amberfield County Fair. Five giant pumpkins are lined up for the championship Weigh-Off, and the whole fairgrounds is buzzing — results go up at 4:00 PM sharp. Just as the biggest pumpkin of the day rolls onto the scale, the display flickers, freezes on an error code, and dies. Nobody knows the true winner. Head Judge Pemberton needs someone sharp with numbers, maps, and records to verify the real champion the old-fashioned way — before the crowd runs out of patience. She hands you a badge: Junior Judging Assistant. The clock is running.

MISSION 01
EASY

Find the Judges' Tent

Badge pinned on, you step through the fair gates. Somewhere in this maze of tents is Head Judge Pemberton — and she needed you five minutes ago. Use the fairground map below to find her tent.

Start at the Ferris Wheel. The Judges' Tent is exactly 1 row SOUTH and 2 columns WEST of the Ferris Wheel. Which grid square is it in?

Rows are letters (top to bottom). Columns are numbers (left to right). Type your answer like this: A1 — row letter first, then column number, no space.
Hint 1

"South" means down the map. "West" means left. Move one direction at a time, starting from the Ferris Wheel's square.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Hall of Fame Wall

Judge Pemberton is buried in paperwork. Before she can brief you properly, she hands you a registration card to file and points you toward the "Hall of Fame" photo wall to wait.

Agent's Notebook: The card reads Hazel Kowalski — registration tag GPW-114. Jot that down, Agent. Fair paperwork has a way of getting jumbled later today.

Pinned next to the old photos is a trivia plaque about the very first pumpkin weigh-off ever held — back when the towns of Half Moon Bay, California, and Circleville, Ohio, argued over who really grew the biggest pumpkin. Search online to find the year of that very first official weigh-off in Half Moon Bay.

Hint 1

Try searching "Half Moon Bay pumpkin weigh-off first year" or "history of pumpkin weigh-offs."

💡 Did you know? During its fastest growing stretch in July, a giant pumpkin can gain up to 50 pounds in a single day — roughly two pounds every hour, day and night.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

What Makes a Pumpkin Go Giant?

Every giant pumpkin here started out as an ordinary-looking seed. Search online to find the name of the plant tissue that carries sugar from the leaves into the growing fruit. Giant pumpkins have extra amounts of this tissue — one big reason they can grow so enormous. (It's a science word, and it is NOT the same as "xylem," which is the tissue that carries water instead.)

Hint 1

Search something like "how do giant pumpkins get so big science." Look for the plant tissue that's the opposite of the one that carries water.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Roots of the Giants

Every Atlantic Giant pumpkin at this fair — the classic giant orange variety used in weigh-offs all over the world — traces back to one small farm. Search online to find the Canadian province where grower Howard Dill first bred the Atlantic Giant pumpkin, back in the 1970s and 80s. Type just the province name (two words).

Hint 1

Search "Howard Dill Atlantic Giant pumpkin" and look for where his farm was located.

💡 Did you know? The very first giant pumpkin world record, set at the 1893 World's Fair in Chicago, weighed just 365 pounds. Every modern champion is now more than seven times heavier than that record-breaker was.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Longest Vine Mix-Up

While the scale technician works, the fair holds its just-for-fun Longest Vine contest among three growers: Roscoe, Dot, and Walt. The registration clerk copied down each grower's claim, but made exactly ONE mistake — one of these three statements does NOT match reality:

Roscoe says: "My vine is longer than Dot's."
Dot says: "My vine is longer than Walt's."
Walt says: "My vine is the longest of the three."

Judge Pemberton already spot-checked one fact herself with a tape measure: Walt's vine is definitely NOT the shortest of the three.

Using logic — not guessing — figure out whose statement is the false one. Type just their first name.

Hint 1

List every possible order for the three vines, from longest to shortest (there are 6 orders). For each one, count how many of the three statements would be true.

Hint 2

Only one of those orders makes "exactly one statement false" true AND keeps Walt from being shortest. Cross out every order that fails either test.

💡 Did you know? Pumpkin weight doesn't grow at the same rate as pumpkin size. A pumpkin that's 50% bigger around can end up almost four times heavier — which is exactly why judges trust math, not guesses, whenever a scale can't be used.

MISSION 08
HARD

Weigh the Behemoth by Math

The scale is officially dead — sparks and all. Judge Pemberton pulls out her tape measures instead. This is the real trick fair judges use whenever a scale fails: estimate the weight from measurements, using the same kind of math real giant-pumpkin judges rely on.

Big Walt's Behemoth is measured at its widest point. Here's the top-down view:

The line from the center to the edge is the radius. Remember: diameter = radius × 2, and circumference = π × diameter.

Stage 1 of 2

What is the circumference (the distance all the way around) of Big Walt's Behemoth at its widest point? Use circumference = π × diameter, with π ≈ 3.14. Round to the nearest whole inch.

Hint 1

The diagram shows the RADIUS, not the diameter. Diameter is double the radius — find that first.

Stage 2 of 2

Judge Pemberton's clipboard also lists the pumpkin's height (ground to top, not counting the stem): 65 inches. Real giant-pumpkin judges use a formula like this simplified version to estimate weight from measurements:

Estimated weight (lbs) = (circumference × circumference × height) ÷ 800

Use your Stage 1 answer as the circumference. Round your final answer to the nearest whole pound.

Hint 1

Multiply your Stage 1 circumference by itself first (that's circumference squared), then multiply that result by the height.

Hint 2

Take (circumference × circumference × 65) and divide the whole thing by 800. Round to the nearest whole number.

MISSION 09
BIG AHA!

The Sponsorship Trap

While everyone waits on Walt's number to be confirmed, Hazel Kowalski asks for your help. Two local businesses want to sponsor Old Hazel (1,150 lbs, remember?), and she can only pick one deal:

DEAL A — Meridian Farm Supply: pays $4.00 per pound... but takes a 15% "handling fee" off the total before paying her.
DEAL B — Ferris & Sons Hardware: pays a flat $3.50 per pound, no fees at all.

At first glance, Deal A's rate looks bigger. Grab a pencil and work out what each deal actually pays Hazel for her 1,150-pound pumpkin, fee included. The better deal pays more money — by how many DOLLARS does it beat the other one? (Type just the number, no dollar sign.)

Hint 1

Work out the FULL payout for each deal first, before worrying about any fee.

Hint 2

Meridian's 15% fee comes OFF Hazel's total pay. Find 15% of Deal A's full payout, then subtract it from that payout.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Announcing the Real Champion

It's almost 4:00 PM. Judge Pemberton climbs onto the stage with the corrected leaderboard in hand — but she needs YOU to confirm the name before she says it into the microphone.

Here's what the case file already tells you: Dot's Dazzler weighed in at 1,280 lbs. Old Hazel weighed in at 1,150 lbs. Roscoe's Round Wonder weighed in at 640 lbs. Nettie's Nugget weighed in at 380 lbs. And you already rescued Big Walt's Behemoth's true weight with math, back when the scale died.

(Don't be fooled by the Longest Vine mix-up from earlier — that contest was about vines, not weight, and has nothing to do with who wins the Weigh-Off.)

Compare every official weight in the case file. Who is the TRUE champion of the Amberfield County Fair Giant Pumpkin Weigh-Off? Type the grower's full first and last name.

Hint 1

You calculated one grower's weight yourself, using math instead of a scale. Scroll back and check that number against everyone else's.

Hint 2

The champion's pumpkin outweighs every other pumpkin in the case file by hundreds of pounds — it isn't close.

Case closed?

Champion confirmed! Judge Pemberton didn't need a working scale to find the truth — she needed someone who wouldn't give up when the numbers got messy and the clock got tight. That's called PERSEVERANCE, and today, Junior Judging Assistant, you've got it in spades.
