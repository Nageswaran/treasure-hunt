The Hall of Fame Wall

Answer: 1974
How to get there

Search "Half Moon Bay pumpkin weigh-off first year" (or similar). Half Moon Bay, California, held its first official weigh-off in 1974, after then-Mayor Melvin Mello challenged Circleville, Ohio — which had proclaimed itself "Pumpkin Capital of the World" — to settle whose pumpkins were really biggest. Mission 03 · Medium

What Makes a Pumpkin Go Giant?

Answer: phloem
How to get there

Search "how do giant pumpkins get so big science." Multiple science sources explain that giant pumpkins have unusually large amounts of phloem — the plant tissue that transports sugars from the leaves into the fruit. Giant pumpkins don't make more sugar than ordinary pumpkins; they simply have more "delivery pipes" (extra phloem) to pump sugar-rich fluid into the fruit, which is a major reason for their extreme size. Xylem, mentioned in the puzzle as a contrast, carries water and minerals instead.

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle explicitly rules out xylem, and only one plant tissue type transports sugar (phloem) — there is no other reasonable single-word answer.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search corroborated by a Plant, Cell & Environment peer-reviewed study on giant pumpkin phloem ("The making of giant pumpkins," Savage et al., 2015), a Botanist in the Kitchen Q&A with the study's author, and IFLScience's summary of the same research.

Mission 04 · Medium

Roots of the Giants

Answer: Nova Scotia
How to get there

Search "Howard Dill Atlantic Giant pumpkin." Howard Dill farmed in Windsor, Nova Scotia, Canada, where he bred and patented the Atlantic Giant pumpkin variety (patented 1979). His farm is still a working pumpkin farm today and is considered the birthplace of the modern giant pumpkin movement.

Why this is the only answer

This is a fixed, well-documented geographical/historical fact about a specific real farm and person — it does not change over time.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search corroborated by Howard Dill's Wikipedia biography, Tourism Nova Scotia's listing for Howard Dill Enterprises, and Maclean's magazine's profile of Dill as "the father of all pumpkins."

Did You Know? (after Mission 04)

1893 World's Fair fact

Verified: the earliest documented giant-pumpkin world record was set at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago by William Warnock of Ontario, Canada, at 365 pounds. Since modern record pumpkins (2023-2025) weigh roughly 2,700-2,820 pounds, "more than seven times heavier" is accurate and will remain true as future records only go up.

Did You Know? (after Mission 07)

Non-linear weight scaling fact

Verified: giant-pumpkin weight-estimation resources note that weight does not scale linearly with size — for example, a pumpkin with a 50% larger "over-the-top" measurement can end up nearly 400% heavier (roughly quadruple), not just 50% heavier. This is exactly why growers and judges use a measurement-based formula instead of eyeballing size, which sets up Mission 08's math.

Mission 08 · Hard (multi-stage: geometry + estimation formula)

Weigh the Behemoth by Math

Stage 1 answer: 157 (inches)

Diameter = radius × 2 = 25 × 2 = 50 inches. Circumference = π × diameter = 3.14 × 50 = 157 inches exactly.

Stage 2 (final) answer: 2003 (pounds)

Estimated weight = (circumference × circumference × height) ÷ 800 = (157 × 157 × 65) ÷ 800 = (24,649 × 65) ÷ 800 = 1,602,185 ÷ 800 = 2,002.73125, which rounds to 2,003 pounds.

Why this is the only answer

Both stages use fixed given numbers (radius 25 in, π ≈ 3.14, height 65 in) plugged into a fully specified formula, producing one forced numeric result at each stage once rounded as instructed.

Source / fact used

The Great Pumpkin Commonwealth's real over-the-top (OTT) weight-estimation method uses three measurements (circumference, side-to-side, end-to-end) run through a complex exponential formula, refined from decades of real weigh-in data (originally developed from Bob Marcellus's data analysis, later regression work by David Martin). That exact formula is far too advanced for a 10-year-old. This mission uses a simplified, real, and independently documented approximation — Estimated Weight (lbs) = (Circumference² × Height) ÷ 800 — commonly cited by pumpkin-growing and gardening education resources (with the constant 800 reflecting average pumpkin density in imperial units) as an accessible way to estimate weight from circumference and height using only multiplication and division. It's explicitly a simplified stand-in for the real, more complex growers' formula, not the exact competition formula itself.

The Sponsorship Trap

Answer: 115
How to get there

Deal A (Meridian): full payout = 1,150 × $4.00 = $4,600. Handling fee = 15% of $4,600 = $690. Net payout = $4,600 − $690 = $3,910. Deal B (Ferris & Sons): 1,150 × $3.50 = $4,025, no fee. Deal B beats Deal A by $4,025 − $3,910 = $115.

Why this is the only answer

Both deals are fully specified (rate, fee, and pumpkin weight are all given), so each nets one exact dollar amount, and their difference is a single forced number.

Why this is the "Aha!"

Deal A's $4.00/lb rate looks better than Deal B's $3.50/lb rate at a glance — the "aha" is realizing the hidden 15% fee flips which deal actually pays more, teaching that a bigger headline number isn't automatically the better deal once fine print is included.

Source / fact used
