Mission 01 · Easy · Technology

The Camera Manual's Missing Page

Answer: 24
How to get there

Search "standard frame rate for film" or "frames per second movies." Motion-picture film and digital cinema projection both settled on 24 frames per second (fps) as the industry standard, which is why the studio's "camera manual" says every filmmaker agrees on this number.

Why this is the only answer

24 fps is the universally cited standard for theatrical film; other numbers a child might find (like 30 or 60) belong to TV broadcast or video games, not the "standard for movies" the puzzle specifically asks about.

Verified via web search across multiple animation-education sources (Adafruit Learning System, Animationclub School, HUE) confirming 24 fps as the standard cinematic frame rate. Also used directly by Missions 5, 9, and 10.

Mission 04 · Medium · Dutch Language

Sorting the Supply Shelf

Answer: klei
How to get there

Of the five words, four take "het": het licht (light), het decor (set), het draaiboek (script), het statief (tripod). "Klei" (clay) is the odd one out — it takes "de": de klei.

Why this is the only answer

Only one of the five words is a de-woord; the other four are het-woorden, so "klei" is the unique odd one out.

Mission 05 · Medium · Mathematics & Technology

Posing Ferris, Frame by Frame

Answer: 12
How to get there

Shooting "on twos" means each pose is held for 2 frames. At the studio's 24 frames-per-second standard (Mission 1), the animator needs 24 ÷ 2 = 12 unique poses per second of finished film.

Why this is the only answer

The math is a direct division using two clearly stated numbers (24 fps and "held for 2 frames"); no other reading of "how many different poses per second" is possible.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search (Stop Motion Studio's own help documentation, animation-technique explainers) confirming "shooting on twos" means roughly 12 unique frames/poses per second of animation at a 24fps standard.

Mission 08 · Hard · Cryptography

The Clapperboard's Mirror Code

Answer: premiere
How to get there

This is an Atbash cipher (A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X ... M↔N). Decoding KIVNRVIV letter by letter: K→P, I→R, V→E, N→M, R→I, V→E, I→R, V→E, giving PREMIERE.

Why this is the only answer

Atbash is a fixed, one-to-one letter-mirroring substitution with no key to guess — every letter has exactly one mirror partner, so there is only one possible decode of KIVNRVIV.

Source / fact used

Self-contained cryptography puzzle (Atbash cipher, a real historical cipher mechanic distinct from Caesar/Morse/binary). Re-encoded and decoded independently to confirm PREMIERE ↔ KIVNRVIV round-trips correctly.

Big "Aha!" Moment · Mathematics & Logical Reasoning (Advanced — working backward)

The Editor's Missing Minutes

Answer: 187
How to get there

Grab-a-pencil tally, chunk by chunk (all at 24 fps):

Chunk 1: 168+192+120 = 480 frames ÷ 24 = 20 sec
Chunk 2: 216+192+144 = 552 frames ÷ 24 = 23 sec
Chunk 3: 168+240 = 408 frames ÷ 24 = 17 sec
Chunk 4: 240+192+144 = 576 frames ÷ 24 = 24 sec
Chunk 5: 264+216+216 = 696 frames ÷ 24 = 29 sec

Total known: 20+23+17+24+29 = 113 seconds. Missing footage: 300 − 113 = 187 seconds.

Why this is the only answer

Every number in the tally is explicitly given, and the operations (sum frames per chunk, divide by the stated 24 fps, sum the five chunk-lengths, subtract from the stated 300-second total) are fully determined — there's only one way to combine the given numbers into a running-time total, so 187 is the unique result. (Cross-check: total known frames 2,712 ÷ 24 = 113 sec also confirms; total film frames 300×24=7,200, minus 2,712 = 4,488 frames = 187 sec — both paths agree.)

Source / fact used

Self-contained arithmetic puzzle (Advanced logical-reasoning tier: working backward from a known total to an unknown part). Recomputed independently from scratch to confirm 187.

The Last Reel Before Curtain

Stage 2 depends entirely on that result to select the correct canister's total (552, uniquely tied to "Reel 4" in the story); the subtraction and division that follow use only the numbers given, with one unique result.
