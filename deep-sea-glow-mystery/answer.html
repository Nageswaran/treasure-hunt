Mission 01 · Easy

The Glowing Fishing Rod

Answer: esca
How to get there

Searching "anglerfish lure glowing tip name" leads to the term "esca" — the bulb-shaped tip of the anglerfish's illicium (fishing-rod-like fin ray), which glows thanks to symbiotic bioluminescent bacteria living inside it.

Why this is the only answer

"Illicium" names the rod itself, not the glowing part — the puzzle specifically asks for the light-making tip, which is the esca.

Source / fact used

Confirmed via Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution ("About the Anglerfish," whoi.edu) and Smithsonian Ocean Portal, which describe the esca as the bioluminescent-bacteria-lit lure tip.

Mission 02 · Easy

Real Glow or Just a Reflection?

Answer: 2
How to get there

Item 1 (vampire squid) and item 4 (anglerfish) are true bioluminescence — both animals chemically produce their own light. Item 2 (moonlight on water) and item 5 (flashlight on scales) are plain reflection. Item 3 (comb jelly rainbow ripples in sunlight) is iridescence — light diffracting off moving cilia, not light the animal generates. So 2 of the 5 are true light-makers.

Why this is the only answer

The wording specifically calls out the comb jelly's rainbow ripples "when sunlight hits" its combs — that phrasing points to iridescence, not the separate, genuine bioluminescent glow comb jellies can also produce in darkness (which this item is not describing).

Source / fact used

Vampire squid bioluminescence confirmed via Monterey Bay Aquarium and NOAA Ocean Exploration ("Not So Bloodthirsty: An Encounter with a Vampire Squid"). Comb jelly iridescence-vs-bioluminescence distinction confirmed via AskNature and multiple aquarium sources describing ctenophore cilia as producing iridescence, a separate phenomenon from their bioluminescent glow.

Mission 03 · Medium

How Low Can You Go?

Answer: 11
How to get there

Challenger Deep, the deepest point of the Mariana Trench, measures approximately 10,935 meters. Dividing by 1,000 gives 10.935 km, which rounds to the nearest whole kilometer: 11.

Why this is the only answer

Some sources give slightly different exact figures (10,902-10,935 m from various expeditions), but all commonly cited recent measurements round to 11 km, so the answer is robust to which reputable source the child finds.

Source / fact used

Wikipedia ("Challenger Deep") and geology.com report the most recent (2020-2021) measurement at 10,935 ± 6 meters.

Mission 04 · Medium

The Diver's Depth Chart

Answer: 2000 (meters)
How to get there

The Midnight zone starts at 1,000 m and is 3,000 m thick, so it ends at 1,000 + 3,000 = 4,000 m. The Abyssal zone runs from there down to the labeled bottom depth of 6,000 m, so its thickness is 6,000 - 4,000 = 2,000 meters.

Why this is the only answer

Every number needed (1,000 m start, 3,000 m thickness, 6,000 m bottom) is directly labeled on the diagram; the only step is the two-part subtraction, giving one forced numeric answer.

Source / fact used

Zone boundaries match real NOAA/WHOI ocean-zone figures: Sunlight (epipelagic) 0-200 m, Twilight (mesopelagic) 200-1,000 m, Midnight (bathypelagic) ~1,000-4,000 m, Abyssal (abyssopelagic) 4,000-6,000 m. Confirmed via NOAA Ocean Exploration and Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution's "Ocean Zones" page.

Mission 05 · Medium

The Crew's Dutch Notebook

Answer: Het
How to get there

"Licht" (light) is a Dutch het-word, so the correct sentence is "Het licht van de inktvis was blauw-groen."

Why this is the only answer

Dutch nouns are either de-words or het-words with no reliable spelling rule — "licht" is simply one of the fixed het-words, confirmed in standard Dutch dictionaries and grammar guides.

Source / fact used

Confirmed via multiple Dutch-grammar reference sites (e.g. "De of Het Licht?" grammar guides) that consistently list "het licht" as the correct, standard form.

Whose Report Can You Trust?

Answer: vampire squid
How to get there

The camera confirms the creature had arms, not fins. Anglerfish are bony fish — they have fins, not arms — so Mia's identification cannot be correct. Vampire squids are cephalopods with eight long, webbed arms, matching the camera detail exactly. So Sam is the crew member who reported accurately, and the creature was the vampire squid.

Why this is the only answer

The "arms, not fins" clue is only consistent with one of the two claims — there is no way to make the anglerfish identification fit an animal with arms, since anglerfish have no arms at all.

Source / fact used

Vampire squid anatomy (eight long arms webbed together) confirmed via Monterey Bay Aquarium and National Geographic. Anglerfish being a true bony fish (fins, not arms) is basic, well-established marine biology.

Mission 09 · Big Aha (Multi-stage)

The Signal Behind the Gaps

Stage 1 answer: 9 (atm)

Stage 1 explanation: Pressure = 1 + (depth ÷ 10) for each dive: 20 m → 3 atm, 60 m → 7 atm, 80 m → 9 atm, 100 m → 11 atm, 140 m → 15 atm. Sum = 3+7+9+11+15 = 45. Average = 45 ÷ 5 = 9 atm exactly (no rounding needed).

Stage 2 (final) answer: isopod

Stage 2 explanation: The 9th letter of the alphabet (A=1) is I. The Morse code "... --- .--. --- -.." decodes letter by letter as S (...), O (---), P (.--.), O (---), D (-..) = "SOPOD". Prefixing with I gives "ISOPOD" — a real deep-sea scavenger with oversized, light-sensitive eyes that would happily crawl right in front of a glowing sensor.

Why this is the only answer

Stage 1's arithmetic produces exactly 9 with no rounding ambiguity. Stage 2's Morse groups map to exactly one letter each on the standard International Morse Code table, and 9 maps to exactly one letter of the alphabet (I), so "ISOPOD" is the single forced result.

Source / fact used

The "roughly 1 atmosphere of pressure per 10 meters of seawater depth" rule is confirmed via NOAA's Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory ("Water Pressures at Ocean Depths") and Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution. Morse code letters hand-verified against the standard International Morse Code table: S = ..., O = ---, P = .--., O = ---, D = -... Giant isopods' oversized, light-sensitive eyes confirmed via Smithsonian Ocean ("Meet the Giant Isopods") and NOAA Ocean Exploration.

Grand Finale

The Truth About the Kraken

Answer: 43 (feet)
How to get there

Searching "Smithsonian giant squid longest recorded length" leads to the Smithsonian Ocean Portal, which states the largest giant squid ever scientifically recorded measured 43 feet (about 13 meters) in total length, including its long feeding tentacles.

Why this is the only answer

The frequently repeated "60 feet" figure is a media exaggeration not backed by any actual specimen measurement — the Smithsonian explicitly notes the true recorded maximum is smaller. 43 feet is the number tied to an actual measured specimen, making it the one defensible, source-backed answer.

Source / fact used

Smithsonian Ocean Portal / Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, "How Big is the Giant Squid?"
