Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Treehouse Club's Blueprint Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60–75 minutes

Welcome to the Acorn Street Treehouse & Inventors' Club! You're the newest member, and Build Day is this Saturday — the whole club is finally putting up the treehouse in the big tree at the back of Mira's yard.

Last night, a gust of wind blew through the clubhouse shed and scattered every blueprint page, tool-bag note, and logbook scrap across the yard. Nobody can agree anymore on the shape of the platform, who's doing which job, or how much rope and wood to buy.

Your job: piece the plan back together, one clue at a time. Solve every mission and you'll land on the exact amount of rope the club needs to finish the build — the last number on the Build Day checklist.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Rule Sheet in the Mud

The first blueprint page you find is a muddy "Build Order Rule Sheet." The six build steps got shuffled and labeled with random letters, but the rule sheet also lists facts about what has to happen before what. Read carefully — the facts force one exact order.

The six steps (shuffled):

A. Attach the ladder to the platform edge
B. Measure the trunk and mark where the support beams go
C. Bolt the support beams onto the trunk
D. Lay the decking planks across the platform frame
E. Build the railing around the platform's open edges
F. Paint the walls and hang the window shutters

The rules:

— You must mark your measurements before you can bolt anything.
— You can't lay decking planks until the support beams are bolted on.
— The railing can only go up after the decking is laid.
— The ladder can only be attached after the railing is finished, so climbers have something to hold onto at the top.
— Painting is always the very last step, once everything else is built.

Hint 1

Look for words like "before," "until," and "after" in the rules — each one links exactly two steps together. Chain the links from the very first step to the very last.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Right Tree for the Job

Mira's backyard has three trees, and one blueprint scrap just says: "Build in the safest one — obviously!" but doesn't say which. Search online to find out which kind of tree real treehouse builders trust, and which kinds they always avoid.

The three candidates are:

🌱 A young pine sapling, thinner than your wrist
🪵 A tall dead elm tree with no leaves and crumbly bark
🌳 A mature, healthy oak tree with a thick trunk and strong branches

Which one should the club build in? Type just the tree name (one word, e.g. "birch").

Hint 1

Search for what real builders look for in a "treehouse tree" — search words like healthy, mature, and hardwood, and think about why a dead tree or a skinny sapling would fail those tests.

💡 Did you know? Trees don't actually heal their wounds the way people do — they seal them off instead. It's called compartmentalization: the tree builds a wall around the damaged wood so it can safely keep growing around it.

📓 Club Logbook — worth remembering

Taped inside the shed door, in Mira's handwriting: "Reminder for the next build: we still have 3 leftover planks from last year's ground fort, stacked behind the shed. Don't buy more than we need!"

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Blueprint Page Two: The Platform Shape

You find Blueprint Page 2, pressed flat under a flowerpot. It shows the platform's outline — with a chunk missing in one corner, where the tree trunk itself will pass through. Every measurement you need is marked right on the drawing.

Figure out the total area of decking (in square meters) the club needs to buy to cover the whole platform, trunk-gap and all.

Every side of the platform's outline is labeled in meters — use them to work out the total decking area.
Hint 1

Try splitting the L-shape into two simpler rectangles, or picture the whole 5 m × 4 m rectangle first and then remove the missing corner.

Hint 2

Whole rectangle: 5 m × 4 m. Missing corner: 2 m × 2 m. Subtract the second from the first.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Three Pop-Ups, One Safe Click

While hunting for a replacement blueprint online, Zara's laptop shows three pop-ups at once. Only ONE of them is safe for the club to click.

A) A flashing red banner: "🎉🎉 YOU'RE THE 1,000TH VISITOR!! Click NOW for 100% FREE Secret Treehouse Blueprints! Timer: 01:58 ⏰ Enter a parent's email + phone number to unlock!"

B) A plain gray box on a woodworking resource page: "Free downloadable treehouse building guide (PDF, 2.1 MB) — published by the Woodworking for Beginners resource page. No sign-up needed."

C) A spinning-logo pop-up: "Install the 'TreehouseHelper' app to see secret plans! (Downloads a file straight to your device — not from your phone's app store)"

Which one is safe to click — A, B, or C?

Hint 1

Real free downloads don't usually need a countdown timer or a stranger's phone number to "unlock" them.

Hint 2

A safe download sits quietly on a normal page. Watch out for anything that pressures you to hurry, asks for personal details, or wants you to install a file from outside an official app store.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Mixed-Up Tool Bag

Someone dumped the whole tool bag onto the porch, searching for a blueprint page underneath — and now kitchen stuff is mixed in with the tools. Here's everything that spilled out:

a tape measure · a spirit level · a carpenter's square · an oven mitt · a chalk line

Four of these are proper building or measuring tools you'd expect in a builder's kit. One doesn't belong there at all. Which item should go back in the kitchen drawer, not the tool bag?

Hint 1

Think about what job each item actually helps you do — building, measuring, and marking straight lines... or something completely different.

Hint 2

Four items help you measure length, check for level, or mark a line for cutting. One item is only useful for handling something hot.

💡 Did you know? On the Janka hardness scale (a real test of how hard wood is), white oak scores about 1,360 — over three times harder than knotty pine's score of around 380! That's part of why Mira insists on oak for anything that has to hold weight.

MISSION 06
HARD

Who Does Which Job?

A torn job-assignment note names the four club members — Mira, Theo, Zara, and Oskar — and the four jobs for Build Day: sawing planks, hammering nails, measuring wood, and painting the door. The note is torn, but these clues survived:

1. Mira doesn't saw, and she doesn't hammer either — she likes precise work.
2. The person measuring the wood is not Theo.
3. Zara loves color, so she always ends up painting something.
4. Oskar has the steadiest hands of anyone in the club, so he's hammering nails — and he never measures.

Work out the full job for each person. Who will be sawing the planks?

Hint 1

Start with the clue that names a job directly and certainly — that gives you your first fixed answer before anything else.

Hint 2

Once you know Zara paints and Oskar hammers, only two jobs and two people are left. Use the "not" clues to place the last two.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Lumber Order

Stage 1 of 2 — grab a pencil for this one!

Each pre-cut deck plank the lumber yard sells covers exactly 2 square meters. Using the platform's total decking area you worked out on Blueprint Page 2 (Mission 3), how many planks does the club need to cover the whole platform with no gaps?

Hint 1

You already figured out the platform's total area earlier in this case — scroll back to Mission 3 if you need to check it.

Hint 2

Divide the total area by how many square meters one plank covers.

Stage 2 of 2 — the final total

The lumber yard sells planks for $6 each. But the club doesn't need to buy every plank from scratch — remember what was noted in the Club Logbook earlier in this case? Subtract those leftover planks from the number you just calculated, then work out the total cost (in dollars) of buying the rest.

Hint 1

Check the Club Logbook note from earlier in the case — how many planks does the club already have sitting behind the shed?

Hint 2

Planks still needed = planks required minus leftover planks. Then multiply that number by the price per plank.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Rope That Won't Slip

The club's pulley rope needs a strong loop tied at the end so it can't slip or tighten by accident while someone is hoisting the toolbox. Search online to find the classic loop knot that sailors and rescue workers trust most for exactly this job — it's sometimes even called the "king of knots."

Type just the knot's name (one word).

Hint 1

Search for a loop knot that holds firm under load but is still easy to untie afterward — it's one of the most famous knots in the world.

Hint 2

Sailors remember how to tie it with a little story: the rabbit comes up out of its hole, goes around the tree, and dives back down the hole.

💡 Did you know? A single fixed pulley doesn't reduce the force you need at all — it just changes the direction you pull. You only get real force-saving "mechanical advantage" by adding a movable pulley that travels along with the load itself.

MISSION 09 · BIG AHA
AHA!

Lifting Without the Muscle

The toolbox and tools the club needs to haul up to the platform weigh about 12 kg altogether. To stay safe, no kid should pull with more than about 4 kg of force on the rope at once.

Search online to learn how a pulley system's "mechanical advantage" works. Here's the key idea real engineers use: the more rope segments are directly supporting the load, the less force each one needs — mechanical advantage equals the number of supporting rope segments.

How many supporting rope segments does the club's pulley system need, so a kid really can lift the toolbox safely?

Hint 1

Search for how mechanical advantage works for a pulley — what happens to the force you need as you add more supporting rope segments?

Hint 2

Divide the weight that needs lifting by the force one kid can safely pull.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Line on the Build Day Checklist

Build Day is tomorrow, and there's one line left blank on the checklist: how much rope to buy for the pulley.

The platform sits 3 meters above the ground in the old oak tree. The pulley needs ONE full length of rope for EACH supporting rope segment you worked out in Mission 9 — and every one of those lengths has to reach all the way from the ground up to the platform. On top of that, add 2 extra meters at the bottom, so the crew has enough spare rope to pull and tie off safely.

How many total meters of rope should the club buy?

Hint 1

How many rope segments did you work out the pulley needs in Mission 9? Each one has to be at least as long as the platform is tall.

Hint 2

Multiply the number of segments by the platform's height, then add the extra 2 meters for tying off.
