Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Case of the Missing Boarding Pass

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes
A navy-blue suitcase just came off the wrong carousel at bustling Meridian International Airport — no name on it, no boarding pass, just stickers, torn tags, and a lot of questions. Somewhere in this airport, a passenger is about to board a flight without her bag, and the gates are closing fast. You've been called in from the Internet Detective Academy to follow every clue on this suitcase, find out who it really belongs to, and figure out exactly where — and when — she needs to be.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Postcard on the Handle

Stuck to the suitcase's handle is a well-worn postcard. On the back, someone has scrawled a description instead of a name:

"Greetings from the Lost City of the Incas — a stone citadel perched high in the Andes Mountains, kept secret from the outside world until an American explorer rediscovered it in 1911."

Search online to find out: which COUNTRY is this famous "Lost City" in? That's the last place this suitcase's owner visited.

Hint 1

Try searching for "lost city of the Incas" together with "1911" — one very famous place comes up again and again.

💡 Did you know? Machu Picchu, high in Peru's Andes Mountains, was built around 1450 without any iron tools or wheels — the Inca builders shaped and moved massive stone blocks using stone hammers, wooden levers, and sheer teamwork.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Torn Tag

Under the postcard, half-hidden, is a torn luggage tag. Someone has written a coded word on it in shaky handwriting:

HOHQD

The airline's rush-tag clerk always shifts each letter forward by 3 places in the alphabet when writing these codes (so A becomes D, B becomes E, and so on). Shift every letter of HOHQD backward by 3 to find the owner's first name.

Hint 1

Write the alphabet out in a row, then for each letter in HOHQD, count back 3 letters — for example, H → G → F → E.

📓 Detective's Notebook: Just below the torn edge of the tag, before it rips off completely, you spot four more digits stamped in ink: 3742. It might just be an old baggage-handling code... or it might matter later. Worth jotting down.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Four Claims at the Counter

At the help desk, four passengers all insist the navy suitcase is theirs: Marcus Webb, Fatima Nasser, Elena Petrova, and Elena Duarte.

Clue 1: You already decoded the torn tag — the true owner's first name is Elena.

Clue 2: Inside the suitcase is a customs declaration form. Under "Occupation," it reads: Marine Biologist.

Clue 3: Elena Petrova is a touring violinist. Elena Duarte works as a marine biologist, on her way to a research station.

Who does the suitcase really belong to? Type the owner's full name (first and last).

Hint 1

Clue 1 already rules out two of the four passengers entirely.

Hint 2

Between the two remaining passengers, only one has a job that matches what's written on the customs form.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Torn Customs Seal

The suitcase carries an old gold customs seal — a rectangular sticker with one corner torn clean off. The airline wants to print an exact replacement patch, so they need to know exactly how many square centimeters of gold sticker are left.

Grab a pencil for this one — it's much easier to work out on paper than in your head! Use the measurements marked on the picture below.

How many square centimeters of gold sticker remain? (Type just the number, no units.)
Hint 1

Start by working out the area of the whole rectangle, as if no corner were missing at all.

Hint 2

Split the shape into one big rectangle (18 cm × 10 cm) and subtract the torn-off corner's rectangle (6 cm × 4 cm).

💡 Did you know? Suitcases didn't roll on wheels until 1970! Inventor Bernard Sadow patented the first wheeled suitcase that year — before that, every traveler carried their whole bag by hand, no matter how heavy.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Through the Terminal

You know the suitcase belongs to Elena Duarte. Now you need to carry it from Lost & Found to the transfer desk. Below is a hand-drawn map of the terminal, laid out in a grid of lettered columns and numbered rows.

Lost & Found (marked with a star) is at square B1. From there: walk 2 squares EAST, then 3 squares NORTH. Which grid square do you land on?

★ = Lost & Found, where you start. Answer using a letter and a number, like "C3".
Hint 1

Do the moves in order: face east first and count those steps, then turn to face north for the rest.

Hint 2

Moving east changes the letter (A→B→C...). Moving north changes the number, going up (1→2→3...).

MISSION 06
HARD

Gate Assignments

At the gate-assignment desk, four passengers are listed for four different gates: 14, 18, 22, and 26. The passengers are Elena, Yusuf, Priya, and Tomas. Here's what the desk clerk tells you:

Clue 1: Priya's gate is not 14, and it's not 18 either.

Clue 2: Tomas's gate number is exactly 4 higher than Priya's gate number.

Clue 3: Take the last digit of the baggage tag number you noted earlier. Subtract it from Elena's gate number, and you get 12.

Which gate is Elena's? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Start with Clue 1 and Clue 2 together — they only leave one possible gate for Priya.

Hint 2

Once Priya and Tomas have their gates, only two gates are left for Elena and Yusuf. Clue 3 tells you which one is Elena's.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Blinking Tracker

Clipped to the suitcase handle is a tiny digital luggage tracker. Its little screen blinks a pattern in binary code, five digits at a time:

01110  10010  10100

Each group of five digits is a number in binary — and each number matches a letter's position in the alphabet (A=1, B=2, C=3, all the way to Z=26). Decode the three letters. They spell a real 3-letter airport code.

Search online to find out: which CITY is that airport in?

Hint 1

Turn each 5-digit binary group into an ordinary decimal number first, before matching it to a letter.

Hint 2

A=1, B=2, C=3, all the way to Z=26. Which three letters do 14, 18, and 20 spell?

MISSION 08
HARD

Race Against the Clock

Stage 1 of 2

Folded inside a zipper pocket is a strip of paper with this written on it:

JSVXCJMZI

The airline's night-shift clerk always shifts each letter forward by 4 places when writing codes. Shift every letter of JSVXCJMZI backward by 4. It spells a number, written out in words. Type just that number.

Hint 1

Write the alphabet out and count backward 4 letters for each letter in JSVXCJMZI.

Stage 2 of 2

You already know Elena's suitcase is Tokyo-bound. Her flight departs at 23:35, Tokyo local time. Boarding always closes the number of minutes you just found in Stage 1 before the flight departs.

Search online for Tokyo's time zone, and for Reykjavik's (that's where Meridian Airport sits) — both places stay on the exact same time all year round, with no daylight saving changes.

What time will Meridian Airport's departure board show, in Reykjavik time, at the exact moment boarding closes? Answer in 24-hour time, like "09:05".

Hint 1

First work out what time boarding closes in Tokyo — subtract the Stage 1 number of minutes from the 23:35 departure time.

Hint 2

Tokyo runs 9 hours ahead of Reykjavik, all year round. Subtract 9 hours from the Tokyo boarding-closure time to get Reykjavik time.

💡 Did you know? Iceland and Japan never change their clocks for daylight saving time — they stay on the exact same time zone all year round, which makes them two of the easiest countries in the world to convert time for!

MISSION 09
AHA MOMENT

Two Agents, One Truth

Word arrives that Elena's gate may have changed at the last minute. Two gate agents are on duty: Agent Silva and Agent Cole. One of them always tells the truth. The other always lies. You don't know which is which — but you can still figure this out.

Agent Silva says: "Cole is telling the truth, and the new gate is Gate 31."

Agent Cole says: "The gate is still Gate 14."

Using only pure logic — no guessing — work out which gate is really correct. Type just the number.

Hint 1

Try assuming Agent Silva is the truth-teller. Follow that assumption all the way through both statements — does everything still fit together?

Hint 2

If Silva is truthful, then Silva's ENTIRE statement must be true, including the part about Cole. That would mean Cole is also truthful — but then Cole's own statement must match Silva's gate number too. Does it?

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Run For It

Elena is sprinting through the terminal with a boarding pass but no bag — and you're the only one with the full picture. You've already worked out exactly which gate her flight is really leaving from, and exactly what time (in Reykjavik time) boarding closes there.

Combine both pieces into one final answer, in this exact format: gate [number] [time] — for example: gate 9 08:05

Hint 1

You worked out this gate number in two completely different ways, in two different missions. Both should agree.

Hint 2

You already calculated the exact boarding-closure time, in Reykjavik time, back in an earlier stage. Just combine gate and time, separated by a space.

Case closed?

Elena makes it to the gate with seconds to spare, suitcase in hand — all because you followed every clue instead of guessing. That's what real detective work feels like: the thrill of discovery, piecing together small clues until the whole picture suddenly, satisfyingly makes sense.
