Mission 01 · Easy · Logical Reasoning (Foundational — Sequencing)

Race Morning, Step by Step

Answer: 4
How to get there

Correct order: (1) Pin on your bib number at home, (2) Drop your gear bag at the bag-drop tent, (3) Warm up near the start area, (4) Show your wristband to enter your starting corral, (5) Hear the starting gun, (6) Cross the finish line. "Show your wristband to enter your starting corral" is the 4th event.

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle text pins the tricky ordering step explicitly: bags are dropped early (30 minutes before the gun) and warm-up happens in whatever time is left before corral entry, so bag-drop must precede warm-up, which must precede corral entry. Pinning the bib at home is the only event that happens before leaving for the race, so it must be first; crossing the finish is the only event that can only happen last.

Source / fact used

General knowledge of how marathon race mornings are structured (bag-drop tents closing well before the gun, corral entry via wristband) — consistent across major marathon event-day guides.

Mission 02 · Easy · History + Internet Research

Why 42.195 Kilometers?

Answer: Windsor Castle
How to get there

Search "1908 Olympics marathon distance 26.2 miles" or similar. Multiple reputable sources confirm the 1908 London Olympic marathon started on the lawn of Windsor Castle (so the royal children could watch from a nursery window) and finished at White City Stadium in front of the Royal Box, adding up to 26 miles 385 yards (42.195 km). That specific distance was formally standardized by the IAAF (now World Athletics) in 1921 and has been the fixed marathon distance ever since. The ancient origin story (a runner from Marathon to Athens, ~40 km, inspiring the first modern Olympic marathon in 1896) explains the CONCEPT of the marathon, but not the exact 42.195 km figure — that comes specifically from 1908.

Why this is the only answer

The question specifically asks for the royal RESIDENCE where the race STARTED — Windsor Castle is a residence/castle, distinguishing it clearly from White City Stadium (the finish location, a stadium, not a residence).

Source / fact used

Verified via web search across multiple sources including Britannica ("Why Is a Marathon 26.2 Miles?"), Marathon Handbook, and Wikipedia's "Athletics at the 1908 Summer Olympics – Men's marathon" — all confirm Windsor Castle as the 1908 start point and the royal-family request as the reason for the extra distance, standardized to 42.195 km by the IAAF in 1921.

Mission 03 · Medium · Geography / Navigation + Internet Research

Follow the Blue Line

Answer: Erasmus Bridge
How to get there

The self-drawn route map shows the course crossing a bridge twice, at roughly km 3 and km 27, with real Rotterdam landmarks (Euromast, Cube Houses / Kubuswoningen, Markthal, Kralingse Bos) marked around it. A search for "Rotterdam marathon route bridge crossed twice" or "Rotterdam marathon Erasmus Bridge" confirms the Erasmus Bridge — nicknamed "The Swan" for its curved white pylon — is crossed at approximately those two points on the real NN Marathon Rotterdam course.

Why this is the only answer

Only one bridge on the real course is documented as being crossed twice at those two specific kilometer marks; other landmarks on the map (Euromast, Markthal, etc.) are visited once and are not bridges.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: multiple sources (course-guide sites, marathon-preview articles) independently confirm "the Erasmus Bridge, Rotterdam's iconic 'Swan,' is crossed twice — around 3km and again near 27km," and that the course starts at the foot of the Erasmus Bridge (Schiedamsedijk) and finishes on the Coolsingel. The bridge itself (completed 1996, designed by Ben van Berkel) is well documented as Rotterdam's most recognizable landmark.

Mission 04 · Medium · Food & Health

Fuel Check

Answer: 3
How to get there

Each gel provides 23g of carbohydrate. 60 ÷ 23 = 2.6..., so 2 whole gels only provide 46g (not enough to reach 60g). 3 whole gels provide 69g, which meets the "at least 60g" requirement. So the minimum number of whole gels needed is 3.

Why this is the only answer

The question requires a WHOLE number of gels reaching AT LEAST 60g — 2 gels (46g) falls short, and any number below 3 falls short, so 3 is the unique minimum.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: sports-nutrition sources (e.g. The Running Channel's marathon fuelling guide) confirm the widely used target of "60 to 90 grams of carbohydrate per hour" for marathon runners, and that typical energy gels contain roughly 20-25g of carbohydrate per packet — the 23g figure used here sits squarely in that real, verified range.

Two Messages, One Scam

Answer: B
How to get there

Message A names a real, well-known Dutch charity (KWF Kankerbestrijding), links to what reads as the charity's own domain, and offers a normal automatic receipt — no red flags. Message B shows several classic, well-documented scam red flags at once: false urgency ("reply within 1 HOUR", "LIMITED chance"), an unsolicited "you've been chosen" prize framing, a request to pay via GIFT CARDS, and a shortened/disguised link (bit.ly) instead of a real organization's own website.

Why this is the only answer

Gift-card payment requests and countdown-style urgency are specifically named by consumer-protection authorities as hallmark charity-scam tactics — Message B has multiple objective, verifiable red flags, while Message A has none. This gives exactly one defensible answer, not a matter of opinion.

Source / fact used

Mission 08 · Hard · Dutch Language

De or Het?

Answer: parcours
How to get there

de hardloper (the runner — a person), de wedstrijd (the competition/race), de medaille (the medal) all take "de". "Parcours" (the route/course) is the odd one out and takes "het" — het parcours.

Why this is the only answer

Verified in Dutch dictionaries: "parcours" is uniquely neuter ("het") among the four listed words, all others are common-gender ("de").

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against woorden.org and general Dutch dictionary usage examples: "de hardloper," "de wedstrijd," "de medaille," and "het parcours" (e.g. "Het parcours was voer voor de tijdritspecialisten").
