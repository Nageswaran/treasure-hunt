The Marathon Day Mixup

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 80-100 minutes (spread across a few sittings if you like)

This morning, at the NN Marathon Rotterdam, a whole city gathered to run 42.195 kilometers past the Erasmus Bridge, the Euromast, and the Cube Houses. But right before the starting gun, two lifelong friends grabbed the wrong grey bag in the crowded bag-drop tent.

Noor, an experienced marathoner aiming for a fast finish from Corral B, and Finn, running his very first marathon from the back-of-pack Corral D, accidentally swapped their timing wristbands — the exact device the results computer uses to know who is who, and which corral each runner may enter.

Now the results are scrambled, and the medal ceremony on the Coolsingel is only hours away. The Race Director needs a sharp young detective to set the record straight. Every clue matters, Agent — even the small ones. Good detectives write things down.

MISSION 01
EASY

Race Morning, Step by Step

Six things happen on marathon morning — but our notes got shuffled! Here's what we know: the bag-drop tent closes a full 30 minutes before the starting gun, so every runner drops off their bag early and warms up in whatever time is left before showing their wristband to enter their corral.

Put these six moments in the order they really happen, then type which position (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6) "Show your wristband to enter your starting corral" belongs in:

  • Cross the finish line on the Coolsingel
  • Pin on your bib number at home
  • Warm up near the start area
  • Drop your gear bag at the bag-drop tent
  • Show your wristband to enter your starting corral
  • Hear the starting gun
Hint 1

One of these six things happens at home, long before anyone even reaches the race village. That one has to come first — count carefully from there.

MISSION 02
EASY

Why 42.195 Kilometers?

Every marathon in the world is exactly 42.195 kilometers (26.2 miles) long — but why that oddly specific number? The story starts in ancient Greece, with a legendary run from the town of Marathon to Athens. But the EXACT distance we use today comes from much later: the 1908 Olympic Games in London.

Search online to find out: which royal residence did that 1908 Olympic marathon START at, so young royal children could watch the runners go by from a window? (Two words, both capitalized, like a famous fairytale-sounding building.)

Hint 1

Try searching "1908 Olympics marathon 26.2 miles royal family start". The answer is a real castle you might already know the name of.

📓 Agent's Notebook: While you're here, the Race Director hands you Noor's original registration slip. Her bib number is printed clearly: 2483. Jot that down somewhere safe, Agent — case files like this one have a way of becoming useful again later.

💡 Did you know? The very first Rotterdam Marathon, back in 1981, had just 200 runners at the start line. Today, tens of thousands cross that same finish line on the Coolsingel every year.

Follow the Blue Line

Here's a simplified sketch of today's marathon route, drawn from the timing crew's course notes. It's not to scale, but the landmarks and the river are real.

Follow the red route line from START. Notice it crosses the same bridge twice — once early, once much later. Search online to name that bridge.

Using the map and an online search, name the bridge — nicknamed "The Swan" for its curved white pylon — that the marathon route crosses TWICE: once near kilometer 3, and again near kilometer 27.

Hint 1

This bridge is Rotterdam's most famous landmark and even appears in the city's own logo. Try searching "Rotterdam marathon route bridge crossed twice".

Locals nicknamed it "The Swan" because of its tall, curved, white pylon. What bridge is that?

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Fuel Check

Sports scientists say endurance runners should take in about 60 to 90 grams of carbohydrate (a kind of energy fuel) per hour during a marathon, to avoid running out of energy. Here's the nutrition label from the energy gel packets in Noor's pocket:

PowerRun Energy Gel

Calories100
Total Carbohydrate23 g
Sugars12 g
Sodium60 mg

If Noor wants to reach AT LEAST the low end of that range — 60 grams of carbohydrate — in one hour, using only these gels, how many WHOLE gels does she need to eat that hour, at minimum?

Hint 1

Divide 60 by the grams of carbohydrate in one gel — but remember, she can't eat part of a gel.

Hint 2

60 ÷ 23 isn't a whole number. If she needs AT LEAST 60 grams, does she round that answer up or down?

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Four Friends, One Podium

Grab some scratch paper for this one, Agent — it's a classic logic grid. Four running-club friends — Priya, Sam, Wouter, and Lotte — ran the marathon together and are comparing notes afterward.

Their bib numbers were 3092, 3311, 3577, and 3648 (not in that order), and they finished 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th among themselves (not in that order). Here's what you know:

  1. Wouter's bib number is the lowest of the four.
  2. Among the four friends, Priya, Lotte, and Sam finished in three consecutive positions — in exactly that order: Priya, then Lotte, then Sam, one right after another with nobody else between them.
  3. Wouter did not finish first.
  4. If you add up all four digits of Sam's bib number, they total 21.
  5. Lotte's bib number is exactly 266 higher than Priya's.

Work out who wore which bib and who finished where. What bib number did the friend who finished 2nd wear?

Hint 1

Start with the clue that pins down one bib number exactly — Wouter's is easiest, since it's simply the lowest of the four numbers.

Hint 2

Clue 2 gives you three friends' finishing order as a block of three in a row. Clue 3 tells you where Wouter can't be — that leaves only one place for Wouter. Then use clues 4 and 5 to match names to bibs.

💡 Did you know? Rotterdam's marathon course is one of the flattest big-city courses in the world — its only real "hills" are the two ramps up and over the Erasmus Bridge.

Grab a pencil, Agent: add up all nine splits below to find chip #1847's full net time, from the start mat to the finish mat on the Coolsingel.

Add all the minutes together first, then add all the seconds together separately. Combine them at the end.

MISSION 07
HARD

Two Messages, One Scam

With the timing mess still unsolved, your phone buzzes. Two different messages showed up today, both asking people to support runners' charity fundraisers. Only ONE of them is safe to trust.

Message A — from KWF Kankerbestrijding (the real, official Dutch cancer research fund) "Support Lotte's Marathon Run for KWF Kankerbestrijding! Donate through our official page at kwf.nl/doneren/lotte-rotterdam. Every euro helps fund cancer research, and you'll get an automatic donation receipt by email."
Message B — from "Marathon Runner Fund" "🎉 CONGRATULATIONS! You've been specially chosen to help our Marathon Runner Fund support sick children! Just send €50 in GIFT CARDS to claim your reward. Reply within 1 HOUR or you'll miss this LIMITED chance! Tap here: bit.ly/w1n-fund-now"

Which message should you NOT trust — and report to an adult instead? Type just A or B.

Hint 1

Real charities never ask you to pay using gift cards — that's one of the most common scam tricks there is.

Hint 2

Look closely at the web link in each message. Which one looks like a real charity's own website — and which looks like a shortened, disguised link?

MISSION 08
HARD

De or Het?

Before the medal ceremony, the Dutch race announcer needs help with the program notes. Here are four Dutch running words, all missing their little article word ("de" or "het"):

hardloper · wedstrijd · medaille · parcours

Three of these four words use "de" — but one oddball uses "het" instead. Search a Dutch dictionary if you need to. Which word is the odd one out? Type just that one Dutch word.

Hint 1

Try looking up each word with "de" and "het" in front in an online Dutch dictionary. Words for PEOPLE and words for EVENTS often use "de" — three of these four fit that pattern.

Hint 2

This one is a French loanword that entered Dutch fairly recently. Words borrowed like this often default to "het". Which of the four fits that pattern?

💡 Did you know? The fastest man ever to run the Rotterdam Marathon, Bashir Abdi, finished in 2 hours, 3 minutes, and 36 seconds in 2021 — a pace of under 3 minutes per kilometer, for all 42.195 of them, and his record still stands today.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

Working Backward From the Finish Clock

You've found chip #1847's net time: 3:37:30 (your answer from the splits). Now the timing crew pulls up the official race CLOCK reading for that same chip — the clock that started the instant the gun fired at exactly 09:00:00. It shows chip #1847 crossing the FINISH line when the race clock read 12:52:18.

Here's how the marathon's four starting corrals work (each one entered by wristband, before the gun):

  • Corral A (elite): starts 0–2 minutes after the gun
  • Corral B (fast, aiming for about 3:15–3:45): starts 2–6 minutes after the gun
  • Corral C (aiming for about 3:45–4:15): starts 6–12 minutes after the gun
  • Corral D (aiming for about 4:15–4:45, run/walk pace): starts 12–20 minutes after the gun

Stage 1 of 2

Grab a pencil. First, find the total race-clock time from gun to finish (12:52:18 minus the 09:00:00 start). Then subtract chip #1847's net time (3:37:30) from that. The leftover is how many minutes and seconds AFTER the gun this runner actually crossed the START line. Type your answer as MM:SS.

Hint 1

First find the total race-clock time from gun to finish by subtracting 09:00:00 from 12:52:18.

Hint 2

Then subtract the net time (chip time) from that gun-to-finish total. What's left over is the start delay.

Stage 2 of 2

That start delay places chip #1847 in one very specific corral. But its net time — a blazing 3:37:30 — is far too fast for that corral's typical pace. Remember: Noor (an experienced racer) signed up for the fast Corral B, and Finn (running his very first marathon) signed up for that slow corral. Only one of the two friends could realistically run 3:37:30.

Type the first name of the friend who must have secretly been wearing chip #1847 all along.

Hint 1

Which corral was built for brand-new, first-time marathoners — and does a 3:37:30 net time sound like a first-timer's pace?

Hint 2

If it can't be the friend who truly signed up for that corral, it must be the OTHER friend — the one who mistakenly ended up wearing that wristband.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Medal Ceremony Countdown

Case nearly closed, Agent! You now know chip #1847 (net time 3:37:30) was secretly worn by the friend you named in Mission 9 — not the friend it was originally handed to. The results computer, confused by the swapped wristbands, is showing:

  • Bib #2483: finished in 4:41:12
  • Bib #1847: finished in 3:37:30

But wristbands don't change what's printed on a runner's OWN shirt — only their bib number does that, and you noted someone's real bib number a while back in this case file.

First, on scratch paper, work out the winning margin: subtract the smaller true time from the bigger true time to see by how many minutes and seconds the faster friend won. Then combine that bib number you noted earlier with everything you now know to answer: what is the TRUE bib number of the friend who really finished FIRST?

Type just that bib number.

Hint 1

Which chip did your Mission 9 friend secretly wear? That tells you which raw time — 3:37:30 or 4:41:12 — is really theirs, and whether they're the faster or slower of the two.

Hint 2

Now recall that friend's OWN bib number from earlier in this case file — not a chip serial number, but the real number printed on their own shirt.

Case closed?

You cracked it, Agent! No single clue solved this alone — it took searching, sketching a map, gnarly pencil math, careful logic, and a bib number you jotted down ages ago, all working together. At the medal ceremony, Noor and Finn laugh about the mix-up and cheer each other on anyway, proving that the real magic of race day was never just one fast finisher. It was TEAMWORK — runners, volunteers, and one sharp young detective, all pulling in the same direction.
