Internet Detective Academy · Case File

Dead Air at Riverbend Radio

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60-75 minutes

Every evening at 6:00 pm, Riverbend Community Radio goes on air. But tonight, something is wrong. Static keeps chewing up the signal, and the special guest for the 7:00 pm interview has vanished without a word. The station manager just called you, the Academy's newest detective-in-residence, straight to the studio. Fix the static, find the guest, and get the show back on air before the red ON AIR light goes dark for good.

MISSION 01
EASY

How Does a Voice Become a Broadcast?

A broadcast is a show sent out over the air to many radios at once. Before tonight's show can start, you need to understand how sound leaves the studio and reaches a listener's radio.

Search online for how a radio broadcast travels from a station to a radio. Then put these five steps in the correct order, from what happens first to what happens last:

  • A. The radio wave zooms through the air at the speed of light.
  • B. A DJ speaks into a microphone.
  • C. A listener's radio catches the wave and turns it back into sound.
  • D. The transmitter turns the signal into a radio wave, and the antenna beams it out.
  • E. The microphone changes the sound into an electrical signal.

Type the five letters in the correct order, with no spaces or commas — like ABCDE.

Hint 1

Think about what has to happen first: someone must make a sound before anything else can happen. Which step is the very start?

💡 Did you know? Radio waves travel at the speed of light — about 300,000 kilometers every second. That's fast enough to circle the whole Earth more than 7 times in a single second.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Odd Item in the Equipment Rack

The station manager keeps a shelf of studio items near the control room. Four of them help send tonight's show out over the airwaves. One item does nothing at all for the broadcast.

Here is the list: microphone, mixing board, transmitter, antenna, framed photograph.

Which one does NOT help the signal reach a listener?

Hint 1

Ask yourself: does this item touch the sound, the signal, or the radio wave in any way at all?

📓 Producer's Notebook: Scrawled on the studio whiteboard — "Reminder: the usual Tuesday sponsor slot runs exactly 15 minutes. No more, no less." Worth remembering, Detective.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Coded Note by the Coffee Machine

Taped to the coffee machine is a note written in code:

MIKE UNIFORM SIERRA ECHO UNIFORM MIKE

Search online for the real "NATO phonetic alphabet" — the code pilots and sailors use so words are never misheard over a crackly radio. Use it to turn each code word back into a single letter.

Put the six letters together in order. They spell a place — inside a building.

Hint 1

Each code word stands for exactly one letter. Search "NATO phonetic alphabet" and find the letter that matches each word.

Hint 2

Once you have all six letters, read them in order. They spell a room full of old, important things.

💡 Did you know? The NATO phonetic alphabet — Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, and so on — was finished in 1956. It helps pilots and sailors understand each other clearly, no matter what language they speak.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Building Tonight's Special Rundown

Tonight's broadcast is a special two-hour show, from 6:00 pm to 8:00 pm — different from any regular weekday show. Grab a pencil and paper for this one.

Here are five notes from the producer, jotted down out of order:

  • The opening theme song lasts 3 minutes and starts right at 6:00 pm.
  • Right after the theme song comes the local news, which takes 7 minutes.
  • After the news, a music set plays 5 songs. Each song lasts about 4 minutes.
  • After the music set comes the weather report. It always takes exactly 4 minutes.
  • Right before the interview, there is a 6-minute break for sponsors.

Add up the minutes to find what time the mystery guest's interview segment is supposed to begin.

Hint 1

Work out how many minutes pass before the interview starts. Add every segment's length, in order, starting from 6:00 pm.

Hint 2

3 minutes, plus 7 minutes, plus a music set (5 songs x 4 minutes each), plus 4 minutes, plus 6 minutes. How many minutes is that in total?

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Night Radio Changed Everything

The station manager mutters, "This whole studio owes something to one night, over a hundred years ago."

Search online for: KDKA first commercial radio broadcast election results. Find the year that station broadcast the results of a U.S. presidential election live, before most newspapers could even print them.

Type that year.

Hint 1

Search for the radio station called KDKA, based in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.

Hint 2

The broadcast happened on November 2nd of that year. Search for what major event happened across the United States that same day.

💡 Did you know? In the 1920s, most families did not own their own radio. Many neighbors gathered around one big radio in someone's living room to listen together.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Weekday Afternoon Lineup

Three DJs host the station's regular weekday afternoon shows: Max, Priya, and Sam. Each one hosts one time slot — 4:00, 5:00, or 6:00 — and plays one kind of music — Jazz, Pop, or Rock.

Use these clues to work out who plays what, and when:

  1. The Rock show is not at 4:00.
  2. Priya's show starts right after Max's show.
  3. Sam never plays Jazz.
  4. The Pop show airs at 5:00.
  5. Max does not host the 6:00 slot.

Which DJ hosts the Rock show?

Hint 1

Start with clues 1 and 4. Together, they tell you exactly which time slot has which kind of music — before you even think about the DJs.

Hint 2

Once you know each time slot's music, clue 2 only allows two possible pairings for Max and Priya. Try both, then use clue 3 to rule one out.

MISSION 07
HARD

How Tall Is the Transmitter Tower?

The chief engineer needs the exact height of the broadcast tower, but nobody can climb it tonight.

At exactly 4:00 pm, you plant a measuring pole that stands 2 meters tall. It casts a shadow 3 meters long. At that same moment, the tower's shadow stretches 42 meters across the parking lot.

The sun hits both the pole and the tower at the very same angle, so their height-to-shadow ratio is equal. Find the tower's true height in meters.

Diagram not drawn to true scale — but the ratio between height and shadow is the same for both.
Hint 1

The pole's height divided by its shadow length gives you a ratio. That exact same ratio applies to the tower.

Hint 2

2 divided by 3 is the same as the tower's height divided by 42. What number, divided by 42, gives that same ratio?

MISSION 08
HARD

Which Sponsor Gets the Better Deal?

Two local businesses want to sponsor tonight's show. The station manager wants to know which one pays less for every minute of airtime — that tells us who is really getting the better deal.

  • WaveTown Bakery pays $180 a week to air their ad in the usual Tuesday sponsor slot.
  • Riverside Bikes pays $150 a week to air their ad in a 12-minute slot on Thursdays.

Figure out the cost per minute for each business. Which one pays less per minute?

Hint 1

You already know how long the usual Tuesday sponsor slot lasts. Check your notes from earlier in the case if you don't remember.

Hint 2

Divide each business's weekly payment by the number of minutes their ad airs. Then compare the two results.

MISSION 09
AHA!

Two Callers, One Truth

The phone rings twice tonight. Two callers phone in with tips about where the missing guest might be. One caller ALWAYS tells the truth. The other ALWAYS lies — every single word. You don't know which is which yet.

Caller 1 — Rick: "Nadia always lies to you, I promise. Also, I saw the missing guest heading into the History Museum earlier tonight."

Caller 2 — Nadia: "Rick and I are actually the exact same kind of caller — either we're both truth-tellers, or we're both liars. And the guest is at the Library, not the museum."

Figure out which caller always tells the truth. Where is the guest really hiding?

Hint 1

Try assuming Rick always tells the truth. Follow what that means for Nadia's two claims — does everything still fit together without breaking a rule?

Hint 2

Now try assuming Rick always lies instead, and follow that all the way through Nadia's claims too. Only one of the two assumptions works without breaking a rule.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Signal Boost Code

You race to the History Museum archive room and find the missing guest — station archivist Mrs. Nomvula Patel — completely lost in an old wartime radio logbook. "I'm so sorry!" she says. "I found something the town forgot about, and I simply could not stop reading." She hands you one last note from the transmitter room to finish the case.

Stage 1 of 2

Taped inside the transmitter panel is the engineer's cheat-sheet: "Signal boost code = (tower height in meters ÷ 4) − 2."

Use the tower height you found earlier tonight to work out today's signal boost code.

Hint 1

You already measured the tower's height using the shadow ratio. Divide that number by 4 first, then finish the rest of the formula.

Stage 2 of 2

On the museum door hangs an old cipher wheel with one rule: "Shift each letter of the scrambled word BACKWARD in the alphabet by the signal boost code."

The scrambled word is: INXHTAJWD

Decode it, letter by letter, to reveal what you have truly done tonight.

Hint 1

For each letter, count backward that many places through the alphabet. I is the 9th letter — count back from there.

Hint 2

The wheel wraps around: if counting back takes you past A, keep going from Z.

Case closed?

The static is gone, the ON AIR light glows steady red, and Mrs. Patel makes it to her interview just in time. Solving tonight's mystery took real discovery — noticing small details nobody else caught, and following them all the way to the truth. Case closed, Detective.
