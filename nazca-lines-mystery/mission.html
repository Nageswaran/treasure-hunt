Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Nazca Lines Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 80-100 minutes
Dr. Elena Quiroga, an archaeologist working near a huge, dry desert, has sent an urgent message to Internet Detective Academy headquarters. A wildly popular online video claims ancient aliens built the giant desert drawings near her dig site — and it is spreading fast, right before her museum's big public talk. Dr. Quiroga needs a sharp detective to dig up the real, verified story before Friday. Crack all ten case files, and the last one will reveal the true value that made this whole discovery possible.
MISSION 01
EASY

Where in the World Is the Desert?

Part 1

Every case starts with a location check. Somewhere in the world, there is a huge, dry desert covered in giant line drawings that are hundreds of years old. Search online to find out: in which country are these giant desert drawings found?

Hint 1

Try searching "giant desert line drawings" or "Nazca desert." The answer is a country in South America.

Part 2

Before your flight leaves, Agent Bakker tests your Dutch. In Dutch, is the word for "the desert" de woestijn or het woestijn? Type the full, correct phrase (both words).

Hint 1

Most Dutch words for a type of land or landscape (de stad, de rivier) use "de," not "het."

💡 Did you know? These desert drawings were named a UNESCO World Heritage Site on December 17, 1994, because nothing else like them exists anywhere in the world.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Ceremonial Plaza Puzzle

Some of the giant desert shapes are not animals at all — they are huge flat trapezoids, wide open spaces where people may once have gathered. Dr. Quiroga's team measured one of these trapezoid-shaped plazas. Find its area in square meters.

A trapezoid's area is the average of its two parallel sides, multiplied by the height: Area = ((side 1 + side 2) ÷ 2) × height.

The dashed line marks the plaza's height. Use it with the two parallel sides to find the area.
Hint 1

Add the two parallel sides together first, then divide by 2, before multiplying by the height.

Follow the Bearing

Many of the desert's long straight lines run dead straight for kilometers, always pointing in one exact compass direction. Dr. Quiroga's field notes say that "Line 7" runs on a bearing of 135°. Use the compass rose below to figure out which direction that is.

Start at N (0°) and count clockwise around the circle in steps of 45°. Where does the arrow point?
Hint 1

N is 0°, NE is 45°, E is 90°... keep counting clockwise by 45° until you reach 135°.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Field Team Logic Grid

Three field researchers — Dr. Alvarez, Dr. Chen, and Dr. Osei — each surveyed one giant drawing, in a different year: 1998, 2005, or 2012. Use the clues below to work out who surveyed which drawing, and when. Grab a pencil and cross off options as you go.

  • The Monkey drawing was surveyed in 2005.
  • Whoever surveyed in 1998 studied the Spider.
  • Dr. Chen's survey year was earlier than the year the Condor was surveyed.
  • Dr. Osei did not survey the Condor.
  • Dr. Chen surveyed the Monkey.

Which researcher surveyed the Spider?

Hint 1

Start with the first two clues — together they tell you exactly which year matches which drawing.

Hint 2

Once you know Dr. Chen surveyed the Monkey (in 2005), only one researcher is left who could have surveyed in 1998.

MISSION 07
HARD

Aliens, or Evidence?

The viral video that worried Dr. Quiroga makes this claim:

CLAIM A (viral video): "These giant drawings can ONLY be seen from airplanes or space — proof that ancient aliens must have made them so they could be seen from the sky."

Archaeologists disagree:

CLAIM B (archaeologists): "The local people made these drawings themselves, roughly 1,400-1,600 years ago, and the shapes can also be seen from nearby hills — no flying required."

Use a trustworthy source — a museum, an encyclopedia, or a science website, not a random blog or video — to check one specific fact: is it true that these giant drawings can also be seen from nearby hills, without flying at all? Type YES or NO.

Hint 1

Try searching "Nazca lines visible from hills" on a reputable site.

Hint 2

The archaeologist who first spotted these drawings in 1927 found them while hiking up a nearby hill — not from an airplane.

💡 Did you know? Extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence. So far, nobody has ever found real proof of aliens visiting the Nazca desert — just a very old, very human building project.

MISSION 08
HARD

Camp Schedule Conundrum

Four visiting specialists — Priya, Marco, Yuki, and Freya — each arrived at camp on a different day (Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday), each studied a different drawing (Whale, Tree, Hands, or Parrot), and each carried different equipment (drone, ladder, camera, or tape measure). This one takes some careful pencil-and-paper work — a small grid can help.

  • Marco arrived exactly two days after Priya.
  • Whoever arrived on Tuesday studied the Hands drawing.
  • The Parrot researcher arrived on Thursday.
  • Freya studied either the Hands drawing or the Parrot drawing.
  • Yuki arrived earlier in the week than Freya.
  • Yuki's equipment was the camera.
  • Priya's equipment was the drone.
  • The Whale researcher arrived earlier in the week than the Tree researcher.
  • The Thursday visitor did not use the ladder.

Which specialist studied the Parrot drawing?

Hint 1

The first clue only allows two possible day-patterns for Priya and Marco. Use the fourth clue to rule one pattern out completely.

Hint 2

Once you know Priya arrived Monday and Marco arrived Wednesday, the "Yuki arrived earlier than Freya" clue tells you exactly who gets Tuesday and who gets Thursday.

Stage 2 of 2

Dr. Quiroga uses a modern field-notebook code with her team: a KEYWORD CIPHER. Take your Stage 1 number. Count that many words into this sentence — that word is today's secret keyword:

"every sharp detective studies clues before revealing mystery"

To build the cipher: write your keyword first (drop any repeated letter, keeping only its first appearance), then write the rest of the alphabet afterward in order, skipping any letters already used. Here is the finished cipher for today:

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ
MYSTERABCDFGHIJKLNOPQUVWXZ

To DECODE a secret message, find each letter of the message in the bottom row, then read the letter directly above it in the top row. Decode this message from Dr. Quiroga:

BQHMI BMITO

Type the two decoded words.

Hint 1

Count the words in the sentence one by one, out loud — small words like "before" count too.

Hint 2

For each letter of BQHMI BMITO, search for it in the bottom row of the cipher table and write down the letter sitting right above it.

"You once looked up how many different animal and bird shapes a certain scientist mapped by hand, all across this desert. Take that number, divide it by 3, then count that many words into this sentence — that word names the true reward of this whole case:"

Case closed?

The desert doesn't just hold giant drawings — it holds WISDOM: the reward of patient people who check the evidence carefully before jumping to wild conclusions. Well done, Agent. The case of the Nazca Lines is officially closed.
