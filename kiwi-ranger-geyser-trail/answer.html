Mission 01 · Easy

Welcome to the Valley

Answer: Pōhutu (Pohutu Geyser)
How to get there

Search for "largest geyser Southern Hemisphere Rotorua" or "tallest geyser New Zealand". Pōhutu Geyser, in the Whakarewarewa Thermal Valley within Te Puia (Rotorua, North Island), is the largest geyser in the Southern Hemisphere. It erupts up to 20 times a day and can reach heights of up to 30 metres.

Why this is the only answer

No other geyser in the Southern Hemisphere is described this way — "largest/tallest active geyser in the Southern Hemisphere" is Pōhutu's specific, verifiable claim to fame, and every source agrees on the up-to-20-times-a-day / up-to-30-metre figures.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against multiple sources including Wikipedia's "Pōhutu Geyser" article and tourism sites (GreatSights, AA Travel, Te Puia). Confirmed: erupts up to 20 times/day, heights up to 30 m, located in the Whakarewarewa Thermal Valley within Te Puia.

Mission 02 · Easy

The Missing Page

Answer: kiwi
How to get there

All four facts (flightless, nocturnal, huge egg relative to body size, nostrils at the beak tip) point toward New Zealand's flightless native birds. The kākāpō (a flightless, nocturnal parrot) also fits "flightless" and "nocturnal" — but only the kiwi has its nostrils at the very tip of its beak; it's the only bird in the world with this feature, which rules out the kākāpō and confirms kiwi.

Why this is the only answer

The nostril-at-the-beak-tip fact is unique to kiwi among all birds, so it single-handedly resolves the kākāpō/kiwi ambiguity created by the other three (shared) facts.

Source / fact used

Audubon and Smithsonian confirm the kiwi's egg-to-body-weight ratio (up to ~20-24% of body weight) is the largest of any bird. Mission 07 · Hard

The Valley's True Name

Answer: Whakarewarewa
How to get there

The valley's full Māori name is "Te Whakarewarewatanga o Te Ope Taua a Wāhiao," meaning "the gathering place for the war parties of Wāhiao." It is shortened locally to "Whakarewarewa" (often further shortened to "Whaka" in casual speech, but the puzzle asks for the one-word name). The Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao people trace their line back to the Te Arawa people who settled the valley in 1325; after Mount Tarawera's 1886 eruption, Ngāti Wāhiao took in the displaced Tūhourangi people, and the two became known together as Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao.

Why this is the only answer

All three clues (the meaning "gathering place for the war parties of Wāhiao," the 1325 Te Arawa settlement, and the 1886 Tarawera merger of Tūhourangi and Ngāti Wāhiao) are specific, verified facts that only describe Whakarewarewa — no other valley or village in the region matches all three.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search: whakarewarewa.com ("About Us" / "Whakarewarewa's Unique History" pages) and general search results confirm the full name and its meaning, the 1325 Te Arawa settlement date, and the 1886 Tarawera eruption/Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao merger. Presented as real, sourced history — no fictional ritual or "code" attributed to Māori culture.
