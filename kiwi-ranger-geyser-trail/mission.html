Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Kiwi Ranger's Geyser Trail

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes
Last night, a huge storm rolled over a geothermal reserve on New Zealand's North Island and knocked out the ranger station's power. When it came back on, the digital release records for tonight's young kiwi chick were completely scrambled — and the chick MUST be set free at exactly the right spot before dark, or it will spend a dangerous extra day in its travel box. The head ranger has called you in: an Internet Detective who can rebuild the day's trail from paper clues, radio static, old maps, and a few numbers worth remembering. Follow the trail, mission by mission, and find the gate before nightfall.
MISSION 01
EASY

Welcome to the Valley

All the head ranger remembers before the power went out is this: the reserve sits inside a famous geothermal valley near the city of Rotorua, on New Zealand's North Island. That valley is home to the tallest, most active geyser in the whole Southern Hemisphere — one that can blast boiling water up to 30 metres into the air and can erupt as often as 20 times in a single day.

Search online to find this famous geyser's name. Type just its Māori name — one word, no need to add the word "geyser".

Hint 1

Try searching something like "largest geyser Southern Hemisphere Rotorua". The valley it stands in has a Māori name starting with "Wh".

MISSION 02
EASY

The Missing Page

Before the storm, the reserve's field guide had a page about one very special bird — the one tonight's chick belongs to. The storm-soaked page tore off, leaving only four true facts behind:

• It cannot fly. Its wings are tiny and almost useless.
• It is nocturnal — active at night, and asleep through the day.
• It is the ONLY bird in the entire world with its nostrils at the very tip of its beak, which it uses like a nose to sniff out food underground.
• Compared to its own body size, it lays one of the biggest eggs of ANY bird on Earth — sometimes close to a fifth of its own body weight.

Careful — New Zealand also has another famous flightless, nocturnal bird: the kākāpō, a giant parrot. It shares some of these facts too. Only ONE of the four facts above is true for this mystery bird and NOT for the kākāpō. Use that fact to be sure. What bird is this?

This photo was taken right in the Rotorua area — look closely at where its beak meets its face.
Hint 1

Flightless AND nocturnal fits more than one New Zealand bird — but only one of them has a nose built into the very tip of its beak.

💡 Did you know? A mother kiwi's egg can weigh up to a fifth of her own body weight — the largest egg of any bird on Earth, compared to body size.

Using the measurements marked on the fence-line, work out the TOTAL AREA of ground protected inside the fence, in square metres.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Retrace Your Steps

Grab a pencil for this one — it's a job for paper, not just your head. Here is today's boardwalk trail, leg by leg, copied from the ranger's paper log:

Leg 1: Ranger Station → Silica Terrace Edge: 240 m
Leg 2: Silica Terrace Edge → Bubbling Mud Pools: 185 m
Leg 3: Bubbling Mud Pools → Pōhutu Geyser Deck: 310 m
Leg 4: Pōhutu Geyser Deck → Kiwi Conservation House: 265 m
Shortcut: Kiwi Conservation House straight back to Ranger Station: 150 m

It's getting dark, and kiwi are waking up for the night, so you must hurry back before you disturb them. Walk the whole outbound trail (all four legs) to reach the Kiwi Conservation House, then turn around and take the shortcut path straight back to the Ranger Station. In total, how many metres did you walk, there and back?

Hint 1

Add the four outbound legs together first to get one big number, then add the shortcut distance on top of that.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Bearings to the Terrace

The ranger hut has a hand-drawn compass map pinned by the flagpole, showing eight lookout points arranged around it like a compass rose:

North (000°): Redwood Ridge Lookout
Northeast (045°): Steam Vent Point
East (090°): Boardwalk Junction
Southeast (135°): Sacred Terrace View
South (180°): Mud Pool Terrace
Southwest (225°): Silica Terrace Edge
West (270°): Visitor Gate
Northwest (315°): Old Kauri Grove

Stand at the flagpole facing due North (bearing 000°). Turn clockwise 225°. Walk straight ahead in that new direction until you reach a lookout point. Which lookout point do you arrive at? Type its full name.

📔 Ranger's Notebook (pinned to the corkboard by the flagpole): "Entry No. 114 — release tag for the last chick we set free before the storm hit. Small numbers like this are easy to lose track of, but worth holding onto."
Hint 1

Turning clockwise from North: 45° = NE, 90° = E, 135° = SE, 180° = S... keep counting up by 45°s to see exactly where 225° lands.

💡 Did you know? Rotorua is nicknamed "Sulphur City" because of the rotten-egg smell caused by hydrogen sulphide gas rising naturally out of the ground.

MISSION 06
HARD

Radio Static

Old ranger radios still use a classic trick to keep messages private: every letter is shifted FORWARD 3 places in the alphabet (A becomes D, B becomes E, and so on). Spaces stay exactly where they are. A crackly message just came through the ranger hut's radio:

WKH FKLFN ZDLWV DW WKH ROG NDXUL JURYH

Shift each letter BACKWARD 3 places to decode it, and type the location it names (lowercase is fine).

Hint 1

Try it on the first word only: W, K, H — shift each letter back 3 places in the alphabet and see what three-letter word appears.

Hint 2

Since A was shifted forward to become D, going backward D becomes A. Work through it letter by letter — the message is five words long, and the last three name a place in the forest.

MISSION 07
HARD

The Valley's True Name

Long before any ranger station stood here, this valley had another name — one still used today. Piece it together from these real, true clues:

• Its full Māori name honours a warrior named Wāhiao, and means "the gathering place for the war parties of Wāhiao".
• Local guides trace their whakapapa (ancestry) back to the Te Arawa people, who first settled this valley in the year 1325.
• In 1886, when nearby Mount Tarawera erupted and buried the land of another tribe, Tūhourangi, the people of this valley took them in — and the two peoples became one, known today as Tūhourangi Ngāti Wāhiao.

Locals usually shorten the valley's long full name down to one word. What is it?

Hint 1

The shortened name starts with "Wh" — it's also the name of a living Māori village you can visit today, right next to the geyser you found in Mission 1.

Hint 2

Search for "living Māori village Rotorua Te Puia" and look for a one-word valley/village name.

💡 Did you know? The National Kiwi Hatchery near Rotorua has hatched more than 2,600 kiwi chicks since 1995, and released over 1,000 of them back into the wild.

MISSION 08
HARD

Who Saw the Kiwi?

Four rangers each patrolled a different part of the reserve this morning, and each spotted a different native bird. Use the four clues below to work out who spotted the kiwi.

Rangers: Aria, Manaia, Koro, Briar
Birds spotted: kiwi, kea, tūī, kārearea (New Zealand falcon)
Locations patrolled: Fern Gully, Redwood Ridge, Boardwalk Junction, Steam Vent Point

1. Manaia spotted the kea at Redwood Ridge.
2. The ranger who saw the kārearea was patrolling Boardwalk Junction.
3. Aria wasn't at Steam Vent Point or Fern Gully.
4. Briar never patrolled Fern Gully and didn't see the tūī.

Which ranger spotted the kiwi?

Hint 1

Start with clue 1 — it hands you a ranger, a bird, AND a location all at once, for free.

Hint 2

Once you know where Aria must be (clue 3), clue 2 tells you exactly what Aria saw. That leaves only two rangers and two locations — clue 4 finishes the job.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

Storm-Scrambled Numbers

Stage 1 of 2

The ranger's tablet is completely fried, but she remembers most of what mattered. Find the release-tag number you noted earlier today — it's easy to overlook, but you wrote it down. Subtract from it the maximum number of times Pōhutu can erupt in one busy day (you found this fact all the way back on your very first case).

What number do you get?

Hint 1

Look back through your case file for a small tag number pinned to a corkboard note, then subtract the eruption number you found in Mission 1.

Stage 2 of 2 (final)

That number is a gate number! Match it to the ranger's gate list below to find exactly where you need to report before dusk:

Gate 58 — Steam Vent Deck
Gate 76 — Fern Gully Gate
Gate 94 — Dusk Release Platform
Gate 103 — Redwood Overlook

Using your Stage 1 number, where do you need to report?

Hint 1

Find the gate number that matches your Stage 1 answer exactly — no rounding needed.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Gate

Tonight, four rangers are each stationed at a different gate, each tracking a different animal, carrying a different piece of equipment, and working a different shift. Work out which gate YOU should report to — the one where a ranger is about to release a young kiwi chick.

Rangers: Ihaka, Nia, Toby, Marama
Animals being tracked: kiwi chick, tuatara, wētā, longfin eel
Equipment: torch, binoculars, thermal thermometer, radio
Shift times: 6 AM, noon, 6 PM (dusk), 9 PM
Gates: Redwood Overlook, Fern Gully Gate, Steam Vent Deck, Perseverance Point

1. Marama works the dusk (6 PM) shift and always carries a torch.
2. The kiwi chick is released at dusk.
3. Ihaka starts the earliest shift, at 6 AM, and is the one tracking the tuatara.
4. Toby has the last shift of the day, at 9 PM.
5. Toby does not study insects.
6. The ranger tracking the tuatara uses binoculars to watch it bask on rocks.
7. Toby radios in his eel counts from the river.
8. The tuatara-watcher reports to Redwood Overlook.
9. The wētā researcher's gate has the word "Fern" in its name.
10. The eel-counter's gate overlooks the steam vents.

Which gate should you report to? Type just the special word in that gate's name (skip the word "Point").

Hint 1

Lock down the four shift times first — clues 1, 3, and 4 place three rangers immediately, and the fourth ranger simply gets whichever time is left over.

Hint 2

Once every ranger has an animal (tuatara goes to Ihaka; clue 5 rules out wētā for Toby, so Toby gets the eel and Nia gets the wētā), match each animal to its gate using clues 8, 9, and 10 — whichever gate is left over is your answer.

Case closed?

Even with every digital file scrambled by the storm, you never gave up — you rebuilt the whole day's trail out of scraps of paper, crackly radio static, an old compass rose, and your own memory. That's perseverance: the very quality tonight's kiwi chick will need too, taking its first steps into the wild. Case closed, Agent.
