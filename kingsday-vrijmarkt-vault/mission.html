The King's Day Vrijmarkt Vault

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes

It's Koningsdag (King's Day)! Your whole street is orange, and the neighborhood vrijmarkt — a giant sidewalk market where anyone can sell secondhand things — is in full swing. Every stall is donating part of today's earnings to the local library's brand-new children's reading corner.

But near closing time, the market organizer, Meneer de Wit, comes running over, notebook flapping in his hand. In all the chaos, the cashbox lock's combination and the day's donation numbers got completely mixed up. He needs a sharp Junior Detective — that's you — to visit each stall, solve what went wrong, and rebuild the day's earnings before the cashbox has to be handed to the library tonight.

🔎 Junior Detective Notebook

Before the market opened, Meneer de Wit mentioned something worth remembering: the donation jar already had €14 sitting inside it — left over and forgotten from last year's Koningsdag collection. Jot this number down somewhere. You won't need it right away, but you will need it later today.

MISSION 01
EASY

Sorting the Stall Signs

Your first stop is the sign-painting table, where handwritten price signs for the vrijmarkt got mixed up. Every Dutch word needs either "de" or "het" in front of it, and someone painted six signs without checking which word goes with which.

Here are the six words: fiets, boek, spel, speelgoed, jas, tas.

Not sure about one of them? It's fine to search online for a "de of het" checker (type the word plus "de of het") — even Dutch grown-ups do this sometimes!

How many of these six words use "het" (not "de")? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Say each word out loud with "het" in front of it. Three of the six sound right that way — the other three sound right with "de" instead.

MISSION 02
EASY

Whose Birthday Is It, Anyway?

A little kid at the toy stall asks you: "Wait, WHY do we even have a vrijmarkt today?" You realize you should know this for sure before you keep investigating.

Search the internet: Koningsdag (King's Day) is celebrated every year on 27 April. Whose birthday does it celebrate? Type the King's name the way the Dutch usually write it: his first name, a hyphen, then his second name, with no spaces (for example: "Firstname-Secondname").

Hint 1

Search "Koningsdag" — the very first sentence of most results explains whose birthday it marks.

💡 Did you know? All that orange you see today comes from the House of Orange-Nassau, the royal family whose name and colors have been tied to the Netherlands for over 400 years.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Lemonade Stand's Digital Display

The lemonade stand has a little digital display for keeping count. It's not a real scannable code of any kind — the stallholder just made up a simplified digital code for fun, using binary: a way of writing numbers using only 0s and 1s.

Here's how it works. Each position in a 4-digit binary number stands for a value: from left to right, the positions are worth 8, 4, 2, and 1. Wherever there's a "1", you add that position's value. Wherever there's a "0", you skip it.

The display shows this code: 1 1 0 1

What regular number does this binary code stand for? Type just the number.

Hint 1

Line the four digits up under the values 8, 4, 2, 1, and only add up the columns where the digit is a "1".

Hint 2

The digits are 1-1-0-1, matched to 8-4-2-1. That means you add 8, 4, and 1 — and skip the 2.

💡 Did you know? The number system computers use today, binary, was first written about by a mathematician named Gottfried Leibniz, all the way back in 1679 — long before any computer existed!

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Two Messages by the Donation Box

Taped to the donation box are two printed messages. One is real. One is a scam someone tried to sneak in. Before you decide, do a quick search online for "why you should never share your PIN code" — real shops, markets, and banks never ask for it, not even by text message. Keep that rule in mind.

MESSAGE A

"Thank you for supporting the library's new reading corner! You can hand your coins to any stallholder, or scan the stall's payment sign yourself. Every euro helps buy new books for the corner."

MESSAGE B

"‼️URGENT‼️ Vrijmarkt Bonus! Send your bank PIN code to www.koningsdag-geld-bonuss.nl within 5 minutes to DOUBLE your donation!!! Act now, before it's too late!!!"

Which message is the scam — A or B? Type just the letter.

Hint 1

Real charity collections never ask you to send your PIN code anywhere.

Hint 2

Look for words that rush you to act fast, and look closely at the web address — does it look like a normal, trustworthy site, or a strange, made-up one?

MISSION 06
HARD

Measuring the Stage Tent

The stage crew needs to know exactly how much orange fabric covers the front of their tent, for tomorrow's cleanup report. The front of the tent is shaped like a rectangle with a rounded awning (a semicircle) on top, like the picture below. Grab a pencil for this one.

The awning's flat edge sits exactly on top of the rectangle, matching its width exactly — that's the semicircle's diameter.

What is the total area of the tent's front, in square meters? Use π ≈ 3.14. Type just the number (for example: 12.34).

Hint 1

Work out the rectangle's area and the semicircle's area separately, then add them together.

Hint 2

Rectangle area = width × height. Semicircle area = (π × radius × radius) ÷ 2, using the 4 m radius shown.

MISSION 07
HARD

Four Stalls in a Row

Along Tulip Street there are exactly four stalls in a row, numbered 1 to 4 starting from the market entrance. Each stall belongs to one person, sells one type of item, and stands in one position. Use only these four clues — no searching, just logic — to figure out the whole row:

  1. The cupcake stall is immediately to the right of the plant stall (one position further from the entrance).
  2. Anna's stall sells cupcakes.
  3. Daan's stall is the very last stall in the row, and he sells neither plants nor books.
  4. Cato's stall is closer to the entrance than Anna's stall, and Cato does not sell plants.

The four items are: books, toys, plants, cupcakes. The four owners are: Anna, Bram, Cato, Daan.

What does Cato's stall sell? Type just the item name.

Hint 1

Start with clue 2 — it hands you one full answer for free. Then use clue 3 to place Daan and his item.

Hint 2

Once Anna sells cupcakes and Daan (at position 4) sells toys, only books and plants are left for Bram and Cato. Clue 4 tells you which one Cato does NOT sell.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Opening the Cashbox

It's almost 16:00 — closing time. Meneer de Wit sets the cashbox on the table in front of you. Its lock needs one number to release: the day's final total in euros, the very same number you just worked out.

Type the final cashbox total to open the vault.

Hint 1

You already solved this in the last mission — check the final answer you worked out there.

Hint 2

It's the jar's starting €14, plus all four stall totals from Mission 9, added together one more time.

Case closed?

The lock clicks open. Underneath the coins, Meneer de Wit finds a small folded note in his own handwriting, written that morning: "Every euro here becomes a book on the new reading corner's shelves — thank you to whoever helps make sure it all gets counted right." Taking the time to carefully rebuild someone else's mess, for a cause that helps the whole neighborhood, is its own kind of kindness. Case closed, Junior Detective — the library thanks you.
