Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Neon Arcade Championship

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-90 minutes

Pixel Peak Arcade is throwing its biggest night ever: the Neon Arcade Championship, where today's best players battle it out under buzzing marquee lights for a spot on the Wall of Fame. But an hour before the trophy ceremony, disaster strikes — Jamie Rook's legendary all-time high score on the Barrel Boss cabinet, the number every player here has chased for years, has vanished from the record book.

All that's left are old scoresheets, faded stickers, and scraps of arcade trivia scattered around the building. Head Judge Alvarez needs a sharp-eyed detective to piece the true record back together before the crowd finds out. That detective is you.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Blinking Bonus Board

Right by the token counter, the Bonus Streak light board is doing its pre-championship show. It blinks one number at a time before it resets for the night:

3 → 6 → 12 → 24 → ?

What's the next number in the pattern?

Hint 1

Look at how each number relates to the one right before it — what simple operation turns 3 into 6, and 6 into 12?

💡 Did you know? The word "arcade" comes from the Latin word arcus, meaning "arc" or "bow." It first meant a covered walkway lined with arches — and only much later came to mean a place full of games!

MISSION 02
EASY

The Arcade's Oldest Cabinet

Tucked in the corner, past all the newer cabinets, sits Pixel Peak's very first machine — battered, but still glowing. A small plaque says it was inspired by one of the very first arcade video games ever made, a simple two-paddle game called Pong.

Search online to find out: in what year was Pong first released?

🗒️ Case File: While you're poking around this corner, something else catches your eye — a brass plaque screwed to the front door reads: "Pixel Peak Arcade — Est. 1984." Worth jotting down before you move on.
Hint 1

Try searching something like "Pong video game release year." Reliable sites like an encyclopedia or a well-known history site will all agree on the same year.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Two Deals at the Token Counter

A hand-lettered sign hangs above the token counter:

  • ROOKIE BUNDLE — 50 tokens for $12.00
  • CHAMPION BUNDLE — 90 tokens for $20.00

But a smaller sign taped underneath adds one more detail: "Champion Bundle purchases include a $2.50 wristband fee."

Work out the TRUE cost per token for each bundle (total cost divided by number of tokens). Which bundle actually gives you more tokens for your money? Type just the bundle name: Rookie or Champion.

Hint 1

Don't just compare the sticker prices — add the wristband fee into the Champion Bundle's total cost first.

Hint 2

Rookie: $12.00 ÷ 50 tokens. Champion: ($20.00 + $2.50) ÷ 90 tokens. Compare the two results in cents per token.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Technician's Shorthand

Every cabinet at Pixel Peak has a number, but the old maintenance technician liked to write them in binary code, just for fun. A note taped inside his toolbox reads:

"The cabinet you need: 1011 in binary."

Convert that binary code into an ordinary decimal number. Which cabinet number is he pointing you to?

Hint 1

Binary place values, from right to left, are 1, 2, 4, 8. Add up the place values wherever there's a 1 in the code.

Hint 2

1011 breaks down as (1×8) + (0×4) + (1×2) + (1×1).

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

The Trivia Wall Argument

On the arcade's trivia wall, two regulars have scribbled rival claims in different colors of marker:

  • CLAIM A (red pen): "Pac-Man was made by Nintendo."
  • CLAIM B (blue pen): "Pac-Man was made by Namco."

Search online to find out which claim a reliable source actually confirms. Type A or B.

Hint 1

Try searching "who created Pac-Man" and check an official-sounding or well-known source, not just a random comment.

Hint 2

Nintendo is famous for Donkey Kong and Mario — but Pac-Man came out of a different Japanese game company entirely.

💡 Did you know? Many classic arcade games only let you type 3 letters for your high-score initials. One big reason was to stop players from spelling rude words on the screen for everyone in the arcade to see!

MISSION 06
HARD

The Torn Scoresheet

In a drawer under the trophy case, you find Jamie Rook's original scoresheet from the record run — torn at one corner, but still readable. It lists five round scores from that legendary game on the Barrel Boss cabinet:

  • Round 1: 8,450
  • Round 2: 7,225
  • Round 3: 9,930
  • Round 4: 6,140
  • Round 5: 8,755

Grab a pencil for this one — add up all five rounds to find the Round Subtotal. Type the number only, no commas.

Hint 1

Add them two at a time to keep it manageable: Round 1 + Round 2 first, then keep adding one round at a time.

Hint 2

Round 1 + Round 2 = 15,675. Add Round 3 to get 25,605, then Round 4, then Round 5.

MISSION 07
HARD

Four Finalists, One Afternoon

This year's semifinal round is done. Four players each played a different cabinet and got a different final score. Using only these clues, figure out who played the Barrel Boss cabinet:

  • Ben did not play Barrel Boss or Cosmic Defenders.
  • The Maze Muncher player scored exactly 12,300 points.
  • Ben scored more points than the Maze Muncher player.
  • The player who scored 21,400 points did not play Rocket Rally or Maze Muncher.
  • Cleo did not play Maze Muncher.
  • Diego's score was not 9,800.
  • Ava scored fewer points than Cleo.
  • The Cosmic Defenders player scored fewer points than the Barrel Boss player.

Players: Ava, Ben, Cleo, Diego. Cabinets: Rocket Rally, Maze Muncher, Cosmic Defenders, Barrel Boss. Scores: 9,800 / 12,300 / 15,750 / 21,400.

Who played Barrel Boss?

Hint 1

Start with Ben — two of the clues about him combine to pin down his exact cabinet before anything else.

Hint 2

Once you know Ben plays Rocket Rally, his score must be more than 12,300 but can't be 21,400 either — only one number is left for him.

💡 Did you know? In 1981, a real arcade in Ottumwa, Iowa started the very first official scoreboard for tracking the world's best arcade high scores. It was called Twin Galaxies, and adjudicators still use records like it to verify big scores today.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Maintenance Sticker

Stage 1 of 2

You flip the Barrel Boss cabinet around and find a yellowed maintenance sticker stuck to the back panel. The old technician — the same one who loved binary — wrote a single number on it:

1001

What number is that in decimal?

Hint 1

Same trick as Mission 4: place values 8, 4, 2, 1 from left to right. Add up the ones with a 1 underneath.

Stage 2 of 2

Underneath the sticker, in faded pencil, is a note: "Perfect Climb Bonus = sticker number × 100 points."

Work out that bonus, then add it to the Round Subtotal you calculated in Mission 6 to find Jamie Rook's true final Barrel Boss score. Type the number only, no commas.

Hint 1

First multiply your Stage 1 number by 100 — that's the bonus. Then add it to Mission 6's Round Subtotal.

Hint 2

Stage 1's number × 100 = the bonus points. Add that bonus to the exact Round Subtotal you wrote down after solving Mission 6.

MISSION 09
AHA!

How Many Ways Could It Have Gone?

Head Judge Alvarez leans in with a grin. "Before I show you the trophy, one more puzzle for you. This year's 4 semifinalists — the ones you identified a couple missions back — were competing for exactly 3 podium spots: gold, silver, and bronze. Any of the 4 could have finished in any of those 3 spots, with no repeats."

"How many different possible podium orders were there in total, before anyone knew the real scores?"

Hint 1

Break it into three separate choices: how many players could take gold? Once gold is picked, how many are left for silver? Then bronze?

Hint 2

4 choices for gold, then 3 left for silver, then 2 left for bronze. Multiply those three numbers together.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The True Championship Score

One last thing before the trophy gets engraved. Pixel Peak has an old tie-breaker tradition for any reconstructed record: search online to find out what year Space Invaders — the oldest style of game once played here — first released in Japan.

Then count how many years passed between that year and the year Pixel Peak itself opened. (Check your Case File notes from earlier in the case!) That small number is the tie-breaker bonus.

Add the tie-breaker bonus to the true Barrel Boss score you found in Mission 8 to reveal the ONE TRUE FINAL CHAMPIONSHIP HIGH SCORE that goes on tonight's trophy. Type the number only, no commas.

Hint 1

Try searching something like "Space Invaders release year Japan." Reliable sources all agree on one year.

Hint 2

Your Case File note about when Pixel Peak opened has the other number you need. Subtract the earlier year from the later year to get the tie-breaker bonus.

Case closed?

The engraver's tool buzzes to life, stamping a fresh number onto the brass plate above Jamie Rook's name. Judge Alvarez shakes your hand: nobody solved this by being the fastest — you solved it by refusing to give up on a single torn scoresheet, faded sticker, or scribbled note. That's perseverance, and tonight, the whole arcade owes you one. Case closed, Junior Detective.
