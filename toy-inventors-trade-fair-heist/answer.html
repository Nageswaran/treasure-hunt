Mission 03 · Medium (Geometry)

The Foam-Lined Crate

Answer: 384 (square centimeters)
How to get there

Split the L-shape into two rectangles. Full outer rectangle: 24 cm × 18 cm = 432 cm². The missing notch (top-right corner): 8 cm × 6 cm = 48 cm². Remaining area = 432 - 48 = 384 cm². (Cross-check by adding the two pieces directly: top-left piece 16 cm × 6 cm = 96 cm², bottom piece 24 cm × 12 cm = 288 cm²; 96 + 288 = 384 cm² — matches.)

Why this is the only answer

Every dimension needed is labeled directly on the diagram, and the two decomposition methods (subtract vs. add) agree, confirming 384 as the unique correct area. The diagram's segment labels are self-consistent: 16+8=24 (top+notch width=bottom) and 6+12=18 (notch height+right edge=left edge).

Source / fact used

Self-contained arithmetic/geometry puzzle, recomputed by hand: 24×18=432, 8×6=48, 432-48=384.

Mission 04 · Medium (Dutch Language)

Sorting the Workshop Shelf

Answer: prototype
How to get there

Full sort: de blauwdruk, het tandwiel, de veer, de uitvinding, het prototype, de werkplaats. Two words take HET: tandwiel and prototype. Of those, tandwiel is a native Dutch compound (its gender follows "wiel" = het wiel, a predictable pattern). "Prototype" is the loanword (from Greek via French/English) whose HET gender surprises many adult Dutch speakers, since it looks like it should follow the same "-e ending = de" pattern as blauwdruk or a word like "auto." That is the answer to type.

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle explicitly asks for "the loanword" among the two HET-words, and only prototype fits that description — tandwiel, veer, uitvinding, blauwdruk, and werkplaats are all native Dutch words/compounds.

Source / fact used

Verified each word individually via Dutch grammar/dictionary sources: welklidwoord.nl/prototype (het prototype), a Reverso Dutch-English corpus search confirming "het tandwiel" (neuter, compound of "wiel"), Reverso/context searches confirming "de blauwdruk," "de veer," and "de werkplaats" (all common gender), and the standard Dutch grammar rule that abstract nouns ending in -ing (like "uitvinding") take "de."

Mission 05 · Medium (History)

The Real Story Behind a Famous Toy

Answer: 1974
How to get there

Hans Beck, a German toy designer working for Geobra Brandstätter, developed a line of small, movable toy figures starting in 1971. They were unveiled to toy buyers at the Nuremberg International Toy Fair in February 1974 — the toy line was Playmobil.

Why this is the only answer

Multiple independent sources (Wikipedia, contemporary obituaries) agree on 1974 as the year the figures were first shown at the Nuremberg fair, distinct from 1971 (when Beck began designing them) or 1975 (when the line went fully international) — the puzzle specifically asks for the year they were "first shown to buyers" at the fair, which is 1974.

Source / fact used

Verified via Wikipedia: Hans Beck and Wikipedia: Playmobil, corroborated by contemporary obituary coverage (Washington Post, Boston Globe, Feb 2009) describing the figures as "launched in 1974" at the Nuremberg toy fair. As a fun aside, Hans Beck's hobby before Playmobil was building lightweight model aircraft with balsa-wood frames — an unplanned real-world echo of Mission 07's material.

💡 Fact check: "The oldest known machine full of gears is the Antikythera mechanism..." — confirmed: a ~2nd-century-BCE Greek geared device recovered from a shipwreck near Antikythera in 1901, containing over 30 bronze gears, used to track astronomical cycles and the timing of events including the ancient Olympic Games. Source: History.com and Scientific American.

Mission 07 · Hard (Financial Literacy)

The Replacement Crate

Answer: Basswood
How to get there

Birch plywood: cost = 5 × €12 = €60 (within €80 budget), weight = 5 × 900 g = 4,500 g = 4.5 kg — exceeds the 3 kg express-courier weight limit, so it's eliminated. Basswood: cost = 4 × €18 = €72 (within budget), weight = 4 × 500 g = 2,000 g = 2 kg (within the weight limit), and basswood is a standard, sturdy hobby/craft wood suitable for repeated handling — passes all three requirements. Balsa: cost = 5 × €9 = €45 (cheapest, within budget), and while it is the lightest of the three (so it would pass the weight limit), it is also famously soft and easily dented/crushed, unsuitable for a display case that will be handled by hundreds of visitors — eliminated on durability grounds. Basswood is the only material passing budget, weight, AND durability simultaneously.

Why this is the only answer

This is a genuine three-constraint trade-off, not a simple "which is cheaper" comparison: the cheapest option (balsa) fails on durability, and the option that might seem obviously durable (plywood) fails on weight. Only basswood clears all three bars at once, and no other combination of numbers in the table supports a different reading.

Source / fact used

Balsa wood's status as the lightest and softest wood sold commercially (specific gravity roughly 0.10-0.15, i.e. about 10-15% the density of water, with very low Janka hardness) verified via The Wood Database and WoodSolutions, both describing it as too soft/fragile for parts subject to regular handling.

💡 Fact check: "Balsa is the lightest and softest wood sold commercially..." — confirmed: specific gravity approximately 0.10-0.15 (about a tenth as dense as water), among the lowest Janka hardness ratings of any commercial wood. Source: wood-database.com.

Mission 08 · Hard (Cryptography + Wordplay, Multi-Stage)

The Note Under the Workbench

Stage 1 answer: bramel
Stage 2 (final) answer: marble
How to get there

Stage 1: The lucky number planted in Mission 02 is 4. "FVEQIP" was created by shifting each letter of the plaintext forward 4 places. Shifting each letter of the ciphertext back 4 places decodes it: F(5)→B(1), V(21)→R(17), E(4)→A(0), Q(16)→M(12), I(8)→E(4), P(15)→L(11) — giving "BRAMEL."

Stage 2: "BRAMEL" is an anagram of "MARBLE" (same six letters: A, B, E, L, M, R). The puzzle text specifies "the name of an ordinary toy," which uniquely points to marble (rather than any other, less common rearrangement) and reveals the hiding place: the marble jar on the windowsill, used in the Grand Finale.

Why this is the only answer

The cipher shift is fully determined by the number planted earlier (4), giving one unique decoded string. The "ordinary toy" framing in Stage 2 rules out any other technically-possible rearrangement of those letters (e.g. "ambler," an obscure word that isn't a toy), leaving marble as the only answer consistent with the clue.

Source / fact used

Self-contained cipher and anagram, verified by hand; the shift key (4) traces back to the "Agent's Notebook" aside in Mission 02.

Every category (order, badge, item) is pinned down through a strict elimination chain with no point where two people remain equally possible for a slot; each of the eight clues is load-bearing and the solution is fully forced.

Grand Finale

The Marble Jar

Answer: rescued
How to get there

Using A=1, B=2, C=3 ... Z=26: 18=R, 5=E, 19=S, 3=C, 21=U, 5=E, 4=D, spelling R-E-S-C-U-E-D = "rescued."

Why this is the only answer

The number-to-letter mapping is fully specified (A=1...Z=26) and deterministic — each number maps to exactly one letter, giving one unique decoded word.

Source / fact used

Self-contained numeric cipher, verified by hand against the standard A=1...Z=26 mapping. Note: the finale's typed answer ("rescued") is deliberately a different word from the reward value itself, so the reward-reveal paragraph below the answer box (always visible on the page) cannot be used to shortcut the puzzle — this matches the pattern used in other missions in this repo, where the vault/lock answer and the narrated reward word are two separate values.

Reward revealed

The value is creativity. It is not the finale's typed answer, but is instead revealed only in the closing reward-reveal narration, which ties it to Pim's resourceful (if panicked) choice to hide the blueprint rather than let it fall into a rival's hands — the case is ultimately solved by piecing together clever, creative thinking rather than a single simple rule.
