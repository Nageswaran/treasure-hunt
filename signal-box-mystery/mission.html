The Signal Box Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 70-90 minutes

Alfred Hooper has kept the Millbrook Junction signal box for thirty-one years. Tonight is the biggest night of his career: a heritage steam special is due to run its anniversary route, and every signal, platform, and timetable has to be exactly right.

But this morning, Alfred didn't open the box. His lamp is still lit. His notebook is still on the desk, half-written. You've been called in as a junior railway detective to follow his notes, timetables, and signal logs — find out what happened to Alfred, and get that train running safely tonight.

MISSION 01
EASY

The Departures Board

Alfred's notebook is open to today's page. In shaky handwriting, it says:

"Whatever else happens — make sure the ANNIVERSARY SPECIAL leaves from the right platform today. She's not the only steam engine getting fired up this afternoon, mind. Don't mix her up with the other one."

Here's today's departures board from Sheffield Park, the station hosting tonight's celebration. Read it carefully — there are two steam services today, not one.

MISSION 02
EASY

Meridian at Sheffield Park

Tucked in the notebook is a scrap of paper with coordinates on it, and nothing else:

50.9956417° N, 0.0010722° W — the real station where tonight's special begins.

Open the map link below. If you can, switch it to satellite view so you can see the station and the river next to it clearly.

This station sits almost exactly on top of one very famous invisible line. That line runs from the North Pole all the way to the South Pole. It passes straight through Greenwich, London — and every time zone on Earth is measured starting from it.

That line has two names: a local one (named after the London area it passes through) and a global one used the world over. Type the global name here — two words, starting with "P".

📍 Open the coordinates in Google Maps
Tip: searching the station's name plus the word "line" will also turn up plenty about it.
Hint 1

Search "Sheffield Park station meridian" if the map alone doesn't spell it out for you.

💡 Did you know? The heritage line hosting tonight's special was the first standard-gauge steam railway in the world to be brought back from closure and reopened for the public — its very first train ran on 7 August 1960.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The First Steam Whistle

A newspaper clipping falls out of the notebook. Alfred had underlined one sentence: "Every steam railway owes something to the very first one."

Search to find out: in 1825, one small steam locomotive pulled the world's very first passenger train on the world's first public railway to use steam engines. That locomotive is still famous today, and it's usually known by one short name.

What is that locomotive's short, well-known name? (One word is enough — you don't need to add "No. 1".)

Hint 1

Search "world's first public railway 1825" and look for the name of the locomotive built by George Stephenson.

Hint 2

Its short name describes exactly what it did that day: it got things moving.

💡 Did you know? On that very first passenger run in 1825, a man on horseback rode ahead of the train, waving a flag to warn people it was coming — trains were so new that nobody quite knew what to expect yet.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Full Length of the Line

Grab a pencil for this one! Tonight, the Anniversary Special will run the entire length of the real heritage line hosting the celebration, from end to end.

Search to find out how many miles long that whole line is, from Sheffield Park to East Grinstead.

She leaves Sheffield Park at 10:30, moving at a steady 22 miles per hour the whole time she's running. Partway along, she stops for exactly 12 minutes to take on water. Counting the water stop, what time does she reach East Grinstead? Answer in 24-hour time, like 13:05.

Hint 1

First work out how long the moving part of the journey takes: distance ÷ speed. Then add the water stop on top.

Hint 2

The line's real length divides evenly by 22 — that's not an accident.

MISSION 05
HARD

Flags in the Fog

Under a loose floorboard, you find one more page from Alfred's notebook. Instead of words, it's covered in little drawings — pairs of flags, each pointing a different way. Signalmen used to send whole messages like this, one letter at a time, long before radios and phones.

Agent's Notebook — worth remembering: whatever this message says, it's clearly something Alfred wanted nobody to forget.

Each drawing below shows a signalman holding a dark-red flag in his RIGHT hand and a brass-yellow flag in his LEFT hand. Every letter is a different pair of clock positions. Use the key to work out each letter, then read the message.

🔑 Flag key

Dark red = right-hand flag. Brass yellow = left-hand flag. Positions are given as clock times (12 = straight up, 6 = straight down, 3 = out to that side, and so on).

📩 The coded message

Decode all ten flag-pairs using the key above. The dashed line marks a gap between two words. Type the full message below, both words, with a space between them.
Hint 1

Go icon by icon. For each one, find the matching (right, left) pair in the key above — that pair only ever means one letter.

Hint 2

The second word isn't a digit — it's a number spelled out in letters.

💡 Did you know? On old railway signals, the "all clear" position didn't show a green light like you might expect — at night it showed a plain white light. Green originally meant "caution," not "go"!

MISSION 06
HARD

The Ticket Clerk's Dilemma

At the ticket window, a family of four is stuck. They need help deciding what to buy for today's trip: two adults and two children, riding out to the celebration and straight back — one round trip, no more.

The clerk lays out two options:

OptionWhat it costsWhat it covers
Individual tickets£9 per adult, £5 per childOne round trip only
Family Day Rover£32 flat, whole familyUnlimited rides, all day long

The clerk grins: "The Rover sounds brilliant, doesn't it — unlimited rides! But work out the sums the old-fashioned way before you decide what this family should buy."

This family is only taking one round trip today. Work out the cost of individual tickets for all four of them, and compare it to the Family Day Rover. How many pounds would they save by picking the cheaper option? Type just the number (no £ sign).

Hint 1

Add up 2 adult tickets and 2 child tickets first. Then compare that total to £32.

Hint 2

"Unlimited rides" is only worth paying extra for if you're actually going to ride more than once.

MISSION 07
BIG "AHA!"

The Vanished Departure

The very first page of Alfred's logbook is torn out — the page that should show this morning's original departure time from Oakford. But the later pages survived, all in a jumble. Nobody wrote down any other clock time except the very last one.

Read all five scraps below. Work backward from the one time you know, undoing each step, until you reach the missing departure time. (Working forward won't help — you don't have a starting time to work forward from!)

"Millbrook Junction, 4:15 pm — she rolled in right on the nose, whistle blowing, crowds cheering."

"Before the final approach, there's always a compulsory 10-minute signal check just outside the junction. Happens every single run, rain or shine."

"The last stretch of track, from Ferndale Halt to the junction, takes exactly 25 minutes at running speed."

"And the very first leg, from Oakford, always takes 45 minutes when everything runs to plan — which, my note says, it did today."

"At Ferndale Halt, the fireman always takes on water — a fixed 15-minute stop, timetabled to the minute."

What time did the Anniversary Special originally leave Oakford this morning? Answer in 24-hour time, like 09:15.

Hint 1

Start from 4:15 pm and undo the LAST thing that happened before arrival first. Which scrap describes that?

Hint 2

Order of events, earliest to latest: leave Oakford → arrive Ferndale Halt → water stop → leave Ferndale Halt → signal check → arrive Millbrook Junction. Subtract each time, one at a time, from the end.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Signal Shed

Following the last of Alfred's clues, you find him — safe, if a little cold and very glad to see you. He'd climbed out to check a stuck signal lever last night, slipped in the dark, and twisted his ankle. He's been sheltering in the old signal shed ever since, unable to walk back, keeping himself busy the only way he could: with his notebook and his flags.

There's just one thing left to do before the Anniversary Special can run: unlock the signal shed's old parts cabinet, where the spare lamp oil is kept, so tonight's train has light in every lantern.

Stage 1 of 2

The cabinet has an old combination lock. Alfred calls out the rule: "It's the number of the signal from my coded notebook page — multiply it by the six carriages on tonight's Anniversary Special."

What two-digit code unlocks the cabinet?

Hint 1

Somewhere earlier in your case notes, a coded message spelled out a signal's number. Scroll back and find it.

Hint 2

The code is that signal number, multiplied by 6.

Stage 2 of 2

The cabinet swings open. Taped inside the lid is a numbered list, worn soft from years of Alfred's thumb running over it — words he says every good signalman needs:

1. Curiosity   2. Patience   3. Courage   4. Honesty   5. Discovery   6. Focus
7. Creativity   8. Bravery   9. Perseverance   10. Teamwork   11. Kindness   12. Wisdom

Take your two-digit code from Stage 1. Add its two digits together to get a single number. Find the word at that number on Alfred's list. That word is your final answer.

Hint 1

If your Stage 1 code was, say, 26, you'd add 2 + 6 = 8.

Hint 2

Add the digits of your actual Stage 1 code, then count down the list to that number.

Case closed?

Alfred never once gave up on that stuck signal, even alone in the cold and the dark — and you never gave up following his trail. Tonight, the Anniversary Special runs exactly on time, lanterns lit, whistle blowing. That's what perseverance looks like. Case closed, Agent.
