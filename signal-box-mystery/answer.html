Mission 02 · Easy · Navigation & Orientation

Meridian at Sheffield Park

Answer: prime meridian
How to get there

The coordinates (50.9956417° N, 0.0010722° W) are the real coordinates of Sheffield Park railway station, the southern terminus and headquarters of the real Bluebell Railway in East Sussex, England. The station sits almost exactly on the Prime Meridian (0° longitude) — the imaginary north-south line that passes through Greenwich, London, and from which all of the world's time zones are measured. Locally it's often called the "Greenwich Meridian"; its globally used name is the "Prime Meridian."

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle explicitly asks for the "global" name, two words, starting with "P" — this steers the child to "Prime Meridian" specifically rather than the equally correct but differently-worded "Greenwich Meridian." If your child types "Greenwich Meridian," that's a correct understanding — just gently point them at the requested wording.

Source / fact used

Sheffield Park railway station's location on the Prime Meridian is documented on Wikipedia: Sheffield Park railway station and confirmed via thegreenwichmeridian.org, which lists a meridian marker at this location. Verified via web search on 2026-08-07.

Mission 03 · Medium · History & Civilizations

The First Steam Whistle

Answer: locomotion
How to get there

The Stockton and Darlington Railway opened on 27 September 1825 as the world's first public railway to use steam locomotives for passenger traffic. The locomotive that pulled that first passenger train was built by George and Robert Stephenson and is known today as "Locomotion No. 1" (originally named "Active"). It's commonly shortened to just "Locomotion."

Why this is the only answer

The puzzle explicitly says "one word is enough," steering the child to "Locomotion" rather than the full "Locomotion No. 1." Both are correct in full, but the hashed answer accepts the single word as instructed.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against Wikipedia: Locomotion No. 1 and Wikipedia: Stockton and Darlington Railway, both consistent with the Institution of Civil Engineers and Historic England summaries. Verified on 2026-08-07.

Mission 04 · Medium · Mathematics (pen-and-paper) + Internet Research

The Full Length of the Line

Answer: 11:12
How to get there

The real Bluebell Railway is 11 miles (17.7 km) long end to end, from Sheffield Park to East Grinstead. At a steady 22 mph, the moving time for the whole 11 miles is 11 ÷ 22 = 0.5 hours = 30 minutes, regardless of exactly where along the route the water stop happens (total moving time only depends on total distance and speed). Add the 12-minute water stop: 30 + 12 = 42 minutes total elapsed. Starting at 10:30, add 42 minutes: 10:30 + 0:42 = 11:12.

Why this is the only answer

The numbers are chosen so 11 ÷ 22 divides evenly (0.5 hours), avoiding fraction ambiguity. The puzzle explicitly says the 22 mph is "the whole time she's running," so the water stop is clearly additional to (not part of) the moving-speed calculation.

Source / fact used

The 11-mile (17.7 km) length of the Bluebell Railway (Sheffield Park–East Grinstead) is documented on Wikipedia: Bluebell Railway. All departure times, speeds, and the water stop are fictional, invented for this puzzle. Verified on 2026-08-07.

Mission 06 · Hard · Financial Literacy

The Ticket Clerk's Dilemma

Answer: 4
How to get there

Individual tickets: 2 adults × £9 = £18, plus 2 children × £5 = £10, total £28. The Family Day Rover costs a flat £32. Since the family only needs one round trip (not unlimited rides), individual tickets are cheaper. Savings = £32 − £28 = £4.

Why this is the only answer

The "hidden catch" is that the Family Day Rover's headline feature — unlimited rides all day — has no value to a family making exactly one round trip, so its higher flat price isn't offset by any extra usage. £28 is unambiguously less than £32, giving one forced correct comparison and one forced savings amount.

Source / fact used

Both ticket options and prices are fictional, invented for this puzzle — not real current fares for any real heritage railway, deliberately avoiding a "current prices" fact that could go out of date.

Mission 07 · Big "Aha!" · Logical Reasoning — Advanced (working backward)

The Vanished Departure

Answer: 14:40
How to get there

Chronological order of events (the child must reconstruct this from context clues, since the scraps are given out of order): depart Oakford → (45 min run) → arrive Ferndale Halt → (15 min water stop) → depart Ferndale Halt → (25 min run) → reach the junction distant signal → (10 min signal check) → arrive Millbrook Junction at 16:15 (4:15 pm).

Working backward from the one known time, 16:15: subtract the 10-minute signal check → 16:05. Subtract the 25-minute Ferndale-to-junction run → 15:40. Subtract the 15-minute water stop → 15:25. Subtract the 45-minute Oakford-to-Ferndale run → 14:40. That is the original departure time from Oakford.

Why this is the only answer

No starting time is given anywhere in the clues — only the final arrival time and four fixed durations describing the legs between it and the (unknown) start. The only way to reach a number is to chain all four durations backward, in the correct order, from the single known time. Reversing any one step or applying them out of order produces a different, clearly "wrong-feeling" time, and there is only one order consistent with the scraps' own internal descriptions (e.g. "the very first leg, from Oakford" and "the last stretch... to the junction").

Source / fact used

Entirely a constructed logic/arithmetic puzzle — no external fact needed. Hand-verified by working the chain forward from 14:40 to confirm it reproduces exactly 16:15: 14:40 + 0:45 = 15:25 (arrive Ferndale) + 0:15 (water stop) = 15:40 (depart Ferndale) + 0:25 (run) = 16:05 (reach distant signal) + 0:10 (signal check) = 16:15 (arrive junction). ✓
