The Secret of Muiderslot

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60-75 minutes

Muiderslot is a real, 700-year-old moated castle in the Netherlands, built by Count Floris V. While unpacking old books for a new exhibit, the museum's curator found a folded, yellowed note hidden inside a book that once belonged to the poet P.C. Hooft, who lived in the castle in the 1600s. The note hints that something has stayed hidden inside these walls since the count's own lifetime.

The curator needs a sharp young detective to research the castle's true history, crack the old codes, and follow the clues to whatever the note is pointing to — before the museum's reopening this weekend. Grab a notebook, open a search tab, and get started, Agent!

MISSION 01
EASY

The Count's Blueprint

The curator explains that Muiderslot sits exactly where a river meets the water, and that it was first built for a Count of Holland named Floris V. But nobody on the tour can remember the exact year the count had it built.

Search online to find out: in what year did Count Floris V have Muiderslot built?

Hint 1

Search for "Floris V Muiderslot built" and look for the year on a trusted history or museum page.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Guard's Vocabulary Test

An old wooden sign near the water ring around the castle walls uses the Dutch word for that ring of water (in English, we call it "the moat").

Search online for the Dutch word used for a castle's moat. Once you find the word, every Dutch noun needs the right little word in front of it — either "de" or "het". Which one goes with this word?

Type just the article: de or het.

Hint 1

Try searching "Dutch word for moat" or "gracht castle Netherlands" — Dutch city guides use this word all the time for their canals too.

💡 Did you know? Muiderslot stands exactly where the river Vecht flows into what used to be the Zuiderzee sea. Long ago, every ship that sailed past had to stop and pay a toll to the count!

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

The Courtyard Measurement

Inside the castle walls is a stone courtyard. The old castle keeper sketched its shape for the curator: a rectangle with a smaller square corner cut away, where the base of one tower pokes into the yard.

Using the measurements on the sketch below, work out the total area of the courtyard in square meters.

Split the shape into two simple rectangles before you calculate.
Hint 1

Try drawing one straight line across the shape to turn it into two ordinary rectangles.

Hint 2

Find the area of the whole big rectangle (as if the corner wasn't cut away) first, then subtract the area of the small cut-away corner.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Two Guards at the Fork

Deep under the castle, a stone passage splits into two doors: Door A and Door B. A guard stands at each one. The old castle log says that exactly one of the two guards always tells the truth, and the other always lies — every single time, no exceptions.

You already know for a fact that one of the two doors truly leads to the hidden passage the note is pointing to.

The guard at Door A says: "Door A leads to the hidden passage."

The guard at Door B says: "Both doors lead only to danger — neither one leads to the hidden passage."

Which door should you choose?

Hint 1

You already know for certain that one door really does lead to the passage. So can the guard at Door B possibly be telling the truth?

Hint 2

If the guard at Door B must be lying, that tells you exactly which guard is the truth-teller — and what the truth-teller said.

💡 Did you know? The poet and writer P.C. Hooft lived at Muiderslot for almost 40 years in the 1600s. He invited so many famous writers, painters, and thinkers to visit that historians later nicknamed the group "de Muiderkring" — the Muiden Circle.

MISSION 05
HARD

The Coded Note

Tucked between the pages of P.C. Hooft's old book, the curator finds the note the legend keeps talking about. It isn't written in letters at all — it's written in dots and dashes.

Decode the message below to find out what the old legend says is hidden in the castle.

.--. .- ... ... .-    --. .
Hint 1

Search "Morse code alphabet" for a chart if you need one. A dot is a short signal, a dash is a long one — each letter has its own pattern.

Hint 2

The message is one single English word, seven letters long. It's the kind of word a detective loves to find in an old castle!

MISSION 06
HARD

The Betrayal of Count Floris

The note also hints at the count's own tragic story. Not long after Muiderslot was built, some of Floris V's own nobles turned against him, captured him, and held him prisoner inside his own castle. It didn't end well for the count.

Search online to find out: in what year was Count Floris V murdered — the very event that led to Muiderslot being destroyed and later rebuilt?

Hint 1

Try searching "Floris V murdered year" or "Floris V Count of Holland death."

Hint 2

It happened just a few years after Mission 1's answer — the castle barely got to stand for a decade before disaster struck.

💡 Did you know? Muiderslot is now an official national museum that anyone can visit — more than 700 years after Count Floris V first had it built.

MISSION 07
BIG AHA

The Shields of the Knights' Hall

In the castle's Knights' Hall, painted shields line the wall. Some show the golden lion of Holland — the emblem the Counts of Holland (including Floris V) used on their own coat of arms: a red lion on a gold background. Others show a different symbol from a different noble house entirely.

Study the sketch of the wall below very closely. How many shields show the golden lion of Holland (gold background, red lion shape)?

Count carefully — the shields aren't in a simple repeating pattern.
Hint 1

Look at the background color of each shield first: gold, or blue?

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Last Page of the Logbook

One final page is folded inside the note. It's written in the same kind of shifted code the castle keepers used to protect their secrets: every letter has been pushed forward through the alphabet by a fixed number of steps.

The keeper's rule: shift each letter of the coded word back through the alphabet by the same number as the lion shields you counted in Mission 7.

GLVFRYHUB

Hint 1

Go back to your Mission 7 answer — that number is your shift. For example, with a shift of 2, the letter Z shifted back becomes X.

Hint 2

The decoded word describes exactly what a good detective loves to make, every time they crack a case.

Case closed?

Every clue you followed — the count's blueprint, the coded note, the guards, the shields — was really one long act of discovery. The curator hangs your case file in the Knights' Hall as proof: Muiderslot's oldest secret was never really lost. It just needed a detective curious enough to go looking.
