Mission 01 · Easy · History & Civilizations, Internet Research

The Count's Blueprint

Answer: 1285
How to get there

A search for "Floris V Muiderslot built" or "when was Muiderslot built" leads to the castle's official page and several history sites, which all state that Count Floris V of Holland had the castle built around 1285, at the spot where the river Vecht meets the water.

Why this is the only answer

Multiple independent, reputable sources (the castle's own official site and independent history sites) agree on this year, with no competing date in circulation for the original construction.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against muiderslot.nl (official, "Floris V en het Muiderslot") and historiek.net, both stating Floris V had the castle built around 1285.

Mission 02 · Easy · Dutch Language, Internet Research

The Guard's Vocabulary Test

Answer: de (the Dutch word is "gracht")
How to get there

Searching "Dutch word for moat" or "gracht castle Netherlands" turns up "gracht" as the Dutch word for a moat or canal. "Gracht" is a common-gender noun in Dutch, so it takes the article "de," not "het" — i.e. "de gracht."

Why this is the only answer

Dutch de/het pairings are fixed per word, not a matter of opinion; every Dutch dictionary and grammar reference lists "gracht" as a de-word. The app only checks for the single word "de" (typed alone), so the child should type just the article, not the full phrase.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against multiple Dutch grammar/lidwoord-checker sites (e.g. welklidwoord.nl, volkabulaire.nl) and the WikiWoordenboek entry for "gracht," all confirming "de gracht."

Mission 03 · Medium · Mathematics (Geometry)

The Courtyard Measurement

Answer: 180 (square meters)
How to get there

Split the L-shaped courtyard into two rectangles. Treat it as one large 16 m × 12 m rectangle (192 m²) with a 4 m × 3 m corner notch removed (12 m²): 192 − 12 = 180 m². The diagram's labeled sides also self-check: the 12 m top segment plus the 4 m notch step equals the 16 m bottom (12 + 4 = 16), and the 3 m notch drop plus the 9 m remaining side equals the 12 m left side (3 + 9 = 12) — confirming the picture and the numbers agree.

Why this is the only answer

The six labeled measurements fully determine one specific shape and area; any other decomposition (e.g. splitting the L differently) produces the same 180 m² total.

Source / fact used

Self-contained math puzzle — the diagram was drawn to these exact numbers and the answer was recomputed independently via the shoelace formula on the polygon's own coordinates (which returned exactly 360/2 = 180), confirming the SVG and the stated dimensions match.

Mission 04 · Medium · Logical Reasoning (Knights & Knaves)

The Two Guards at the Fork

Answer: Door A
How to get there

We're told as fact that exactly one door truly leads to the passage. The guard at Door B claims "neither door leads to the passage" — but that directly contradicts the given fact, so this statement is false no matter which door is correct. A guard who says something false must be the liar, so the guard at Door B is definitely the liar. That makes the guard at Door A the truth-teller. The truth-teller at Door A said "Door A leads to the hidden passage" — so that statement is true, and Door A is the answer.

Why this is the only answer

This is forced, not guessed: Door B's guard's statement is false under any possible assignment of which door is correct, which pins down "Door B = liar, Door A = truth-teller" unconditionally, which in turn pins down "Door A = passage" with no remaining branch to check. Solved independently from only the two stated clues plus the given fact, confirming exactly one forced solution.

Mission 05 · Hard · Cryptography & Secret Codes

The Coded Note

Answer: PASSAGE
How to get there

Decoding the Morse code letter by letter: .--. = P, .- = A, ... = S, ... = S, .- = A, --. = G, . = E, spelling P-A-S-S-A-G-E.

Why this is the only answer

Morse code is a fixed, unambiguous standard — each letter has exactly one pattern of dots and dashes, so there is only one way to decode this message.

Source / fact used

Verified each letter's Morse pattern (P = .--., A = .-, S = ..., G = --., E = .) via web search against standard Morse code alphabet references.

Mission 06 · Hard · History & Civilizations, Internet Research

The Betrayal of Count Floris

Answer: 1296
How to get there

A search for "Floris V murdered year" leads to the well-documented story: in 1296, a group of nobles Floris V had humiliated (including Gijsbrecht van Amstel and Gerard van Velsen) kidnapped him during a hunting trip and held him prisoner at Muiderslot. When they tried to move him, he was stabbed to death. Muiderslot was destroyed shortly after in the unrest that followed, and rebuilt decades later.

Why this is the only answer

The year 1296 is consistently reported across independent historical sources (Wikipedia, regional Dutch history sites, and museum-affiliated pages); there is no competing date for this event.

Source / fact used

Verified via web search against the English Wikipedia entry for Floris V, Count of Holland, and the-low-countries.com, "The Murder of Floris V."
