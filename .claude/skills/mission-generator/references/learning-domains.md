# Learning Domains

Pick 4-6 of these per mission set, and rotate the combination across mission sets for the same user so the adventures don't feel samey. Each one lists a few example puzzle seeds — use these as inspiration, not a script to copy verbatim (always verify any fact-based seed with `web_search` before using it for real).

## 🇳🇱 Dutch Language
de vs het words, spelling patterns, grammar, synonyms and opposites, word categories.
*Seeds: "Is it 'de' or 'het' [woord]?" sorting puzzle; unscramble a Dutch word from letters found in clues; find the odd-one-out in a list of synonyms.*

## ➕ Mathematics
arithmetic, percentages, fractions, dates and timelines, prime numbers, number patterns, estimation, geometry and measurement.
*Seeds: a discount/percentage puzzle tied to a "shop" in the story; a number-pattern lock combination; calculating how many days/years between two real historical dates; find the area or perimeter of a composite shape (e.g. an L-shaped garden made of two rectangles, or a room with a rectangular alcove) drawn as a labeled diagram — see "Diagrams" in SKILL.md for how to draw these; find a missing side length given a shape's total perimeter or area; compare the area of two differently-shaped regions to see which is bigger.*

## 🧩 Logical Reasoning
The richest domain here — deliberately tiered so difficulty can track the mission curve (Foundational for Easy/Medium, Intermediate for Medium/Hard, Advanced for Hard/the "Aha!" slot). Rotate which specific mechanic *and* tier you draw from across mission sets for the same child, not just which domain. Every logic puzzle still obeys the skill's core rule — solvable purely by reasoning from what's given, with exactly one logically forced answer, no outside lookup, no lucky guessing — **except** the one item flagged below that can't follow that rule by design.

**Foundational** (Easy/Medium):
- Pattern & sequence recognition — number sequences (2, 4, 8, 16...), shape/color patterns, "what comes next and why."
- Classification & categorization — sort by shared attributes, spot the odd-one-out, or a two-circle Venn diagram for overlapping categories (e.g. animals that are both mammals *and* live in water).
- Simple deductive reasoning (syllogisms) — "All cats have tails. Felix is a cat. Does Felix have a tail?"
- Analogies — "Hand is to glove as foot is to ___."
- Conditional (if-then) logic — "If it rains, the fair closes early. It's raining. What happens?"
- Process of elimination — a mystery-style puzzle where clues rule out suspects/objects one at a time (this is detective-story logic, and maps directly onto the mission's own framing).
- Spatial reasoning — mazes, mental rotation ("what does this shape look like flipped or rotated?"), map reading, mirror/symmetry puzzles.
- Sequencing & cause-effect — put story events in the right order, predict the outcome of a simple scenario, follow a "because" chain.

**Intermediate** (Medium/Hard):
- Logic grids — the classic "who owns the red bike, who lives in which house," 3-4 clues, solved by crossing off options one by one.
- Truth-tellers and liars (knights and knaves) — one character always lies, one always tells the truth; the child reasons about which statements are consistent with each other.
- Critical evaluation of information — spot the inconsistency in a story, tell a reliable clue from a red herring, compare two "witness" statements for a contradiction (overlaps with Critical Thinking & Media Literacy below — use whichever framing fits the story better, don't do both in one set).

**Advanced / stretch** (best for the Hard slot or the "Aha!" moment in an 8-10 mission set; don't force these into every mission set — use when you want real depth):
- Multi-constraint logic grids — 4-5 categories instead of 3, where a clue only becomes useful once combined with two others. A real jump in working-memory demand over the intermediate version.
- Three-way truth-tellers, liars, and "sometimes" tellers — add a character who alternates or answers randomly, which forces reasoning about *reasoning itself* ("if he says X, and he's the type who sometimes lies, then...").
- Formal connectives: AND / OR / NOT / XOR — a puzzle that hinges on "or" meaning "one or the other, or both" versus "exactly one" (XOR); e.g. "the treasure is under the oak tree OR the well — but not both."
- Proof by contradiction (informal) — "assume the opposite is true — what breaks?" Have the child test an assumption and find where it collapses, instead of only deducing forward.
- Combinatorics and counting logic — "how many different ways can 4 friends line up?" or "how many possible codes exist if...?" This is systematic enumeration, a different muscle than pattern-spotting.
- Probability/uncertainty reasoning — a "what's more likely, given these clues?" comparison with one clearly correct answer (not a raw probability calculation) — most detective-puzzle logic assumes full certainty; this is the deliberate exception.
- Game theory basics — a simple strategic puzzle ("if you pick first, what's the smartest move knowing your opponent will react?"); Nim-style take-away games with a forced winning strategy work well and are genuinely hard to solve without working backward.
- Working backward (backward induction) — solvable only by starting from the goal state and reasoning back to the start, rather than forward from the clues. A distinct skill from forward deduction.
- Nested conditionals — "if A, then (if B then C, else D)" — layered if-then logic rather than a single step. An escape-room "if you did X, go to Y, unless Z" branch builds this naturally.

**Not a scored puzzle — an optional aside only:** self-referential statements and paradoxes ("this statement is false") deliberately have no clean answer, which conflicts with this skill's exactly-one-correct-answer rule. If you use one at all, present it the same way as a "Did you know?" fact — a no-answer-box curiosity between missions (e.g. a locked box the Carnival Master left with a note that just *is* a paradox, meant to be talked through with a parent, not solved for points) — never as one of the numbered missions.

*Decomposition — breaking a big problem into smaller solvable steps — isn't a puzzle type on its own; model it in how Hint 1 is phrased for any Hard/Advanced puzzle ("what's the first small thing you'd need to figure out?").*

## 🌍 Geography & World Knowledge
countries and capitals, flags, rivers and mountains, continents, time zones, distances, maps.
*Seeds: identify a country from its flag/outline; use a real map to measure a distance; figure out what time it is somewhere else in the world.*

## 🔬 Science & Nature
space, planets, animals, weather, ecosystems, the human body, scientific discoveries.
*Seeds: order the planets by a given property; identify an animal from a set of true facts; a simple weather-reading puzzle.*

## 💻 Technology & Computers
binary numbers, QR codes, internet basics, AI and robots, computer history, digital problem-solving.
*Seeds: decode a short binary number into a letter/number; explain what a QR code is, then "scan" one described in text; spot the odd device out across computer history.*

## 🔐 Cryptography & Secret Codes
Caesar cipher, Morse code, substitution ciphers, emoji codes, hidden messages, multi-step decoding.
*Seeds: a short Caesar-shifted message revealing the next clue; a Morse code snippet; an emoji-to-word substitution key.*

## 💰 Financial Literacy
money, discounts, savings, budgets, comparing prices, value and trade-offs.
*Seeds: comparing two "deals" to find the better value; a savings-goal math puzzle; spotting a hidden fee.*

## 🧭 Navigation & Orientation
compass directions, coordinates, route planning, maps, Google Maps, Street View.
*Seeds: follow compass directions from a starting point on a real map; use Street View to find a landmark described in clues; plan the shortest route between two points.*

## 🍎 Food & Health
nutrition, food labels, healthy choices, food origins.
*Seeds: read a real nutrition label and answer a question about it; figure out which country a dish originates from.*

## 🏛️ History & Civilizations
Ancient Egypt, Romans, Vikings, inventions, Dutch history, historical timelines.
*Seeds: put historical events in order on a timeline; identify an invention from clues about its inventor and era; a Dutch-history "who am I" riddle.*

## 🎨 Art & Culture
famous paintings, monuments, architecture, festivals, music.
*Seeds: identify a famous painting/monument from an image clue; match an artist to their most famous work.*

## 📰 Critical Thinking & Media Literacy
compare sources, identify official websites, verify facts, determine trustworthy information.
*Seeds: given two "claims," figure out which one a quick search confirms is true; identify which of two websites is the official/more trustworthy one for a topic.*

## 🛡️ Internet Safety & Digital Citizenship
strong passwords, phishing, privacy, scams, safe internet behaviour.
*Seeds: spot the red flags in a fake "you won a prize!" message; rank passwords from weakest to strongest and explain why.*

## 🏠 Everyday Life Skills
reading timetables, understanding weather forecasts, planning trips, measurements, following instructions.
*Seeds: read a (fictional but realistic) train timetable to answer a question; convert between units of measurement as part of a recipe or building clue.*
