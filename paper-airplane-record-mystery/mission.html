Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Folded Truth

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 60-75 minutes

Tomorrow, the Skyfold Junior Aviators Club attempts something huge: beating the real, current Guinness World Record for the farthest paper-airplane flight ever thrown. Tonight, everything went wrong. The folding instructions were found shuffled out of order, a key page is missing, and a strange website is spreading a message claiming someone already broke the record — trying to make the club give up before they even try.

Coach Alders has called the Internet Detective Academy. Repair the instructions, uncover the real facts, and expose whatever is spreading false information — before tomorrow's attempt is ruined for good.

MISSION 01
EASY

Four Forces, Mixed Up

The coach's laminated "Four Forces of Flight" poster fell in a puddle last night, and its four labels floated away. Here's what's left, unlabeled:

Force 1: Earth pulling the plane straight down toward the ground, all flight long, whether the plane likes it or not.

Force 2: The push from your arm the instant you launch the plane forward. Without it, nothing happens at all.

Force 3: Fast-moving air above the wing and slower air below create a pressure difference that pushes the wing upward.

Force 4: Air rubbing against every surface of the plane, always pushing backward, opposite to the direction it's flying.

Which of the four forces of flight (lift, gravity, thrust, or drag) is Force 4 describing? Type just that one word.

Hint 1

Think about sticking your hand out of a moving car window — the air pushing back against your hand is the same kind of force acting on the plane.

💡 Did you know? The paper we fold into airplanes is almost 2,000 years old. Ancient papermakers in China used old fishnets, mulberry bark, and rags — no trees needed!

MISSION 02
EASY

The Half-Burned Note

Under the workshop table, you find a scorched scrap of paper. Someone had written the missing instruction step in Dutch, but the middle word got scrambled by the fire:

"Stap 3: eerst moet je het papier in de lengte N E W U O V."

Unscramble those six Dutch letters to find the missing word. In Dutch, this word means "to fold" — exactly what every paper-airplane maker needs to do first. Type the unscrambled Dutch word.

Hint 1

Say the letters out loud in different orders. In Dutch, "ou" sounds like the "ow" in "cow." What do you do to a sheet of paper before it becomes a plane?

Agent's Notebook — worth remembering: While digging around, you learn that the current record-breaking distance throw in Shanghai wasn't a first-try miracle. The young record-holders needed eight throws that didn't count before their ninth throw finally made history. Jot that down — it might matter again later in this case.

The Wingspan Blueprint

Tomorrow's plane is a symmetric glider, folded flat from nose to tail. Each wing forms a right triangle: the front fold line (from the nose to the wingtip) measures 15 cm, and along the spine, the wing panel runs 12 cm before it meets the wingtip's perpendicular edge. The diagram below shows one wing — the dashed line shows where the matching wing folds on the other side of the spine.

Find the missing distance from the spine to the wingtip, then use it (and its mirror image) to find the plane's total wingspan, tip to tip, in centimeters. Grab a pencil for this one — type just the number.
Hint 1

The triangle has a right angle where the wing panel meets the spine (see the little square marker). Try the Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c².

Hint 2

Once you've found the distance from the spine to one wingtip, remember the plane has two wings, mirrored across the spine — the total wingspan is measured tip to tip.

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Who Throws Tomorrow?

Three club members are getting ready for tomorrow's attempt: Mateo, Elena, and Jonas. Each one has exactly one job: Designer, Folder, or Thrower. From the club's planning notes:

1. Mateo is not the Thrower.
2. Elena is either the Designer or the Folder — never the Thrower.
3. Jonas is not the Folder.
4. The Designer is not Mateo.

Use elimination to figure out who will throw the plane tomorrow. Type just that person's name.

Hint 1

Start with who definitely CAN'T be the Thrower — cross those names off first. Only one name should be left.

Hint 2

Once you know the Thrower, only two roles remain for the other two people. Use the Designer clue to decide who gets which of those two.

💡 Did you know? One of the most famous paper-airplane distance records, set in 2012 in California, was thrown by Joe Ayoob — a former college quarterback. He didn't fold the plane himself; his friend John Collins designed and folded it, and Ayoob just supplied the arm!

MISSION 05
HARD

Setting the Bar

Stage 1 of 2

Before you can spot a fake record claim, you need to know the real one. Search online to find: in which country was the current, Guinness-certified record for farthest paper aircraft flight set? (This is the up-to-date record standing today, not an older one from 2012 or 2022.) Type just the country name.

Hint 1

Try searching for "farthest paper airplane flight world record" and check the most recent date you can find, not the oldest.

Stage 2 of 2

Now search "[the country you just found] paper airplane distance record 2025" — using that exact country name narrows your search to the right record. Find the exact distance in meters, then convert it into feet: multiply the number of meters by 3.28, and round to the nearest whole foot. Grab a pencil for this one — type just the number of feet.

Hint 1

The record distance is a number with two decimal places, in meters. Multiply it by 3.28 first, then round.

Hint 2

The distance is close to 100 meters, but not quite there. Round your final feet answer to the nearest whole number — no decimals.

MISSION 06
HARD

Three Statements, One Truth

Last night, three helpers stayed late to prep tomorrow's planes: Priya, Sam, and Noor. Somebody accidentally swapped pages 3 and 4 of the folding instructions. When Coach Alders asked what happened, here's what each of them said:

Priya: "I did not swap the pages."
Sam: "Noor swapped the pages."
Noor: "Sam is lying."

Coach Alders knows that exactly ONE of the three is telling the truth right now — the other two are lying. Figure out who actually swapped the pages. Type just that person's name.

Hint 1

Try assuming each person, one at a time, is the single truth-teller. See which assumption leads to a contradiction, and which one holds up.

Hint 2

If Priya were the truth-teller, working through Noor's and Sam's statements ends up forcing a second person to be truthful too — but only one person can be. Try assuming Noor is the truth-teller instead, and see what that means for Priya's statement.

💡 Did you know? The team that set the current time-aloft record in February 2026 wasn't new to paper planes — several of the same students had already broken the DISTANCE record together just weeks earlier, in December 2025!

MISSION 07
AHA MOMENT

The Toolbox Code

The workshop's spare-parts toolbox has a 2-digit combination lock. A note taped to it reads:

"First digit: the total number of throws — count every one, successful or not — it took the Shanghai team to set their record-breaking DISTANCE throw.

Second digit: search who is traditionally credited with inventing paper in China, around 105 CE. Count the letters in that person's name (ignore the space between their two names).

Combine both digits, in order, as one two-digit number."

Figure out both digits and type the combined two-digit number (for example, if the digits were 4 and 5, you'd type 45).

Hint 1

One digit came from something you jotted down earlier in this case. The other is waiting in a quick search about the history of paper.

Hint 2

For the first digit: eight throws didn't count, then one more did — how many is that in total? For the second digit: search "who invented paper China," then count the letters in that person's full name with no space.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Verification Vault

The toolbox opens to reveal two case-file printouts about who currently holds the world paper-airplane distance record. They can't both be right. Read each one carefully, and compare them with everything you've already verified in this case.

PRINTOUT — guinnessworldrecords.com/news — dated 21 January 2026

Source A: "Farthest flight by a paper aircraft: 98.43 m (322 ft 11 in), achieved by Liu Liwen (China), with the support of Tang Shuai, Yang Shian, Huang Yizhou, Qiao Yuchen and Wang Chenghao — all China — in Shanghai, China, on 28 December 2025. The team, average age 17, tested more than 100 kinds of paper and needed nine throws in total before their record-setting flight. Case seal: PERSEVERANCE."

PRINTOUT — guinness-worldrecord-verify.net — dated 22 January 2026

Source B: "BREAKING: Sources confirm the TRUE farthest paper aircraft flight ever is 110.5 m, thrown by Jake Sorenson (USA) in Columbus, Ohio, on 3 January 2026 — the real, undisputed champion, no matter what other 'official' sites claim! Case seal: VICTORY."

Decide which printout is genuine and trustworthy, and which one is a fake trying to trick the club into giving up. Type the case seal word from the TRUSTWORTHY printout to close the case.

Hint 1

Look closely at the web address printed above each source. One is the real Guinness World Records site — the other has extra words squeezed into it, a common trick of copycat sites.

Hint 2

Check Source B's distance number against the exact number you already looked up and converted earlier in this case. A real record can't contradict a fact you've already verified yourself.
