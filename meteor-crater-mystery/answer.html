Mission 01 · Easy

The Empty Display Case

Answer: 1200 (meters)
How to get there

Search "Meteor Crater Arizona diameter." Sources agree the crater is about 1,200 meters (4,000 feet, or roughly 0.75 miles) across, rounded to the nearest hundred meters. Some give a slightly more precise figure (e.g. 1,186 m), which still rounds to 1,200.

Why this is the only answer

Every reputable source converges on "about 1.2 km" once rounded to the nearest hundred meters, so the instruction to round removes any ambiguity between sources.

Source / fact used

Britannica: "The crater is 4,000 feet (1,200 metres) in diameter." britannica.com/place/Meteor-Crater; corroborated by NASA Earth Observatory and USGS descriptions of "1.2 km diameter."

Mission 02 · Easy

The Sample Tray Mix-Up

Answer: B
How to get there

Real meteorites (especially iron and stony-iron ones) are almost always magnetic, feel unusually heavy for their size (no air bubbles), and have a smooth, dark fusion crust from burning through the atmosphere. Rock B fails on magnetism, is full of bubbles/holes, and is light — that combination matches slag or vesicular volcanic rock, the classic false positives people mistake for meteorites.

Why this is the only answer

Rocks A, C, and D all match every real-meteorite criterion given (magnetic, heavy, no bubbles). Only B fails more than one, and non-magnetic + bubbly + light is specifically the profile of a non-meteorite.

Source / fact used

ASU "Ask an Earth and Space Scientist" — meteorite identification (fusion crust, magnetism, density, absence of bubbles/vesicles as the key tests). askanearthspacescientist.asu.edu/identifying-meteorites

Mission 03 · Medium

Opa's Coded Page

Answer: 1903
How to get there

Shifting each letter of "ILQG ZKHQ VWDQGDUG LURQ EHJDQ" backward by 3 decodes to "FIND WHEN STANDARD IRON BEGAN." Searching "Standard Iron Company Meteor Crater founded" shows Daniel Barringer formed the Standard Iron Company on March 15, 1903, to dig for the meteorite he believed was buried under the crater.

Why this is the only answer

Multiple independent sources (Wikipedia, Encyclopedia.com, historical accounts of Barringer) agree on 1903 as the company's founding/claim-staking year, so the search converges cleanly on one year.

Source / fact used

"On March 15, 1903 Barringer forms the Standard Iron Company... with the intent of locating and mining the buried deposit of nickel-iron." See Daniel Barringer (geologist), Wikipedia, and Barringer Crater Company history pages.

Delayed recall

This year (1903) is needed again, unprompted, in the Grand Finale — six case files later. The finale does not restate it or point back to this mission.

Mission 04 · Medium

The Ring Around the Model

Answer: 942 (square meters)
How to get there

Area of outer circle = π × 20² = 3.14 × 400 = 1256 m². Area of inner circle = π × 10² = 3.14 × 100 = 314 m². Ring area = 1256 − 314 = 942 m².

Why this is the only answer

The two radii (20 m outer, 10 m inner) are fixed and labeled directly on the diagram, and the instruction to use π ≈ 3.14 removes any rounding ambiguity — the subtraction gives exactly 942 with no remainder to round.

Source / fact used

Standard geometry (area of a circle = πr²); the walkway/model numbers are an invented story detail for this puzzle, not a claim about the real crater's actual visitor facilities.

Mission 05 · Medium

Opa's Dutch Postscript

Answer: heelal
How to get there

"Meteoriet," "krater," and "ster" are all "de" words in Dutch. "Heelal" (universe) is a neuter noun and takes "het" instead — "het heelal."

Why this is the only answer

Dutch dictionaries mark "heelal" explicitly as neuter (het; o), while "krater" and "ster" are explicitly marked as common-gender (de) nouns, and "meteoriet" is used consistently with "de" across independent Dutch science and education sources.

Source / fact used

Van Dale: "heel·al (het; o)" — vandale.nl; Encyclo.nl marks "krater" as masculine (de); Woorden.org and multiple Dutch science sites (aa.quae.nl, National Geographic NL, KIJK Magazine) consistently pair "meteoriet" with "de."

Mission 06 · Hard

Four Scientists, Four Craters

Answer: Amara
How to get there

Clue 1 gives Dr. Liang = Card 3 (mentions the ocean = Chicxulub crater, Mexico, half underwater). Clue 2 gives Dr. Sofía = Card 4 (oldest = Vredefort crater, South Africa, ~2 billion years old). Clue 3 gives Dr. Tomas = Card 1 (mentions NASA = Meteor/Barringer Crater, Arizona). That leaves Card 2 (Wolfe Creek Crater, Australia, called "Kandimalal") for the only scientist left over: Dr. Amara.

Why this is the only answer

Each of the three given clues uniquely identifies one card by a detail that appears on only one card, so there is exactly one valid assignment — a classic elimination logic grid with no other consistent solution.

Source / fact used

Card facts verified: Wolfe Creek Crater is known as "Kandimalal" to Aboriginal people (Wikipedia, "Wolfe Creek Crater"); Vredefort crater is Earth's oldest/largest verified impact crater at ~2.02 billion years (Wikipedia, "Vredefort impact structure"); Chicxulub crater is ~66 million years old and half submerged in the Gulf of Mexico (Britannica, "Chicxulub crater"); Meteor/Barringer Crater hosted NASA Apollo astronaut training (Barringer Crater Company, meteorcrater.com).

Mission 07 · Hard (multi-stage)

From the Rim Marker to the Drill Site

Stage 1 answer: 50 (paces)

40² + 30² = 1600 + 900 = 2500. √2500 = 50. This is a scaled-up 3-4-5 right triangle.

Stage 2 (final) answer: 70

Binary 1111000 = 64+32+16+8 = 120 in decimal. 120 − 50 (Stage 1's shortcut) = 70.

Why this is the only answer

Stage 1 is a fixed right triangle with given legs (40, 30) — the Pythagorean theorem gives exactly one hypotenuse length (50). Stage 2's binary-to-decimal conversion of a fixed number (1111000) is likewise deterministic (120), and the instruction to subtract Stage 1's own result makes Stage 2 genuinely dependent on Stage 1 — it cannot be solved correctly without first solving Stage 1.

Source / fact used

Pythagorean theorem and binary place values (64/32/16/8/4/2/1) — both self-contained math, no outside lookup needed. The map, distances, and lockbox are invented story details.

Mission 08 · Hard

The Order of the Case Files

Answer: C
How to get there

Cards B (1886), E (1891), and A (1960) give explicit years; Card D is deliberately left undated in the kid-facing file but is the 1903 Standard Iron Company dig from Mission 3 (its exact year isn't needed to answer this question — the text already places it between Card E and Card A). Card C describes NASA astronaut training "around the time humans first set foot on the Moon" — searching this shows the Moon landing was in 1969. Ordered: B (1886) → E (1891) → D (1903) → A (1960) → C (~1969). Card C is last.

Why this is the only answer

Three of the five years are stated outright and Card D's relative position is given in the text, so none of that needs research; only Card C's year is deliberately left vague, and looking it up (1969) places it unambiguously after all four other events. Card D is intentionally left without its 1903 year in the kid-facing file so this mission doesn't re-expose the number planted in Mission 3 before the Grand Finale asks for it back.

Source / fact used

NASA sent Apollo astronauts (including the Apollo 11 crew, who first walked on the Moon on July 20, 1969) to train at Meteor Crater in the 1960s. See Barringer Crater Company / meteorcrater.com Apollo history pages and USGS "Walk in the Footsteps of the Apollo Astronauts."

Big "Aha!" Moment

The Volcano That Wasn't

Answer: impact (accept "meteorite impact" / "space impact")
How to get there

Assume the volcano theory is true. Fact 2 says no lava rock or magma chamber was ever found — but a real volcano would have left one. Fact 3 says a mineral was found that can only form from a sudden, powerful impact — something a volcano cannot produce. Both facts contradict the volcano assumption, so it must be false; the crater formed from an impact instead.

Why this is the only answer

Facts 2 and 3 are each individually incompatible with "it's a volcano," so the assumption cannot survive — there is no reading of the three facts together that keeps the volcano theory alive.

Source / fact used

In 1960, Eugene Shoemaker (with Edward Chao and Beth Madsen) found coesite — a mineral that only forms under the shock pressure of an impact or nuclear blast, never in a volcano — in the crater's rock, ending the decades-long volcano-vs-impact debate. Published in Science, July 1960. See "Benchmarks: July 22, 1960: Mineral discovery ends Meteor Crater debate," EARTH Magazine, and the Barringer Crater Company's history of Eugene Shoemaker's research.

Grand Finale

Opa's Locked Drawer

Answer: 3091
How to get there

The code is "the year my old company first started digging here — but written backwards." That company and year is the Standard Iron Company, 1903, from Mission 3. Reversing the digits of 1903 gives 3091.

Why this is the only answer

Only one earlier case file involves a company digging for a buried meteorite (Mission 3's decoded cipher), so there is exactly one year to recall and exactly one way to reverse its four digits.

Source / fact used

Callback to Mission 3's verified answer (1903, the Standard Iron Company's founding year) — this mission deliberately does not restate the year or point back to Mission 3 by name, so the child must recall it unaided.

Reward revealed

Perseverance — the closing note ties the decades that scientists (and Barringer himself) spent doubting, digging, and searching before the crater's true origin was finally proven, to the value of not giving up.
