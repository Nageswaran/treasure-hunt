Internet Detective Academy · Case File

The Meteor Crater Mystery

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 75-100 minutes (10 case files — grab a notebook and a pencil!)
Your grandfather — everyone calls him Opa Sam — spent forty years studying a giant hole in the Arizona desert called Meteor Crater. This week a package arrived: his old field journal, and a short note. "Something important is locked away in my study," it says. "You'll need everything inside this journal to find it. Good luck, Agent." Ten case files stand between you and Opa's hidden secret — crack them all, in order, to finish the case.
MISSION 01
EASY

The Empty Display Case

Opa's very first case file is short: "Before anything else, Agent, learn how big this crater really is." Search online for the diameter (width) of Meteor Crater in Arizona, USA — trustworthy sites give slightly different exact numbers, so round your answer to the nearest hundred meters and type just the number, like 800.

Hint 1

Try searching "Meteor Crater Arizona diameter" — an encyclopedia site or an official crater/science site will have this fact clearly.

MISSION 02
EASY

The Sample Tray Mix-Up

Opa's old sample tray got mixed up. Four rocks are labeled A, B, C, and D. Only three are real meteorites — one is just an ordinary rock that fooled someone once. Opa's notes explain how to tell them apart:

• Rock A: sticks to a magnet, dark smooth crust, no bubbles or holes, feels very heavy for its size.
• Rock B: does not stick to a magnet, full of tiny bubbles and holes, feels light for its size.
• Rock C: sticks to a magnet, dark crust, no bubbles, feels very heavy.
• Rock D: sticks to a magnet, dark crust, no bubbles, feels heavy.

Which rock is not a real meteorite? Type just the letter.

Hint 1

Real meteorites are almost always magnetic, feel heavy for their size, and are never bubbly inside — bubbles are a sign of ordinary volcanic rock or old furnace waste, not space rock.

💡 Did you know? The meteorite that carved out Meteor Crater was moving so fast — around 42,000 km/h — that the impact almost completely vaporized it on the spot.

MISSION 03
MEDIUM

Opa's Coded Page

The next page is written in code. Opa always used a Caesar cipher — every letter is shifted forward through the alphabet by the same number of steps. This message was shifted forward by 3 (A→D, B→E, and so on). Decode it:

ILQG ZKHQ VWDQGDUG LURQ EHJDQ

Once you've decoded the message, search online to find that year — the year a real mining company started digging at Meteor Crater, certain that a giant iron meteorite was buried underneath. Type just the year, like 1776.

Jot this year down, Agent — Opa's notes have a habit of circling back.

Hint 1

To decode, shift each letter backward by 3 instead (D→A, E→B, and so on) — it undoes the same shift.

Hint 2

The decoded company's name has "Iron" in it. Try searching "[that company name] Meteor Crater founded".

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

The Ring Around the Model

Grab a pencil for this one. Opa's museum built a round scale model of the crater with a safety walkway running all the way around it, like a ring. The walkway's outer edge is 20 meters from the center, and its inner edge — right at the model crater's rim — is 10 meters from the center.

Using π ≈ 3.14, work out the area of just the ring-shaped walkway (not the whole circle) in square meters.
Hint 1

Find the area of the whole big outer circle, then the area of the smaller inner circle, then subtract the second from the first.

Hint 2

Area of a circle = π × radius × radius. Outer: 3.14 × 20 × 20. Inner: 3.14 × 10 × 10.

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Opa's Dutch Postscript

Tucked in the journal is a page in Dutch. Opa wrote: "Onthoud dit rijtje!" ("Remember this list!") — followed by four words: meteoriet, krater, ster, heelal.

Three of these words use de (de meteoriet, de krater, de ster). One of them is different — it uses het instead. Which word is the odd one out? Type just that Dutch word.

Hint 1

Try saying "de" in front of each word out loud — one of them will sound wrong to a Dutch ear.

Hint 2

This word means "the universe" — everything that exists, all of space put together.

💡 Did you know? In the 1960s, NASA sent real astronauts — including Neil Armstrong — to train at Meteor Crater, because its rocky, cratered ground looks so much like the surface of the Moon.

MISSION 06
HARD

Four Scientists, Four Craters

Four scientists — Dr. Amara, Dr. Liang, Dr. Sofía, and Dr. Tomas — each study a different impact crater somewhere on Earth. Here are the crater's case cards:

• Card 1: 50,000 years old. Still shaped like a clear round bowl today. NASA astronauts once trained here for walking on the Moon.
• Card 2: 120,000 years old. Still shaped like a clear round bowl today. Local Aboriginal people call it "Kandimalal."
• Card 3: 66 million years old. Half of it now lies under the sea.
• Card 4: 2 billion years old — the oldest by far. So worn down by time you can't see a crater shape anymore, just a huge dome of rock.

Clues:
1. Dr. Liang's card mentions the ocean.
2. Dr. Sofía's crater is the oldest of the four.
3. Dr. Tomas's card mentions NASA.

Use the process of elimination to work out which card belongs to each scientist. Which scientist studied the crater the local Aboriginal community calls "Kandimalal"? Type just that scientist's first name.

Hint 1

Match each of the three clues to exactly one card first. That leaves exactly one card — and one scientist — unmatched.

Hint 2

Dr. Liang = Card 3 (ocean). Dr. Sofía = Card 4 (oldest). Dr. Tomas = Card 1 (NASA). Only one card and one scientist remain.

MISSION 07
HARD

From the Rim Marker to the Drill Site

Stage 1 of 2 — grab a pencil for this one too

A hand-drawn map in the journal shows one of Opa's old walks: starting at the Rim Marker, he walked 40 paces due east, then 30 paces due south, and reached the Old Drill Site.

If you cut straight back across the desert from the Old Drill Site to the Rim Marker, instead of retracing his steps, how many whole paces is that shortcut? (Assume every pace is the same length, and picture the right angle where he turned.)
Hint 1

His path forms two sides of a right triangle. The shortcut is the third side.

Hint 2

Use the Pythagorean theorem: shortcut × shortcut = (40 × 40) + (30 × 30).

Stage 2 of 2

The Old Drill Site's rusty lockbox has a dial with only 0s and 1s on it. Convert this binary number to a normal decimal number: 1111000. Then subtract your shortcut distance from Stage 1. Type the final number that opens the lock.

Hint 1

In binary, each digit (counting from the right) stands for 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 — add up the ones that have a 1 underneath them.

Hint 2

1111000 in decimal is 120. Now subtract your Stage 1 answer from 120.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Order of the Case Files

Opa's case files about the crater's history got shuffled. Read all five, then figure out which happened LAST of all:

• Card A (1960): A geologist finds a rare mineral in the crater's rock — one that can only form from a sudden, incredibly powerful impact, never from a volcano. This finally proves how the crater really formed.
• Card B (1886): A sheepherder finds strange, heavy metal rocks scattered near a huge hole in the desert. He thinks they might be silver and stays quiet about it for years.
• Card C (year unknown): A group of astronauts train on the crater's rocky floor to get ready for something history-making — something that happened "around the time humans first set foot on the Moon." Search online to find roughly which year that was.
• Card D (no year given): A mining company forms and starts digging, certain a giant buried meteorite is hidden below the crater floor — sometime after Card E's volcano test, well before Card A's mineral proof.
• Card E (1891): A government scientist tests the ground with a magnetic instrument, finds no buried metal, and decides the crater must be an old, collapsed volcano.

Which card happened LAST? Type just the letter (A, B, C, D, or E).

Hint 1

Three of the five cards already give you a year: 1886, 1891, 1960. Card D sits somewhere between two of those — you don't need its exact year to answer this question.

Hint 2

Search "when did humans first land on the Moon" to place Card C compared to 1960 — that's all you need to know which card is last.

💡 Did you know? The Vredefort crater in South Africa is about 2 billion years old and so worn down by time that its bowl shape has completely eroded away — all that's left is a giant dome-shaped ring of rock.

BIG "AHA!" MOMENT
AHA!

The Volcano That Wasn't

For decades, the government scientist from Card E insisted the crater was just an old, collapsed volcano. Let's test that idea using proof by contradiction: first, assume he's right.

Fact 1: A volcano forms when melted rock (magma) pushes up from below and leaves lava rock behind on the surface.
Fact 2: In over a hundred years of digging and testing, nobody has ever found lava rock or a magma chamber anywhere near the crater.
Fact 3: Scientists did find a rare mineral in the crater's rock that can only be created by sudden, extremely powerful pressure — like a space collision — and never by a volcano.

If the "it's a volcano" assumption were true, Facts 2 and 3 couldn't both exist — so the assumption must be false. In one word: how did the crater really form?

Hint 1

If it were really an old volcano, Fact 2 wouldn't be possible — there would be lava rock somewhere close by.

Hint 2

Fact 3 describes proof of a very fast, very powerful collision from above, not from below.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

Opa's Locked Drawer

You've reached Opa's locked drawer. A small note taped to it reads: "The code is the year my old company first started digging here — but written backwards."

Think back to the case file where you first uncovered that company and its year. Type the 4-digit code.

Hint 1

Which case file had you decode a message about a company that dug for a buried meteorite?

Hint 2

Once you have that 4-digit year, just reverse the order of its digits.

Case closed?

The drawer creaks open to reveal a small iron-dark fragment and a final note: "People doubted this crater for seventy years, Agent, but the scientists who believed in it never stopped searching for proof." That's perseverance — and now you've got a piece of it in your hands too. Case closed!
