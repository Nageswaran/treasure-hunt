Mission 02 · Easy · Logical Reasoning (Foundational — pattern/sequence)

A Badge and a Hallway Full of Numbers

Answer: 42
How to get there

The sequence is 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?. The differences between consecutive terms are 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 — each gap is exactly 2 more than the last. Continuing that pattern, the next gap is 14, so 30 + 14 = 42. (These numbers are also n×(n+1) for n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6: 1×2=2, 2×3=6, 3×4=12, 4×5=20, 5×6=30, 6×7=42 — both routes to the pattern agree.)

Why this is the only answer

The constant-second-difference pattern (gaps growing by 2 each time) is unambiguous and is the standard, intended reading of this style of sequence puzzle; no other simple rule fits all six given terms.

Source / fact used

Pure arithmetic pattern — recomputed by hand: differences 4,6,8,10,12 (arithmetic progression, common difference 2); next difference 14; 30+14=42. Confirmed independently via the n(n+1) formula.

Mission 06 · Hard · Mathematics (combinatorics / counting logic, pen & paper)

The Backup Keypad

Answer: 2296
How to get there

Split into two cases by the last (even) digit:

Case A — last digit is 0: the other three digits come from {1-9} (9 digits), and the first digit is automatically nonzero. Arrangements: 9 × 8 × 7 = 504.

Case B — last digit is 2, 4, 6, or 8 (4 choices): for each, the remaining pool is 9 digits (0-9 minus that last digit). The first digit can't be 0, so it has 8 choices; the second digit then has 8 choices (from the remaining 8, 0 now allowed); the third has 7 choices. That's 8 × 8 × 7 = 448 per last-digit choice, × 4 = 1792.

Total: 504 + 1792 = 2296.

Why this is the only answer

Cross-checked independently: total 4-digit codes with 4 distinct digits and a nonzero first digit = 9 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 4536. Splitting that same total by last-digit parity (0 / even non-zero / odd) gives 504 + 1792 + 2240 = 4536, which matches exactly — confirming 504 + 1792 = 2296 is the correct even-ending count.

Source / fact used

Pure combinatorics, recomputed twice by two different case breakdowns (by last digit, and as a total-minus-odd-endings cross-check) to confirm the arithmetic.

Mission 07 · Hard · Multi-Stage · Technology & Mathematics (image)

The Old-Fashioned Failsafe

Stage 1 answer: 39

How to get there (Stage 1)

A search for "combination padlock dial numbers" confirms that classic dial combination padlocks (like the Master Lock-style padlock in the photo) are numbered 0 through 39 — 40 total positions on the dial — before the numbers repeat.

Why this is the only answer (Stage 1)

This is a fixed, verifiable manufacturing fact about classic 3-number dial combination padlocks (as opposed to, say, digital keypad locks), confirmed by multiple sources including lock manufacturers and lock-picking reference sites.

Stage 2 (final) answer: 24

How to get there (Stage 2)

Count the letters (not spaces) in "THE PUZZLE PARLOR": THE = 3, PUZZLE = 6, PARLOR = 6, total = 15 letters. Take Stage 1's answer (39) and subtract 15: 39 − 15 = 24.

Why this is the only answer (Stage 2)

Stage 2 is pure arithmetic that depends directly on Stage 1's number — a child who didn't correctly find 39 in Stage 1 cannot reach 24 here, confirming the dependency is real. Recomputed by hand: 3+6+6=15; 39-15=24.

Letter-by-letter: B→O, C→P, R→E, A→N ("OPEN"); G→T, U→H, R→E ("THE"); I→V, N→A, H→U, Y→L, G→T ("VAULT").

Why this is the only answer
