Internet Detective Academy · Case File

Escape Room Design Contest

Agent briefing — read carefully before you begin
⏱ Estimated case time: 80-100 minutes

The Puzzle Parlor, downtown's newest escape-room company, just made the final five for the Golden Key Design Awards — a national contest for the best new escape rooms in the country. Head Designer Jordan Reyes has hired you as a Junior Puzzle Tester: your job is to inspect every prototype room and make sure each puzzle has exactly one correct answer. Miss a puzzle with two possible answers, and the judges disqualify the whole room. Ten stops on your inspection list, one submission deadline — let's get testing.

MISSION 01
EASY

Badge In With a Scan

Your very first task: scan your new staff badge to clock in for the day. The scanner reads a square, dotted pattern called a QR code — Jordan mentions it's actually much older technology than most people guess. Before you scan, she wants you to know exactly how old. Search the web: in what year were QR codes invented? Type just the year.

Hint 1

Search "QR code invented year" and look for the Japanese company that first created it to track car parts on a factory floor.

💡 Did you know? The QR code was invented in 1994 by engineer Masahiro Hara at the Japanese company Denso Wave — originally just to track car parts faster on the factory floor.

MISSION 02
EASY

A Badge and a Hallway Full of Numbers

Jordan pins a badge to your shirt. "Every staff member gets a two-digit badge number," she says. "Yours is 47. Keep it on you — you'll need to know it by heart before this case is closed."

📓 Agent's Notebook: Jot this down — Junior Tester badge #47.

Down the hallway, the room number plaques follow a pattern: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? What number belongs on the very next door?

Hint 1

Don't just stare at the numbers themselves — look at the gaps between them. How much does each gap grow compared to the last one?

MISSION 04
MEDIUM

Three Designers, Three Rooms

Three of the Parlor's top designers — Priya, Marcus, and Talia — just handed in their prototype rooms. Jordan needs each designer matched to their room and its signature prop before she can schedule inspections. Here's what you know:

Clue 1: Marcus's room is not "Pharaoh's Curse."
Clue 2: Talia's signature prop is a padlock.
Clue 3: Priya designed "Heist at Midnight."
Clue 4: Whichever room is "Space Station Zero" uses a laser grid as its signature prop.

Who designed "Space Station Zero"? Type their first name.

Hint 1

Start with the clue that names a specific room outright — that pins down one full match before you touch the others.

Hint 2

Once you know who designed "Heist at Midnight," only two designers are left for the other two rooms. Clue 1 tells you which of those two can't be "Pharaoh's Curse" — so the other one must be "Space Station Zero."

MISSION 05
MEDIUM

Two Trainees, One Vault

The star prototype, "Vault of Ages," ends with a locked door that opens on a 2-digit code. The design notes say the code is simply "the atomic number of gold." Two junior trainees tested the room and filed different reports:

Devon's report: "The code is 79 — that's gold's atomic number."
Marisol's report: "The code is 82 — I'm sure that's gold's atomic number too, I just double-checked."

They can't both be right, and this contest only accepts rooms with exactly one correct solution. Search to verify gold's real atomic number (the number of protons in a gold atom), and figure out which trainee mixed up gold with a different, similar-sounding metal. Type the correct 2-digit code that should be submitted to the judges.

Hint 1

Search "atomic number of gold" on a reliable science source, like a periodic table site. 82 is a real atomic number too — just not gold's.

MISSION 06
HARD

The Backup Keypad

"Vault of Ages" also ships with a backup keypad lock, in case the main lock ever needs replacing before judging day. The design spec says the backup code must follow ALL of these rules:

• It's exactly 4 digits long.
• It uses four DIFFERENT digits chosen from 0-9 (no digit repeats).
• It can never start with 0 (the keypad reads a leading 0 as "power off").
• It must end in an EVEN digit — 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 — because the last tumbler only accepts even numbers.

This one has enough moving parts that it's really worth grabbing scratch paper and working through it case by case, rather than trying it in your head. How many different valid 4-digit codes fit every one of these rules?

Hint 1

Split it into two cases based on the last digit: one case where the last digit is 0, and a separate case for when it's 2, 4, 6, or 8. Zero is special, since it also can't be the first digit.

Hint 2

In each case, count how many digits are left for the first spot, then the next, then the last, multiplying as you go — then add the totals from both cases together.

💡 Did you know? The world's first commercial real-life escape room opened in Kyoto, Japan, in 2007. It was created by Takao Kato's company SCRAP — at the debut event, only 6 out of 150 players managed to escape!

MISSION 07
HARD

The Old-Fashioned Failsafe

Stage 1 of 2

"Vault of Ages" has one more backup: a real dial combination padlock, just like the one in the photo below, bolted inside the prop chest in case the electronics ever fail.

A classic single-dial combination padlock — the same style used on gym lockers for generations.

Before the team sets a fresh combination, Jordan wants you to confirm one fact about how a padlock like this actually works. Search to confirm: on a classic dial combination padlock, what is the highest number that appears on the dial before the numbers loop back around to 0? Type that number.

Hint 1

Search "combination padlock dial numbers" — these dials don't go all the way up to 100, or even 50.

Stage 2 of 2

Take that highest dial number and subtract the number of letters in the company's full name, "THE PUZZLE PARLOR" — count only the letters, not the spaces. Whatever's left over is the code stamped inside the vault door as its manufacturing number. What's the final code?

Hint 1

Count the letters in THE, then PUZZLE, then PARLOR separately, add those three totals together, and subtract that from your Stage 1 number.

MISSION 08
HARD

The Review Terminal

The "Vault of Ages" review terminal reveals its secret starting number through a chain of steps. Jordan reads the process aloud while she tests it: "Take the secret starting number, add your own badge number to it, double that result, then subtract 10. That's exactly how we got today's terminal reading: 200."

Work backward from 200 to find the secret starting number.

Hint 1

Undo the steps in reverse order: first undo "subtract 10," then undo "double it." That leaves you with "start number + badge number."

Hint 2

Once you've undone those two steps, you'll have a total equal to "start number + badge number." Don't forget the badge number Jordan gave you on your very first day at The Puzzle Parlor.

MISSION 09
AHA!

Jordan's Scrambled Note

Jordan slides you a note before the judges arrive: "BCRA GUR INHYG"

Her hint: take the highest number from that classic padlock's dial — the one you already found a few rooms back — and divide it by 26, the number of letters in the alphabet. The remainder is your shift number. Shift every letter in the note forward through the alphabet by that many places (wrapping past Z back around to A) to reveal the real message.

Type the decoded message.

Hint 1

39 divided by 26 doesn't come out even — it's the remainder left over that becomes your shift, not 39 itself.

Hint 2

A shift of 13 is famous — shifting forward 13 places or backward 13 places lands on the exact same letter, since the alphabet has 26 letters. Try writing out A→N, B→O, C→P... and keep going.

GRAND FINALE
FINALE

The Golden Key Envelope

The judges leave one sealed envelope on the front desk: a single coded word, "QFSOHWJWHM", with a note that says to combine two numbers from your casefile to unlock it.

First: the true atomic number of gold you confirmed earlier — not Marisol's mixed-up guess. Second: the highest number on that classic padlock's dial. Subtract the smaller number from the larger one, then find the remainder when you divide that result by 26 — that remainder is your shift.

Shift every letter in the coded word BACKWARD through the alphabet by that many places (wrapping past A back around to Z) to reveal the one word that best describes what makes The Puzzle Parlor's rooms so special.

Type that word.

Hint 1

Subtract first (the larger of your two numbers minus the smaller), then divide by 26 and keep only the remainder — that remainder, not the subtraction result, is your shift.

Hint 2

Shifting backward means Q becomes C, F becomes R, and so on. Try writing the alphabet out twice in a row so it's easy to count backward without losing your place.

Case closed?

Every prototype needs exactly one correct answer — but building a room that clever takes real CREATIVITY. Thanks to your sharp eyes, careful math, and cipher-cracking, "Vault of Ages" passes inspection with zero ambiguous puzzles. Golden Key judges, meet your winner.
